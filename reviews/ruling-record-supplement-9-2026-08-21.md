## S9-3 — oath anatomy

An oath clause commits five components:

1. **Derivation rule** — static enumeration, role-derived, or
   **party-derived**: the roster computed from the enactment's
   own content (the parties it binds, their designated
   watchers). Party-derivation is jurisdiction made concrete.
2. **Threshold rule** — count, fraction, or weighted, with
   sufficiency defaults so unanimity is never accidental.
3. **Disjointness constraints**, two-grade: prefix-disjointness
   from the interested set is a byte-checkable guard;
   entity-disjointness is clause-grade — the sole-authority
   pattern's warranty, surfaced linkage converting to breach.
   (The reviewer's cure, adopted: rosters verifiably distinct
   from proposers where possible, warranted where not.)
4. **Set-stability rule** — roster churn is an attack; oath
   amendments for designated classes are themselves designated
   acts, establishment-anchored, capture-floor-declared.
5. **Evidence-gated degradation** — substitution only on
   committed proof of a roster member's unavailability (the
   member's own cadence breach), or degradation is the capture
   route.

**Placement resolved:** the *rule* is always constitutional; the
*roster* is derived per-enactment from content, never chosen
per-enactment. Party-derivation yields enactment-specific
rosters with zero proposer discretion, because the derivation
was committed before the proposal existed. Grade honesty rides
the carrier: static and role rosters can ride the medium's
escrow (guard grade — witness and backer machinery);
party-derived rosters cannot (the registry fold is blind to whom
an enactment binds) and enforce at seating grade through the
finality finding. Both are typed positions.

**Attack surfaces, cured from ratified machinery:** oath capture

ante, silence dateable against cadence, substitution
evidence-gated. The roster polices the institution; cadence law
polices the roster.
