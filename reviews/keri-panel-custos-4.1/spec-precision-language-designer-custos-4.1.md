# Spec-Precision & Language Designer (SPC) Review: Custos 4.1

**Date:** 2026-07-27  **Target(s):** /home/daniel/code/3GR/custos/spec/custos-4.1.md (live, read in full); KERI spec repo (kswg-keri-specification @ 4df80aa) for substrate cross-ref  **Effort:** medium  **Objective lens:** survivability (replayable governance)
**Sources used:** keri-doctrine (claims 9, 13; I2I/edge-operator shibboleth; evidence standard), review-house-style, orchestrating-reviews, bible 01 §8, bible 04 §3/§5c-5d/§12, bible 07 §10.5/§12, and the live Custos 4.1 spec text.

## Executive summary

Custos 4.1's entire load-bearing claim is in my lens: **replayable governance** — "the same Constitution, the same findings, the same refusals, byte for byte" (L29), operationalized as "Two evaluations of the same triple SHALL return byte-identical findings" (L1038). The document is unusually disciplined about fencing its open frontier (§15 openness clause, §2 non-goals, §16 confessed cross-implementation deliverable), which correctly defuses most "you didn't specify X" reflexes — faulting the undesigned Gever interior would be exactly the mirror-image error my calibration warns against. But the determinism machinery the document *does* pin has two concrete internal contradictions and two consistency gaps that will make two independent implementers diverge — and, critically, at fixture scale (one implementation) they are **invisible**, surfacing only when the §16 cross-implementation-equality deliverable is attempted. The single biggest finding: the canonical defeat-selection rule (§7.3) names "lexicographic minimum" and then mandates "empty … orders last," which are contradictory unless the encoding is specified, so two honest verifiers cite different defeats and the explicit "same defeated finding down to the byte" SHALL (L1127) is falsified.

## Steelman

The author is building the layer KERI deliberately stops short of: consequence for detected facts, committed and recomputable rather than improvised. The design goal is that a stranger with only the logs re-derives law, findings, and refusals identically — carrying KERI's controller/validator split (only a controller writes; any validator verifies) up one tier into evaluate/enact, so nothing that judges ever writes and a missing rule yields refusal, not legislation. Byte-identity is the right north star: it is exactly the discipline (insertion-ordered maps + SAIDs over exact bytes, bible 04 §11) that makes KERI key state replayable rather than testimonial, lifted to governance. The document is candid that byte-identity is exercised only at fixture scale and that cross-implementation equality is an undischarged debt (§2, §16). My findings are not "this is unsound in principle" — the principle is sound and well-fenced — they are "these specific canonical rules, as written, will not produce the byte-identity the standard promises when a second implementation is built against the text."

## Top findings

### F1: Canonical defeat-selection is self-contradictory ("lexicographic minimum" vs. "empty … orders last")
- **Severity:** HIGH  **Confidence:** CONFIRMED  **dedupe_key:** canonical-defeat-selection-empty-subcode-order  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** governance
- **Location:** proposal §7.3 "Canonical selection," L1123–1136 (live)
- **Finding:** The rule requires the finding to "cite the lexicographic minimum of (defeater-class rank, citation identifier, subcode)" (L1123–1125), then states "where the clause defines none, the subcode is empty **and orders last**" (L1135–1136). Under standard lexicographic byte comparison the empty string is the *minimum* (it is a prefix of every string), so it would be *selected first* — the exact opposite of "orders last." The two clauses are only reconcilable under an unstated encoding in which the empty subcode compares *greater* than any non-empty subcode. The text pins neither that encoding nor an override precedence.
- **Why it matters:** Two defeats with identical class rank and identical citation identifier but differing subcodes — one empty (clause defines no enumeration), one non-empty — are reachable ("subcode is the defeat's discriminator within its citation"). Implementer A does a raw tuple/lexicographic compare (empty sorts low → empty defeat cited); implementer B honors "orders last" (non-empty cited). Different `defeated(citation)` findings from the same bundle — directly falsifying "Two verifiers holding the same bundle SHALL emit the same defeated finding down to the byte" (L1126–1127), which is a `SHALL`. This is latent at fixture scale (one implementation is self-consistent) and detonates precisely at the §16 cross-implementation-equality deliverable ("equal … refusal grounds," L2141).
- **Recommendation:** Replace "lexicographic minimum" with an explicit total order and state the empty-subcode rank inline: e.g. "the minimum under the order where subcodes compare by their committed bytes and the empty subcode compares greater than every non-empty subcode." One clause closes it.

### F2: The pending→self-convicted transition admits a trigger its own required payload cannot satisfy
- **Severity:** HIGH  **Confidence:** CONFIRMED  **dedupe_key:** self-convicted-trigger-vs-required-proof  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** governance
- **Location:** proposal §7.3 transition table L1062–1064; required payloads L1052–1053; value definition §7.1 L954–959
- **Finding:** `self-convicted` is defined by a contradictory pair — "the subject's own committed bytes contain a contradiction: two voices where its constitution demands one" (L954–955) — and its required payload is exactly that pair: "A self-convicted finding SHALL carry the identifier of the canonical proof package for the contradictory pair" (L1052–1053). But the `pending → self-convicted` edge is conditioned on "a bearing contradictory pair, **or** new governed-status evidence (committed evidence newly bearing on the subject's status under the governance tier's committed predicates)" (L1062–1064). The `or` introduces a second, distinct trigger that is *not* self-evidently a contradictory pair — and it appears on *only* this edge (the `affirmed→self-convicted` and `defeated→self-convicted` edges, L1065–1066, require a contradictory pair with no second clause), which signals it is meant as a genuinely different trigger. If a verifier reaches self-convicted via "governed-status evidence" that is not a pair, it has no contradictory-pair proof to attach, violating the payload `SHALL`; the alternative reading (the clause is merely a way a governance-tier pair completes) makes the `or` redundant and misleading. It also sits in tension with §7.4's rule that "duplicity elsewhere … does not convert this question's finding" (L1110–1112).
- **Why it matters:** This is inside one of the six *fixed walls* the openness clause certifies "explicitly and completely enumerated" (§15, L2060–2061). Implementer A treats self-conviction as strictly pair-only (drops the second trigger as unsatisfiable); implementer B fires self-conviction on status evidence and must invent a proof or emit a payload-less terminal finding. Divergent terminal-value behavior on the same evidence — and a terminal value is unrehabilitatable (L1088–1089), so the divergence is permanent.
- **Recommendation:** Either delete the "or new governed-status evidence" alternative (if it is subsumed by "bearing contradictory pair"), or, if it is a real distinct trigger, define the value it produces and the proof it must carry — do not leave a terminal constructor reachable by a trigger the required-payload clause cannot serve.

### F3: "Byte-identical" is the stated conformance predicate, but the object encoding is deferred and §16's discharge test silently relaxes to semantic equality
- **Severity:** MEDIUM  **Confidence:** LIKELY  **dedupe_key:** byte-identity-predicate-vs-deferred-encoding  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** governance
- **Location:** proposal §2 L424–425 and §7.3 L1038 ("byte-identical") vs. §15 L2069–2072 (carriage encoding is an undesigned committed deliverable) vs. §16 L2139–2142 (discharge criterion)
- **Finding:** The class-defining obligation is byte-level: "recomputable, byte-identically" (L424–425); "byte-identical findings" (L1038); "byte-identical Constitutions" (L2211). But the serialization that makes byte-identity meaningful — "the carriage encoding of this document's object classes" — is explicitly an undesigned deliverable (§15, L2069–2072). Meanwhile §16's actual discharge test for cross-implementation equality is *semantic*: "equal corpus identities, admission sets, refusal grounds, and cited law heads" (L2140–2142) — set/identity equalities, not byte-identical serialized findings. So the headline predicate is stated more strongly (byte-identity) than any committed artifact can currently test, and in a different form than the standard's own discharge criterion.
- **Why it matters:** "Byte-identical" is not conformance-testable across implementations until the encoding is committed; a conformance-suite author cannot today write a decidable pass/fail for the headline obligation, and two readers will build two different conformance notions (byte-diff vs. structural equality). The gap is *mostly* confessed (the encoding deferral is on the record), so this is a precision/testability finding, not an unsoundness claim — but the standard should say which predicate is the conformance obligation.
- **Recommendation:** Pin the finding/object serialization to the substrate's canonical composable form (insertion-ordered CESR + SAID) as the committed default, or explicitly restate the conformance obligation as the enumerated semantic equalities of §16 until the encoding is committed — and make §2/§7.3 and §16 use one predicate.

### F4: "The same refusals, byte for byte" vs. refusal classified as a non-committed "operational fact"
- **Severity:** MEDIUM  **Confidence:** LIKELY  **dedupe_key:** refusal-not-a-committed-replayable-artifact  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** governance
- **Location:** proposal Abstract L29 ("the same refusals, byte for byte"); §7.1/§7.5 refusal-is-not-a-finding (L967–972, L1197–1201); §2 replay obligation scoped to "every judgment" (L424); §7.2 L1008–1012 (uncommitted drop ≠ finding)
- **Finding:** The Abstract promises refusals are reproduced "byte for byte" (L29), but the body classifies refusal as "not a finding … recorded as an operational fact" (L1199–1201) and excludes it from the finding codomain (L967–972); §2's byte-identical-replay obligation is scoped to "every judgment it issues" (L424), and a refusal is expressly *not* a judgment. The document itself argues in §7.2 that an operational observation with no committed receipt is not consumable ("a verifier that cannot distinguish 'judged absent' from 'silently dropped' holds no judgment at all," L1010–1012) — the same logic makes an un-committed refusal non-reproducible by a stranger. Whether a refusal is a committed replayable artifact is, moreover, exactly openness-clause unresolved-question #1 (finding-of-undecidability vs. record-of-incapacity, L2079–2081).
- **Why it matters:** The refusal *decision* is a pure function of the committed triple (rule present or absent), so it replays *as a decision*; but the Abstract claims the refusal itself is byte-reproducible, which requires a committed refusal record whose form is undesigned and whose status is confessed unresolved. Two implementers: one emits a committed refusal record (reproducible); one declines locally with no artifact (not reproducible) — both satisfy different sentences. The headline "same refusals byte for byte" outruns what the body commits.
- **Recommendation:** Reconcile the Abstract with the body: either scope the byte-identity claim to *findings* (and say refusals replay only as *decisions* derivable from the triple), or commit a refusal-record form and resolve unresolved-question #1 before claiming refusals are byte-reproducible.

### F5: §3.2's single "placeholder" pin discipline does not describe the document's own whole-file sha256 pins
- **Severity:** LOW  **Confidence:** CONFIRMED  **dedupe_key:** pin-discipline-covers-only-said-form  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** governance
- **Location:** proposal §3 reading-rule 2, L548–557; §16 predecessor pins L2106–2111
- **Finding:** Reading-rule 2 states a *single* pin discipline: "Where this document pins a digest, the pinned preimage is defined … computed with the digest's own field carrying a **placeholder** of the same length as the encoded digest … forty-four characters is this document's current profile" (L548–557). But the document's most prominent pins — the 4.0 kernel and 3.3 (sha256, 64 hex chars) — are the *other* kind: "whole-file preimage, **no placeholder**" (L2109). §3.2's universal "Where this document pins a digest … carrying a placeholder" is falsified by §16's own pins, and the "44 characters" profile does not cover a 64-char sha256 hex pin. Also, §3.2 does not name the placeholder *character* (the substrate fixes `#`, bible 04 §11); it is inherited-by-reference but not stated.
- **Why it matters:** A reader implementing §3.2 literally applies the placeholder mechanism to *all* pins and cannot reproduce the §16 sha256 (which has no self-referential placeholder field). §16 self-corrects locally, so the harm is contained, but the reading rule overclaims a uniform discipline the document does not follow.
- **Recommendation:** In §3.2 distinguish the two pin forms — (a) self-addressing SAID pins via same-length placeholder-in-field (name the placeholder char or cite the substrate rule), and (b) external whole-file digest pins with no placeholder — so the reading rule matches actual practice.

## Additional patterns noted (checked, not raised as findings)

- **Two-tracks silent registry-form reuse (§17).** A track-one GEL event is byte-identical to an ordinary substrate registry event; a governance-blind consumer sees only registry semantics. This *looks* like my headline silent-downgrade smell, but it is defused by the layered-semantics non-goal: governance meaning exists only under law a consumer committed to, and "no party is subject to a regime it never committed to" (§12.3, L1714–1716). Correct by design; not a finding.
- **Unknown-operator / unrecognized-ilk handling (§17).** Unlike the keripy `verifying.py` L354 coercion the doctrine warns about (I2I fallthrough), Custos draws the pending/refusal line explicitly "at the grammar grain" (L2298–2300): underivable ilk-table → *refuse* (loud); unrecognized ilk under a derivable table → committed evidence judged under law. This is the fail-loud discipline I hunt for, and it is present. Not a finding.
- **"As defined by X" references.** The dossier/ACDC threshold-operator profile (§8, §12) is content-addressed (revision pinned by digest in the engagement companion, §3), and uncommitted composition routes to refusal (§7.5) — a safe default. The residual ACDC edge-operator underspecification (bible 07 §10.5) is a substrate issue Custos cites rather than redefines; hand to KRT/GOV/CSR under `edge-operator-semantics-unenforced` if it surfaces. Not fully mine.
- **Multi-log fold order.** The Gever folds the GEL "in the context of the KELs and TELs it cites"; §17 pins order *within* the GEL (KEL-anchoring order, then seal-list order) but not the cross-log interleave. Defused because canonical defeat-selection (lexicographic min) + subset-monotonicity + the permuted-arrival conformance vectors (L2208–2211) force order-independence of the *result*. Residual, not a finding — but it depends on F1 being fixed, since the canonical selection is the thing that makes interleave irrelevant.

## Residual unknowns

- I cannot close whether F2's second trigger is intended as a distinct path or a redundant gloss without the author's intent; I exhibit the two incompatible readings and the payload clause that only one satisfies. Either way the text needs one sentence.
- Whether F1 and F2 are inherited verbatim from the 4.0 kernel (the appendix says the transition system "embeds without normative alteration" and canonical selection is "carried from the predecessor unchanged," L1129, §7.3) — if so, the "gauntlet round" and census gate did not catch them, which is itself worth noting: a byte-diff census cannot detect a canonical-rule contradiction that no fixture with empty-subcode collisions or non-pair status-evidence was written to exercise. This reinforces F3: the fixture-scale exercise structurally cannot surface F1/F2.
