# Governance, Interop & Lifecycle Architect (GOV) Review: Custos 4.1

**Date:** 2026-07-27  **Target(s):** Custos 4.1 proposal text; `kswg-keri-specification` @ `4df80aa` (re-anchored)  **Effort:** medium  **Objective lens:** survivability
**Sources used:** keri-doctrine (claims 9, 12, 13; three-bucket rule), review-house-style, bible 06 (§1-2, §5-7, §9), bible 05 (§5.1), bible 07 (§5.4, §12, §14), KERI spec `spec-body.md` (message-type/ilk namespace, version-string genus fields — re-anchored)

## Executive summary

In my lens the proposal is **sound and unusually well-defended.** Custos 4.1 is, structurally, a Layer-2 governance engine — it computes "the Constitution" (law-in-force) and four-valued findings from committed law — and it realizes KERI's two-layer split correctly rather than violating it: findings are frame-local ("judgment never crosses frames; evidence does"), cross-frame congruence is explicitly **evidence, never force** with **no canonical comparison algorithm claimed**, the medium **ranks no frame** (no root registry, no super-frame), group-multisig federation is explicitly **rejected**, and governance terms bind by SAID. It even pre-empts my sharpest instrument — the sealed-verdict/evaluation seal is deferred with the exact discipline my lens would demand ("Commit predicates, never verdicts; a sealed verdict a stranger cannot recompute is smuggled authority," §9). Migration is non-retroactive by construction (§16), so it forces **no** re-issuance of deployed holders. The residual findings are three, all at the seams my mandate owns and none of them fatal: (F1, my headline) **track-two governance-minted ilks / genus reservation are self-allocated into the substrate's *shared* wire namespace with steward recognition explicitly NOT a precondition** — a latent cross-frame/substrate namespace-collision hazard; (F2) the "replayable governance / byte-identical agreement" property is a **categorically larger cross-implementation determinism surface** than Kever/Tever because the Gever folds *committed-data* law, and the claim that it "introduces no new anchoring pattern / inherits registry-layer discipline" understates that burden; (F3) the **warranty economics presuppose a credible-threat replayer population that the proposal confesses is absent.** F2 and F3 are largely confessed by the document itself; F1 is the one my lens finds materially under-addressed.

## Steelman

The strongest reading: KERI stops at a deliberately drawn line — an honest validator must not trust duplicitous key state, and *nothing follows*. Every consumer of key state must then improvise the governance layer above that line, and improvisation does not compose across ecosystems. Custos closes exactly the loop KERI leaves open (evidence → finding → grounded act → law → next finding) *without* re-importing an authority: it makes the governance layer a **fold** — a pure, replayable function from committed bytes to findings — so that "who decides what the law means" becomes "recompute it yourself from the committed logs." This is the correct realization of the authenticity/veracity split (doctrine claim 12; bible 06 §1): it does **not** mechanize veracity or fitness (the constructor/`enact` verb, human, stays human); it mechanizes only the *evaluation of already-committed law*, refusing (not legislating) where committed law runs out. That is KERI's own controller/validator division carried up one tier — "only a controller writes a KEL; any validator verifies; no validator may write." The proposal earns further credit for the discipline my lens most often finds missing: it keeps arbitration frame-local, refuses a global frame, binds governance by SAID, defers the one construct (sealed verdicts) that would smuggle authority, and grades every executable claim honestly at fixture scale. This is the anti-federation, self-certifying, no-super-frame posture bible 07 §5.4 endorses, applied to governance itself.

## Top findings

### F1: Governance-minted ilks and self-reserved genus consume the substrate's *shared* wire namespace without steward allocation as a precondition
- **Severity:** MEDIUM  **Confidence:** LIKELY  **dedupe_key:** `governance-genus-ilk-namespace-collision`  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** wire
- **Location:** proposal §17 ("track two uses governance-minted ilks whose ilk registry is itself committed data in the GEL"; "Genus reservation is an enactment; substrate-steward recognition is a distinct later bilateral event, **not a precondition**"); KERI spec `spec-body.md:277-279` (message-type `t` is a fixed 3-char enumerated namespace), `:935` (stream parser pre-routes by message type), `:2149` (`[icp, rot, ixn, dip, drt]`), `:880`/`:915` (`[rct]`, `[qry, rpy, pro, bar, xip, exn]`), `:2077-2086` (genus version is a fixed-width field in the shared version string).
- **Finding:** The KERI/CESR message-type (ilk) space and the protocol genus/version code table are **centrally-allocated, spec-enumerated namespaces** — not content-addressed. This is deliberate: unlike a schema (whose SAID *is* its namespace and cannot collide, bible 06 §5), an ilk or genus code is a short registered token whose collision-freedom depends on a single allocator. bible 06 §6/§7 states the failure mode precisely: a code added in one place but not coordinated across the shared namespace means "a parser that predates your code desyncs the entire stream — silently, because CESR is self-framing." Custos content-addresses everything *else* by SAID, but the two things it cannot content-address — the track-two ilk tokens and the reserved genus — it proposes to **self-mint**, with steward recognition demoted to later "Exhibit-class evidence" rather than a namespace-safety precondition. Intra-frame this is safe (the genesis knot commits the ilk table, readable before any GEL event; §17 bootstrap). The hazard is cross-frame and against the shared substrate wire: two Custos-shaped domains can mint the *same* 3-char ilk with different semantics, and — worse, because GEL events are byte-immutable and the genus rides the version string — a genus the domain self-reserves and anchors into history can later **collide** with a genus the KSWG allocates to a different protocol, retroactively rendering already-anchored bytes ambiguous under the shared allocation.
- **Why it matters:** A stranger's substrate-level stream parser that pre-routes by message type (`spec-body.md:935`) meets an unrecognized `t`, or two frames' streams carry the same genus meaning different code tables — a silent wire desync in exactly the layer doctrine claim 9 names as the top interop invariant. This is not the *confessed* cross-implementation-equality gap (that is about two engines folding the same inputs); it is a distinct, un-addressed **namespace-allocation** gap.
- **Recommendation:** Make substrate-steward genus/ilk **allocation** (not recognition/endorsement) a precondition for track-two events that traverse the shared substrate wire, OR confine track-two ilks to a domain-private carriage that never enters the substrate pre-router, OR require track-two to ride only genus/ilk values inside a steward-reserved experimental range. Track one (reusing substrate registry event forms) already avoids this and should be the recommended default until allocation lands. Co-reported to **CSR** under the shared key (the 24-bit/version-string encoding is CSR's; the allocation *policy* above the bytes is mine).

### F2: The Gever folds committed-data law, so "byte-identical agreement" is a categorically larger determinism surface than Kever/Tever — understated by "no new anchoring pattern"
- **Severity:** MEDIUM  **Confidence:** LIKELY  **dedupe_key:** `replayable-governance-cross-engine-determinism-unproven`  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** governance
- **Location:** proposal §1.2 ("the GEL is 'a TEL-shaped log with governance semantics'... introducing no new anchoring pattern"), §1.5 ("The Gever is the first fold whose transition rule is committed data"; "two conforming engines owe byte-identical agreement on the same committed inputs"), §2/§16 (replay confessed at *fixture scale*, one keripy checkout; cross-implementation equality an undischarged deliverable), §15 (undecidability "whether a finding of undecidability is a finding or a record of incapacity" left **unresolved**), §17 ("Vector families are owed").
- **Finding:** Kever and Tever fold under law fixed by protocol *constants*; two implementations agree because the transition rule is the same fixed function. The Gever folds under law that is **committed data**, enacted and amendable by the domain — §1.5 acknowledges this is "the first fold whose transition rule is committed data." The proposal's byte-parity claim rests on "the observable boundary is fixed; interiors may grow freely," but for a committed-data transition rule the mapping from boundary to output is only guaranteed total and deterministic if the committed law is *not* expressive enough to make evaluation non-terminating or undecidable — which §15 explicitly leaves open. Meanwhile §1.2's "introduces no new anchoring pattern / same discipline KERI's registry layer uses" is true for *anchoring* but glosses that the *determinism/conformance* burden is new and strictly larger than the registry layer's, because registry-layer folds do not evaluate domain-authored predicates. bible 06 §7 is the ground truth: cross-implementation parity is maintained "by convention and vigilance rather than a single shared codebase," and the deepest layers are independently reimplemented — and here the "replayable governance" property *is* that cross-implementation-parity property, and its conformance vectors are still "owed."
- **Why it matters:** The single asserted property that distinguishes Custos from "improvised governance" — that any stranger recomputes the same Constitution/findings "byte for byte" — is the property most exposed to two conforming engines diverging on a hard committed predicate (non-termination, undecidability, differing analysis interiors), and it has no conformance suite yet. Until the both-order permuted-arrival vectors and a second implementation exist, "replayable" is a single-implementation claim, exactly the "passing keripy's own tests is not sufficient" trap (doctrine claim 9).
- **Recommendation:** State a committed **totality/decidability constraint** on the class of predicates admissible in enacted law (so that evaluation is provably terminating and the fold is total), and land the §17 both-order conformance vectors plus a second engine before the "byte-identical agreement" property is relied on outside fixtures. This is largely a proof-scope confession already; the concrete revision is to stop describing the Gever's conformance burden as inherited from the registry layer.

### F3: The warranty economics presuppose a credible-threat replayer population the proposal confesses does not yet exist
- **Severity:** MEDIUM  **Confidence:** CONFIRMED  **dedupe_key:** `warranty-economics-absent-replayer-population`  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** deployment
- **Location:** proposal "economics" framing ("a warrantor's committed backing stands in for re-folding until the credible threat of a stranger folding again is exercised... Judgment stays cheap to verify because it is expensive to fake"); §2/opening ("its deployment-scale record — an open replaying population exercising the credible threat — is a confessed, unfinished deliverable"); §14 availability charter ("never enumerates observers... the deterrence value of unenumerated observation survives only while unenumerated").
- **Finding:** The warranty layer's security rests on the same mechanism as KERI's detection posture: deterrence by *observation that might happen*. A false warranty is "replay-falsifiable, convicting the warrantor on its own signature" — but only if some stranger actually re-folds. bible 07 §14 / cr-ad-trust §5 name this as KERI's genuine ecosystem-maturity residual: "detection-not-prevention is only as good as the watcher/observer infrastructure actually deployed," and "a KERI deployment without robust watchers... is not a secure system." Custos's "credible threat of a stranger folding again" is precisely a watcher/replayer population, and the proposal candidly confesses it is undeployed. Until it exists, "expensive to fake" is unfunded: a false warranty is cheap to *verify* and, absent any actual re-folder, also cheap to *fake*.
- **Why it matters:** This is the load-bearing deployment assumption under the entire replay-amortization economics, and it is the super-watcher/observer-maturity residual (bible 06 §9, DG-C05) applied to Custos. It is an **ecosystem** gap, not a protocol defect, and the proposal is honest that no clause presumes it discharged — so it is not a hidden flaw. I surface it as the assumption a deployer must fund, not as grounds to reject.
- **Recommendation:** Accept as confessed; make the security-economics claims explicitly conditional on a stated minimum replayer/observer deployment, and treat "expensive to fake" as a property that *activates* at a named deployment threshold rather than one the design supplies. Revisit when an open replaying population exists.

## Additional patterns noted

- **Positive — the two-layer split is realized, not violated.** Cross-frame congruence is "evidence, never force" with "no canonical comparison algorithm committed" (§12) — the correct answer to bible 06 §5's "content-addressing fixes identity of meaning, not agreement." No super-frame, no root registry, group-multisig federation explicitly rejected (§12), medium ranks no frame (§5). This is the anti-re-centralization posture bible 07 §5.4 endorses; my decentralization scorecard (bible 06 §8) finds **no drift** — no issuer registry, no verification-time phone-home (the cone is holder-carried, §6), no jurisdiction-bound root.
- **Positive — migration done right.** §16 binds clauses only at/after the effectuation coordinate, "never retroactive"; old positions judge under old law. This forces **no** re-issuance/re-anchoring of deployed holders — the cheap, non-breaking migration KERI is designed for (doctrine claim 13; bible 06 §6). Contrast the schema-SAID-bump-orphans-every-referrer hazard my heuristics hunt: Custos avoids it.
- **Positive — the sealed-verdict deferral pre-empts my headline heuristic.** §9's "Commit predicates, never verdicts" is exactly my undefined-arbiter guard: a recomputable predicate is Layer-2-bound-and-checkable; a sealed verdict a stranger cannot recompute "is smuggled authority." The document draws the line where my lens would.
- **Watch — "adjudicates" verb (D-3 / C-2 repair).** The office verb "a GARD adjudicates" is stronger than KERI's watcher-*observes* register (bible 06 §2; wtbo §4). It survives because adjudication is explicitly frame-local (force is frame-local; across frames it degrades to evidence). No finding, but a reader could mistake a frame-local finding claimed "byte-for-byte recomputable by any stranger" for a Layer-1 universal judgment; §1.6 (color belongs to the domain, evidence is colorless in the commons) is the load-bearing disclaimer and should stay prominent.

## Residual unknowns

- I cannot close whether the admissible-law predicate class is decidable/terminating without the deferred Gever construction and the §15 undecidability resolution — F2 hinges on it.
- I cannot exhibit an actual genus/ilk collision without a second Custos-shaped deployment or a future KSWG allocation into the same slot; F1 is a latent-hazard argument grounded in the shared-namespace mechanism, rated LIKELY not CONFIRMED for that reason.
- Whether track-two is ever expected to traverse the *substrate* wire (vs. staying in a domain-private carriage) is not fully pinned by the proposal; if track-two is provably substrate-private, F1's severity drops to LOW.
