## Executive verdict

In KERI's own terms Custos-4.1 is broadly sound: it faithfully lifts the substrate's detection-not-prevention, deterministic-fold, observer-local posture up one tier and introduces no new anchoring pattern, so the core mechanism is coherent and the objective (survivability, not invulnerability) is honestly chosen. The blocking problems are not conceptual but definitional, and they cluster exactly where the headline promise lives — "any stranger recomputes the same Constitution, byte for byte." Four HIGH findings must be resolved: the governance state machine has no lawful edge or current for prospective revocation of a grounding credential, so the central §1.1 worked example forces two conforming engines to diverge (KRT-F1); the canonical defeat-selection rule is internally contradictory ("lexicographic minimum" vs. "empty subcode orders last"), which falsifies the byte-identity SHALL (SPC-F1); the pending→self-convicted edge admits a trigger its own required payload cannot satisfy (SPC-F2); and the motivating harm is asserted in the abstract but never instantiated against a real applied-corpus schema, leaving adoption cost weighed against an anticipated rather than exhibited need (SKP-F1, needs-info). A supporting cluster of MEDIUMs shows the same fault line — "byte-identical" is claimed while the object encoding is deferred and §16 actually tests only semantic equality (SPC-F3, SPC-F4, GOV-F2), and the "expensive to fake" warranty economics rest on a replayer/observer population the proposal confesses does not yet exist (SEC-F1, GOV-F3). The single most important thing the author should address is to make the byte-identity conformance claim decidable from spec text before attempting the §16 cross-implementation-equality deliverable: pin the state-transition algebra (revocation motion and the self-conviction trigger), the canonical-selection total order, and the finding/refusal serialization — otherwise the one property that distinguishes Custos from improvised governance is unprovable across independent engines.
