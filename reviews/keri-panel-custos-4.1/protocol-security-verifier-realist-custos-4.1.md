# Protocol Security & Verifier Realist (SEC) Review: Custos 4.1 — a standard for governed domains on KERI
**Date:** 2026-07-27  **Target(s):** kswg-keri-specification (spec-body.md @ `4df80aa`)  **Effort:** deep  **Objective lens:** survivability
**Sources used:** keri-doctrine, review-house-style, orchestrating-reviews, bible §§02/03/07, KERI spec-body.md re-anchored (L178, L1719, L1792, L1806-1810, L1842, L1884)

## Executive summary

In my lens the proposal is unusually clean. It is written by someone who has internalized survivability-over-invulnerability and detection-over-prevention: it declines to claim prevention, it keeps evaluate (fold) strictly separated from enact (constructor) as KERI's controller/validator division carried up one tier, it refuses rather than legislates at uncommitted seams, and it grades its own deployment-scale claims as confessed-undischarged. The substrate claims I could check against the live spec are faithful (the promise/physics anchor-grade maps exactly onto superseding rules A0/A1; "recovery is repair" is the spec's own word at L1792; the GEL introduces no new anchoring pattern). I found **one** genuine, in-terms finding worth surfacing: the design's load-bearing framing of *replayable governance* and *warranty economics* ("every verifier computes the duplicity relation identically," "expensive to fake," "any stranger holding the logs recomputes the same Constitution byte for byte") states as an achieved property something KERI makes **conditional on an optional, unenforced observer layer** (A5, A8-A11). The design confesses this at deployment scale, which discharges most of the adoption-obligation — so this is a framing/grading tightening, not an unsoundness. There is no reintroduced authority, no phone-home, no firewall or anchor-relative erosion, and no prevention-dressed-as-detection in the substrate claims.

## Steelman

The strongest version of what Custos 4.1 is doing, in KERI's terms: KERI ends at a deliberately drawn line — an honest validator MUST NOT trust duplicitous key state, and nothing follows (spec L53). Every consumer of key state must then improvise a governance layer above that line, and improvisation does not compose. Custos types that layer as a fold (the Gever) over a committed governance log (the GEL) that is anchored in the gAID's KEL by the *same* seal discipline KERI's registry (TEL) layer already uses — introducing no new anchoring primitive, and inheriting KERI's own determinism: a pure function from committed bytes to computed state that writes nothing. The evaluate/enact split is exactly KERI's controller/validator split ("only a controller writes a KEL; any validator verifies; no validator, however convinced, may write") lifted one tier. Refusal-not-legislation at a missing rule is the governance analogue of KERI dropping an event it cannot authorize. The four-valued finding codomain with mandatory grounds, the no-backward-edge transition system, and "commit predicates, never verdicts" are all disciplined restatements of "a green checkmark is not a truth oracle" and "authenticity is mechanizable, veracity is not." Crucially, the author does not claim a global judge, a super-frame, or convergence: frames compose like charts in an atlas, force is frame-local, permanent divergence is an allowed outcome, and the whole edifice is graded honestly against fixture-scale evidence with cross-implementation replay and deployed-warranty economics confessed as open. This is KERI's posture, faithfully extended — not a PKI/consensus prior smuggled back in.

## Top findings

### F1: The replay/warranty security is observer-dependent; the load-bearing framing states it as achieved rather than conditional on the (confessed-undischarged) observer layer
- **Severity:** MEDIUM  **Confidence:** LIKELY  **dedupe_key:** `governance-replay-depends-on-optional-observer-layer`
- **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** governance
- **Location:** proposal §5 ("a shared medium — key state, SAID-addressed bytes, and the duplicity relation — that every verifier computes identically because agreement is cryptographic, not negotiated"); the "Economics" paragraph ("Judgment stays cheap to verify because it is expensive to fake"); the opening thesis ("any stranger holding the logs recomputes the same Constitution, the same findings, the same refusals, byte for byte"); §12.4 ("mutual convictability over the shared medium"); §13.2 ("cross-frame duplicity as the evidentiary floor"). Re-anchored against KERI spec L178, L1719, L1842, L1884; bible §02 (A5, A8-A11), §02 §6.7.
- **Finding:** The fold is a pure function, so *given identical committed inputs* it yields identical outputs — that part is sound and is all the "cross-implementation replay" deliverable (§16) actually promises. But two of the design's headline sentences quantify over *verifiers*, not over *inputs*, and there KERI's guarantee is weaker in a specific, load-bearing way:
  - **"the duplicity relation... every verifier computes identically."** The duplicity *relation as a function* is frame-invariant, but whether a given verifier *sees* duplicity at all is not: KERI's one-valid-KEL rule is explicitly validator-local ("From a Validator's perspective, either there is one-and-only-one valid KEL or none at all," spec L178), detection requires the verifier to actually *hold* the second conflicting copy ("One verifiable copy... is enough to detect duplicity in any other verifiable but inconsistent copy," L1719), and obtaining that copy depends on observers under the *validator's* control ("a malicious alternate... **may** be eminently detectable," "at the validator's discretion," L1842). Isolation defeats it outright ("a successful dead attack requires the isolation of a validator from ambient sources of the KERL," L1884). None of A8-A11 (observer coverage/diligence/flow/integrity) are enforced by KERI (bible §02 §6.4), and duplicity "may persist indefinitely without detection" (bible §02, §6.7). So "every verifier computes [the duplicity relation] identically" is true of the verdict-given-two-copies but false of the reachability of that verdict.
  - **"expensive to fake" / "the credible threat of a stranger folding again."** This is exactly ambient duplicity detection — which KERI states is "**not** guaranteed by protocol mechanics" (bible §02 §6.3). A false warranty is *replay-falsifiable in principle*, but the falsification only bites if a replaying population actually exists and re-folds. The proposal itself confesses this: "an open replaying population exercising the credible threat is a confessed, unfinished deliverable, and no clause presumes it discharged." The gap is that the falsifiability is asserted as the *reason* judgment is cheap ("expensive to fake"), in the same breath, without the observer-presence conditional inline.
- **Why it matters:** This is the DG-C02/DG-C05 residual (detection delegated to an optional, still-immature observer layer) transposed from KERI's key tier to Custos's governance tier — the *one* class of KERI critique that survives objective-function alignment (bible §02 §12). Concrete failure scenario: a malicious gAID controller running its own witnesses produces two verifiable-but-conflicting KELs (spec-permitted; bible §02 §5.8), anchoring GEL head H_A in one and H_B in the other. Each folds deterministically to a *different* Constitution; each is internally "replayable governance"; and KERI guarantees no meeting of the two verifiers (permanent divergence is an allowed protocol outcome, bible §02 §7.4.4). A warrantor who signs an attestation of the H_A-Constitution is "convicted on its own signature" only for a party that also obtains H_B and re-folds — which, absent the confessed observer/replay population, may be no one. Until then the warranty is, operationally, a staked-but-untested assertion relied on without verification. That is not a phone-home and not reintroduced authority (the warranty travels as replay-falsifiable evidence, no verification-time callback) — it is the honest survivability posture; the only defect is that the framing states the conclusion ("cheap because expensive to fake," "computes identically") as unconditional.
- **Recommendation:** Smallest change: attach the observer-presence conditional *inline* to the three load-bearing sentences, not only to the grading paragraph — e.g. "expensive to fake **wherever a diligent replaying population is present**," and, for the medium, distinguish "every verifier computes the same *verdict on the same two copies*" from "every verifier *observes* the same duplicity" (the latter being observer-dependent and, per §2's own non-goals, undeployed). If any *compositional* clause — §12.4 "mutual convictability," §13.2 "cross-frame duplicity as the evidentiary floor" — is intended as a *guaranteed* property rather than an observer-conditional one, that clause must be re-graded to HIGH and rewritten, because federation recourse would then rest on an unenforced KERI assumption (A8-A11).
- **Bucket:** deployment-gap  **Disposition:** recommend-revise

## Additional patterns noted

- **Anchor-grade promise/physics (§9) is well-grounded, not an overclaim.** "A seal in an interaction event is 'promise' (erasable by superseding recovery); a seal in an establishment event is 'physics'" maps exactly onto the live superseding rules: A0 "Any rotation event may supersede an Interaction event at the same sn" (spec L1806) makes ixn-anchored seals erasable; A1 "A non-delegated rotation may not supersede another rotation" (L1808) plus A2 "An interaction event may not supersede any event" (L1810) make establishment-anchored seals displaceable only by forking the establishment lineage — which is duplicity-evident. The normative "designated act classes SHALL anchor in establishment events" *strengthens* anchoring rather than eroding a firewall. No finding.

- **"Recovery rotation is repair, not duplicity" (§5) is spec-faithful.** The spec uses the same word: recovery "enables the KEL to be repaired so that future validators... will not see the compromised events after recovery" (L1792). The scoping rule — that the governed layer must not misclassify a lawful superseding recovery as governance-tier duplicity — is correct and necessary. (A residual subtlety for SPC/GOV, not SEC: recovery and malicious duplicity are cryptographically *indistinguishable at the moment the fork is created*, resolved only by the reconciliation rules and validator discretion; the proposal's one-line gloss is fine but the governed layer inherits that indistinguishability wholesale.)

- **Governance-tier duplicity is correctly kept law-relative, not medium-grade.** §7.4's "Key-tier duplicity convicts in the medium for every verifier under no frame's law; registry/governance-tier duplicity is law-relative" is the right call and avoids the trap of claiming cryptographic (frame-invariant) force for a fold-computed (law-relative) verdict. Governance duplicity *within one law-history* ("contradictory enactments under one committed predicate") is caught by the fold itself; cross-verifier divergence necessarily reduces to key-tier KEL duplicity, which is medium-grade. The tiering composes. (The phrase "convicts... for every verifier" in isolation could be misread as universal *detection* rather than frame-invariant *verdict* — a spec-precision hazard I hand to SPC under the same dedupe_key.)

- **The genesis knot (§4 gAID / (K0,C)) resolves the self-reference the way KERI's SAID derivation does.** C computed first, referenced via a reserved sentinel resolved at verification, K0 sealing C's SAID, gAID = K0's self-addressed prefix, and gAID excluded from C's transitive closure — this is the dummy-fill self-referential-but-bound pattern (CESR §SAID) applied to a two-artifact genesis. I see no substitution attack: the K0→C binding is a byte-exact digest seal and gAID is the digest of K0, so both directions are content-addressed. Handoff to KRT/SPC for whether the sentinel-resolution semantics are fully specified.

- **No reintroduced authority.** The warranty (§6, §12.3) is a schema-typed, registry-bound ACDC attestation that travels as evidence and is replay-falsifiable — not a verification-time registry callback. The verification cone (§6) is "closed transitively to the depth replay touches" with "exactly one entry point held in advance — the gAID," which is the OOBI-the-root discipline, not a phone-home. The availability charter (§14) is explicitly discharged by each stratum's *witnesses* and "never enumerates observers beyond obligated witness sets" — consistent with witnesses-provide-availability-not-authority. Clean in my lens.

## Residual unknowns

- I could not exercise the fold against any implementation (the proposal itself says the executable evidence is fixture-scale against one keripy checkout, and no keripy code implements a Gever). So F1 rests on the *design's own claims* cross-checked against KERI's substrate guarantees, not on a Gever code path. That is appropriate for a DESIGN review, but it means the "cross-implementation byte-identical replay" deliverable (§16) — the thing that would make "any stranger recomputes byte for byte" a *machine* fact rather than a design aspiration — is genuinely unverified, by the author's own admission.
- Whether §12.4's "mutual convictability" and §13.2's "cross-frame duplicity as the evidentiary floor" are meant as guaranteed or observer-conditional determines whether F1 stays MEDIUM or escalates to HIGH. I read them as observer-conditional (consistent with §2's non-goals and §14's availability charter framed as an *obligation*), but the text does not say so at those two sites. Flagged as the revisit condition.
