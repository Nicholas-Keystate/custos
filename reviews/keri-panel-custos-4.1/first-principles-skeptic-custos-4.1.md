# First-Principles Skeptic Review: Custos 4.1 — a standard for governed domains on KERI

**Date:** 2026-07-27  **Target(s):** /home/daniel/code/3GR/custos/spec/custos-4.1.md (the candidate); kswg-keri-specification (substrate)  **Effort:** medium  **Objective lens:** survivability
**Sources used:** keri-doctrine, review-house-style, orchestrating-reviews, bible 01 §1/§6-8, bible 02 §1-13, bible 07 §9/§10.5/§14; custos-4.1.md re-anchored (line cites below)

## Executive summary

The proposal is unusually candid about its own grade — it confesses, repeatedly and in normative language, that its deployment-scale evidence is unfinished (custos-4.1.md L39-43, L426-427, L1944-1956). That candor discharges most of the *invulnerability*-flavored critiques a reviewer might reach for. It does not discharge mine. My lens asks a prior question the document never answers with a concrete instance: **is there a present presentation, in a real domain, that fails today because governance above the KERI line cannot be replayed — and does Custos actually solve *that* failure?** The document argues the motivating problem entirely in the abstract ("improvisation does not compose", L20-22), names no failing use case from the applied corpus (no vLEI/SEDI/GCD/Dossier presentation is exhibited as breaking), and — at the one seam where the "does not compose" pain actually lives, the cross-frame boundary — its own §12 declines to deliver composition ("commits no canonical comparison algorithm", "congruence is evidence, never force", L1616/L1621). So my two findings are: (F1) the whole standard's demand is *anticipated, not exhibited*; and (F2) the central claimed advantage over do-nothing — that governance *composes* — is not delivered where do-nothing hurts. Neither is a category error against KERI; both are burden-unmet / problem-solution-fit findings, and both are *needs-info* rather than *reject*: name one present presentation, and F1 falls.

## Steelman

**Steelman of the proposal.** KERI deliberately stops at the honest-validator line: "an honest validator MUST trust when there is no evidence of duplicity and MUST NOT trust when there is any evidence of duplicity" (KERI spec, bible 02 §10) — and *nothing follows*. Everything a real relying party does *after* "the key state is honest" — may this holder act in this role, is this credential defeated, what recourse attaches — is today improvised per-deployment in EGF prose, ad-hoc code, and human judgment, and two deployments' improvisations do not interoperate or replay. Custos's genuine insight is that this after-the-line layer can be given the *same* discipline KERI already gives key state: commit the law to an append-only anchored log (the GEL, "a TEL-shaped log with governance semantics", introducing no new anchoring pattern), compute judgment by a pure fold (the Gever, structurally identical to Kever/Tever), and make every governance act itself committed evidence — so that governance becomes *replayable* rather than *testimonial*. Carried up faithfully from KERI are exactly the right things: the controller/validator (enact/evaluate) split so nothing that judges ever writes; refusal-not-legislation at uncommitted seams (the direct analogue of "MUST NOT trust duplicitous state, and nothing follows"); frame-local force with frame-invariant evidence and *no super-frame* (permanent divergence permitted, bible 02 §10, carried up as "no global frame"); and honest, graded confession of what is undischarged. This is minimally-sufficient-means applied one tier up: no exotic crypto, one reused anchoring discipline, capability added as a thin layer. If some real domain today truly cannot replay its own governance and is harmed by that, this is a plausibly-correct shape for the fix.

**Steelman of do-nothing (the null hypothesis I am obligated to try to win with).** KERI-core already refuses to trust duplicitous state; ACDC already carries governance in the committed, SAID-bound `r` (rule) section, statically schema-verified; the vLEI/SEDI/Dossier stacks already commit their rulebooks and chain issuers so a verifier "can traverse the chain back to GLEIF without consulting a trust registry — the entire graph is self-certifying" (bible 01 §7). And the doctrine explicitly assigns the layer *above* authenticity — "what a credential means, whether an issuer is fit", standing, veracity — to Layer-2 human/governance judgment, warning "never automate the verification of the proof" (bible 01 §7; bible 07 §9.2-9.3). So the do-nothing position is strong: a committed `r`-section rule plus a committed EGF plus a human at the end already gives a domain a replayable *record of its law* and a place to apply it. What do-nothing lacks is a *typed, folded, byte-identical* recomputation of the governance verdict — which is exactly what Custos adds. The question my findings turn on is whether that delta answers a present harm.

## Top findings

### F1: The motivating problem is asserted in the abstract; no present failing use case is exhibited — the entire standard's demand is anticipated, not present

- **Severity:** HIGH  **Confidence:** CONFIRMED  **dedupe_key:** governed-domain-present-need-unexhibited  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** governance
- **Location:** proposal "animating thesis" and Why section; custos-4.1.md L17-43 (motivating claim), L426-427 / L1944-1956 (self-confessed fixture-only scale). Applied-corpus search of the live spec returns *no* named present user that fails today (grep: no vLEI/SEDI/GCD presentation exhibited as breaking).
- **Finding:** The document states its problem — "detection without consequence leaves every consumer of key state to improvise its own governance above that line, and improvisation does not compose" (L19-22) — and never instantiates it. It exhibits no concrete present-day presentation, in any real schema in the applied corpus (vLEI, SEDI, GCD, Dossier), that a verifier cannot handle today for lack of a Gever/GEL/Constitution. Its own record confesses the evidence is "exercised against one implementation at one pinned checkout, with purpose-built identifiers" (L1954-1955) and that the open-replaying-population deliverable is "unfinished" (L41-43). By the SKP method's first step (doctrine §Objective-function-first; bible 01 §1, capability added "when needed, not ahead of need"), the burden is on the expanding proposal to show a present failing case, and that burden is unmet: the demand is anticipated.
- **Why it matters:** A complete 17-section standard (seven primitives, a four-valued codomain, seal ladder, object classes, transformation law, recourse ladder, GEL grammar) is a very large surface — an implementer/conformance/interop obligation across the four KERI implementations and a maintenance liability — justified by a hand-waved problem statement. If the real present pain is, say, "every EGF reinvents governance semantics and they don't interoperate," that is a *nameable* instance (GLEIF's vLEI EGF; a Provenant framework) with an exhibitable failing presentation — but the document argues it abstractly and names none, so a reader cannot check that the mechanism's size matches the harm's size. This is the exact shape my null hypothesis exists to test: mechanism described at length, problem in one hand-wave.
- **Bucket:** non-goal (governance/veracity above the authenticity line is substantially handed by KERI to Layer-2 human judgment and EGFs — bible 01 §7, bible 07 §9.3; the proposal to mechanize it bears the burden, here unmet). Overlaps deployment-gap.
- **Recommendation (proposed, not imposed):** Exhibit at least one *present* presentation — a real or imminent governance decision in a named applied-corpus domain — that a today's verifier cannot recompute, and show the do-nothing path (committed `r`-section rule + EGF + Layer-2) failing on it. That single worked instance would convert this from `needs-info` toward `recommend-accept`. Absent it, adoption of the *standard* should wait on the demonstrated need even though the *design* is coherent.

### F2: The claimed advantage over do-nothing — that governance *composes* — is not delivered at the cross-frame seam where "improvisation does not compose" actually hurts

- **Severity:** MEDIUM  **Confidence:** CONFIRMED  **dedupe_key:** custos-composition-claim-overreaches-frame-boundary  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** governance
- **Location:** proposal animating thesis (L20-22) vs §12 Computed congruence; custos-4.1.md L1608-1626 ("commits no canonical comparison algorithm", "predicate congruence... travels at conjecture grade", "congruence... is evidence, never force. It confers nothing").
- **Finding:** The motivating complaint is that improvised governance *does not compose across consumers*. But Custos's own composition law delivers byte-identical replay only *within* a frame. Across frames it explicitly declines to compose: predicate congruence "is computable only under a stated lens, this document commits no canonical comparison algorithm" (L1614-1616); "Congruence of either grade is evidence, never force. It confers nothing — no standing, no adoption, no recognition follows" (L1621-1623); and "what the finding confers is B's Constitution's decision" (§12.2 Confer). So the very thing the problem statement faults do-nothing for — different frames applying incompatible, non-composing governance — is *preserved by design*, not solved. Custos makes intra-frame governance *replayable* (a real, smaller property); it does not make governance *compose* between sovereign frames.
- **Why it matters:** The headline benefit ("improvisation does not compose" → "this standard specifies the layer that gives... consequence... replayable governance") reads as if composition across consumers is the deliverable. It is not; each frame still decides locally and cross-frame congruence is lens-relative and non-forcing. A reader weighing whether to adopt the standard over do-nothing may over-credit it, buying a large surface for a benefit (intra-frame replayability) narrower than the one advertised (composition). The delta over "a committed EGF rulebook + Layer-2" is *replayable recomputation within one domain*, which is worth stating honestly rather than framing as solving composition.
- **Bucket:** non-goal — cross-frame convergence is a KERI non-goal the proposal *correctly* declines (permanent divergence permitted even with observers present, bible 02 §10; carried up faithfully as "no global frame"). The finding is that the *motivating framing overreaches* what §12 delivers, not that §12 is wrong.
- **Recommendation:** Restate the animating claim to what §12 actually delivers: intra-frame *replayable* governance (verdicts recomputable byte-for-byte by any stranger holding one frame's logs), explicitly *not* cross-frame composition — cross-frame you get committed, replay-falsifiable *evidence* (edicts, warranties, congruence measurements) that each sovereign frame appraises under its own law. This is the honest and still-valuable claim, and it removes the tension with the design's own (correct) no-super-frame stance.

## Additional patterns noted

- **Premature-generality / completeness-for-symmetry (folded into F1, not separately manifested to avoid padding).** The document regenerates the *whole* kernel — every noun, verb, axiom, seal kind, object class — as a complete grid before any cell is shown with a real emitting presentation. The YAGNI-minimal path the document never steelmans as do-nothing: a single domain committing its rulebook to a TEL-shaped log and applying it, with the existing ACDC `r`-section and human Layer-2 covering the rest. I do not manifest this as its own finding because (a) a *specification* is legitimately allowed to be comprehensive, and (b) coherence-of-the-grid is KRT's call, not mine; my in-lens content here is exactly F1's "no exhibited present user per construct."

- **"Consequence as computation" survives the authenticity-vs-veracity boundary — deliberately noted as a NON-finding.** My reflex was to fault "a finding is a judgment; recourse is consequence as computation" (L1767, L1782-1783) against the doctrine's "never automate the verification of the proof" (bible 07 §9.3). It survives: the fold only *applies committed law* to committed evidence (Layer-1-shaped, mechanizable authenticity of the computation), while *enactment* — choosing the law, seating authorities — remains the human trust decision (the constructor's verb). That decomposition actually honors "automate the proof; do not automate the trust decision." I flag it here so a sibling does not re-raise it as a category error; it is not one.

- **Honest-grading discipline is real and should be credited.** §14's "stated evidence scale" rule ("a claim borrowing a scale it was never exercised at is a defect") and §16's two named undischarged deliverables are exactly the doctrine's "state guarantees relative to assumptions" (bible 02 §6) carried into the document's own self-description. This is why my findings are `needs-info`/`revise`, not `reject`: the author has not overclaimed the *evidence*; the gap is that the *motivating problem* is never instantiated and the *composition* claim is looser than §12.
