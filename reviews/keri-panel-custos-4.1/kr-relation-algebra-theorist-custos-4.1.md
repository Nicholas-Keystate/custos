# KR & Relation-Algebra Theorist (KRT) Review: Custos 4.1

**Date:** 2026-07-27  **Target(s):** proposal "Custos 4.1"; substrate re-anchored against `kswg-keri-specification` @ `4df80aa`  **Effort:** deep  **Objective lens:** survivability
**Sources used:** keri-doctrine, review-house-style, orchestrating-reviews, bible 05/06/07, `kswg-keri-specification/spec/spec-body.md` (re-anchored: seals-anchor-into-KEL L31/L1690; first-seen/superseding/duplicity-DAG L1788/L1792)

## Executive summary

Custos 4.1 is, in my lens, unusually disciplined: several of its relations are *correctly* refused where a naive design would over-reach (federation is kept explicitly non-transitive — "no super-frame," `n(n-1)/2` pairwise envelopes; color is kept non-intrinsic — evidence "has no color of its own"; genesis breaks its self-reference cycle with a SAID-sentinel the way SAID dummy-fill does). The four-value codomain's transition system is also combinatorially complete (5 permitted + 7 forbidden = all 12 non-identity edges over 4 states, forming a clean P<{A,D}<S diamond). So most of the algebra composes.

My one load-bearing finding is at the heart of that codomain: the **"settled findings do not flip / affirmed is final under evidence growth" wall (§7.3, and a §15 *fixed* wall) has no lawful transition or "current" for the single most ordinary event in the substrate it sits on — prospective revocation of a grounding credential.** The document's own worked example ("may the holder of credential X act in role Y?") flips from *affirmed* to *defeated* the moment credential X's TEL emits a `rev` event, at a fixed GEL law head — precisely the A→D edge the transition system forbids, and a motion the two named "currents" (defeat-annihilates-retroactively / duplicity-taints) do not classify. Beyond that: the "seven-primitive composition" closure is asserted without a composition operator (definitional coverage dressed as an algebra), "predicate congruence" mislabels a directional subsumption relation, and §8 borrows the ACDC operator grammar's syntax while silently widening its codomain from boolean to four-valued.

## Steelman

The strongest reading of Custos: it lifts KERI's controller/validator division up one tier into an **evaluate/enact** split, and types the whole governed domain as *folds over committed logs* — the Gever is Tever with its transition rule parameterized by committed law rather than fixed by protocol (§1.5 concedes exactly this: "one taxonomy column changes — 'whose law?' — everything else is inherited," so it does *not* claim a spuriously-new relation). Judgments become pure functions of a closed triple (evidence bundle, law head, position), which is genuinely replayable, and the four-value finding codomain with mandatory grounds is a real improvement over a boolean valid/invalid: it distinguishes "defeated with citation," "pending with the typed requirement that would discharge it," and "self-convicted with proof," and it correctly routes a *missing rule* to refusal rather than to a finding. The transition system, the seal ladder, the two propagation currents, and the consumption/federation relations are each distinct machine relations with distinct emitters and distinct downstream behavior — this is not a family filled for symmetry. The genesis knot, the non-transitive federation, and the non-intrinsic color show an author who knows which closures to *decline*. My job is only to test whether the relations the design *does* claim actually deliver what their names and walls promise.

## Top findings

### F1: The "no-flip / affirmed-is-final" wall has no lawful edge for prospective revocation of a grounding credential
- **Severity:** HIGH  **Confidence:** LIKELY  **dedupe_key:** `finding-no-flip-conflicts-with-revocation-of-grounds`  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** governance
- **Location:** proposal §7.3 ("five permitted transitions ... seven forbidden ... affirmed/defeated are final except for arrival of a bearing contradictory pair (→ self-convicted)"), §7.4 (the two currents), §15 (fixed wall "no backward edge"); §1.1 worked example; grounded on substrate revocation semantics — `kswg-keri-specification/spec/spec-body.md` L1788/L1792 (first-seen, superseding, TEL/registry state) and bible 05 §9 (revocation is a TEL *state*, not deletion).
- **Finding:** The transition system is defined over a *closed triple* (evidence bundle, committed law head, position) and its "transitions" are "conditioned on evidence growth" at, implicitly, a fixed law head. Custos reads three logs; a credential's issuance/revocation lives in a **TEL**, whose state is registry state (Tever fold), *not* the GEL law head (Gever fold). So a standing finding — "may the holder of credential X act in role Y?" — that is *affirmed* while X is issued becomes *defeated* the moment X's TEL emits a `rev` event: this is pure evidence growth at a **fixed GEL law head**, and it is exactly the `affirmed → defeated` edge §7.3 lists as *forbidden* and §15 fixes as a wall. The two upward "currents" of §7.4 do not classify it either: defeat "annihilates upward (dependents were never valid — discovery, not change)" describes *retroactive* invalidity, but a validly-issued-then-revoked credential was *not* "never valid" (that would violate the document's own non-retroactivity, §11/§13); and duplicity-taint requires a fork, which revocation is not. Ordinary prospective revocation is a third motion the codomain's propagation algebra does not name.
- **Why it matters:** Two conforming Gevers will diverge on the central case. Implementer A, reading "affirmed is final under evidence growth" literally, keeps the standing *affirmed* after revocation (unsound — force grounded on a revoked credential). Implementer B routes it to *defeated* (violating the fixed "no-flip" wall). Implementer C, to preserve the wall, must classify *every revocable-grounded standing* as perpetually `pending` (§7.2 "window-open") — but since nearly every real ACDC is revocable, that makes the *affirmed* state almost unreachable and guts the codomain. Implementer D treats affirmed@position-p and defeated@position-p′ as two independent immutable findings and declares no rule violated — under which reading the "no-flip" wall is *trivially* true (you never overwrite a fixed triple's finding) but delivers *none* of the durability its prose asserts, because the answer to the live question still flips. The proposal's `recourse SHALL be grounded only on terminal findings` rule (§13.1) inherits the ambiguity: is a post-revocation standing "terminal"?
- **Recommendation:** State explicitly which reading governs. The cleanest is: findings are position-indexed and immutable, "no-flip" means "a finding at a fixed triple is never rewritten" (not "the answer to a question is stable over positions"), and revocation of a ground is a lawful `affirmed@p → defeated@p′` *across positions* — then either add a third propagation current for prospective lifecycle termination, or fold it into "defeat" while dropping the "dependents were never valid" retroactive gloss for the revocation case. Re-word §7.3/§15 so "settled findings do not flip" cannot be read as durability of an answer over evidence/position growth.

### F2: The "seven primitives compose into every construct" closure is asserted with no composition operator — definitional coverage, not an algebra
- **Severity:** MEDIUM  **Confidence:** LIKELY  **dedupe_key:** `seven-primitive-closure-has-no-composition-operator`  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** governance
- **Location:** proposal §1 ("every later construct is a named composition of those seven"), §1.7 the comprehension gate ("a construct that cannot state its composition is declared a defect" — made normative for the document itself), Appendix D-3 (the two-census register).
- **Finding:** The document leans on type/algebra vocabulary as *normative machinery*: "seven primitives," "named composition," "the Gever's public type," "codomain," "closed." A closure claim ("every construct is a composition of the seven") is only checkable if there is a defined **composition operation** over the generator set {Log, Fold, Finding, Seal, Succession, Evaluate, Enact} with a closure law. None is given — there is no operator that takes primitives and yields a construct, no arity, no associativity, no typing rule for a well-formed composition. "Composition" here means "definable using these seven terms" (definitional reduction / vocabulary coverage), which is a documentation discipline, not an algebra. The Appendix confirms this: the comprehension gate is discharged by a *line-census* (a first-match classifier bins each departed predecessor line into one accounted group), i.e. by demonstrating *line coverage*, never by exhibiting a compositional derivation of any construct. So the strong-sounding "type-theoretic" guarantee delivers only "every term is glossed in these words."
- **Why it matters:** The comprehension gate is normative ("declared a defect") but unfalsifiable as stated: because there is no composition calculus, two readers cannot objectively adjudicate whether a given construct (e.g. "warranty," "organ," "frame," "color") *has* stated its composition or merely been paraphrased. A normative gate that cannot be mechanically failed is a gate "that has never been shown failing" — which the document itself (§17) calls "not yet a guard."
- **Recommendation:** Either (a) define the composition operator(s) and the closure/typing law so "X is a composition of the seven" is a checkable judgment, or (b) down-scope §1/§1.7 to what is actually delivered — a *definitional-reduction* discipline ("every construct is *defined in terms of* the seven"), dropping the "composition"/"closed algebra" framing that promises a calculus the document does not supply.

### F3: "Predicate congruence" names a directional subsumption relation with a term reserved for equivalence
- **Severity:** LOW  **Confidence:** CONFIRMED  **dedupe_key:** `predicate-congruence-not-an-equivalence`  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** governance
- **Location:** proposal §12.1 ("Computed congruence: digest congruence (clauses equal by SAID) is medium-grade; predicate congruence (rules one Constitution's rules satisfy) ...").
- **Finding:** The family "congruence" spans two relations of different algebraic type. *Digest congruence* (clause equality by SAID) is a genuine **equivalence** (reflexive, symmetric, transitive). *Predicate congruence* — "rules one Constitution's rules satisfy" — is a **directional satisfaction/subsumption** relation (X's rules satisfy Y's ≠ Y's satisfy X's); it is a preorder, not symmetric, hence not a congruence in the relation-algebra sense the word denotes. Naming an asymmetric relation "congruence" overloads a term whose whole content is symmetry.
- **Why it matters:** Low, because the proposal defuses the machine consequence ("congruence of either grade is evidence, never force"; "no canonical comparison algorithm is committed"). But the label invites an implementer or a governance reader to assume symmetry — "A is predicate-congruent to B" ⟹ "B is predicate-congruent to A" — which the satisfaction reading does not support, and to treat one-directional rule-subsumption as mutual agreement.
- **Recommendation:** Rename the directional grade (e.g. "predicate subsumption" or "predicate satisfaction"), or, if mutual satisfaction is intended, state the symmetric-closure requirement explicitly. Reserve "congruence" for the SAID-equality grade.

### F4: §8 borrows the ACDC operator grammar's *syntax* but widens its codomain from boolean to four-valued without saying so
- **Severity:** LOW  **Confidence:** LIKELY  **dedupe_key:** `acdc-operator-grammar-codomain-mismatch`  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** acdc
- **Location:** proposal §8 ("composition rules ... MAY be expressed in the ACDC edge/threshold-operator grammar (as profiled by the dossier spec); an unsatisfied operator group discharges as pending (enumerating unfilled slots), not as defeat"); ground: bible 05 §5 (m-ary operators `AND/OR/NAND/NOR/AVG/WAVG` are a *boolean* validity calculus, "group valid iff all members valid," ACDC spec §Edge Section) and §5.1 (in the reference verifier the group operators are "read but unused," landmine L16). *Note: the ACDC spec is not in my re-anchoring target set (kswg-keri-specification); this rests on the bible cite, hence LIKELY.*
- **Finding:** The ACDC/Dossier operator grammar returns a two-valued verdict (member/group valid or not). The proposal maps it onto its four-valued finding codomain with a "pending, enumerating unfilled slots" readout — which requires the evaluation to (a) emit a third value distinct from valid/invalid and (b) report *which* members are unfilled and *why* (absent vs present-but-invalid), information the boolean `AND`/`OR` discards. So "expressed in the ACDC operator grammar" undersells a real semantic lift: the grammar supplies the AND/OR *tree syntax*; the four-valued evaluation over that tree is new (and, in the substrate, the group operators are not even enforced).
- **Why it matters:** Low and largely rescued by steelman (a clean syntax/semantics split is a legitimate intent). But left implicit it invites a reader to believe an off-the-shelf ACDC operator evaluator yields Custos findings, when in fact the Gever must supply the four-valued, slot-enumerating semantics itself.
- **Recommendation:** State that §8 borrows only the operator grammar's *structure* (the AND/OR/threshold tree) and that the four-valued discharge (including "enumerate unfilled slots") is Gever-supplied evaluation, not ACDC operator semantics. Hand wire-precision to SPC/CSR and enforcement-in-code to SEC under the shared key.

## Additional patterns noted
- **Two "distance" taxonomies with no stated correspondence.** §11's relation axis is four-valued (self-directed / seat / adoption / peer); §13.2's recourse ladder is five-rung (within-frame / across-federation / under-consumption / into-commons / in-medium), plus §13.3's fork as a sixth. Both are described as graduating by "distance from the frame's keys," yet no mapping between the two axes is given. Not a defect (they measure different things — what is governed vs. where recourse lands), but the shared "distance" framing invites a reader to expect a 1:1 alignment that is not claimed. A one-line disclaimer would remove the trap.
- **Genesis knot is sound (steelman, not a finding).** The gAID↔K0↔C self-reference is broken exactly as SAID dummy-fill breaks self-addressing: C is computed first through a reserved sentinel, K0 seals SAID(C), gAID is K0's prefix, and the "gAID SHALL NOT appear in C or anything C cites transitively" exclusion is *automatically* satisfied by the temporal/hash acyclicity of SAID references (nothing C cites can contain a gAID that does not yet exist), with the residual fixpoint-forgery case foreclosed by preimage resistance. The exclusion is defensive restatement, and the transitive-closure walk terminates because SAID citation graphs are acyclic by construction. I checked whether this was an "assert-what-is-derivable" redundancy and concluded it is defensible explicit-checkability, not a defect.
- **Transition-system completeness is genuine (steelman).** 5 permitted + 7 forbidden exhausts the 12 non-identity edges over 4 states; the permitted edges are exactly the up-edges of the P<{A,D}<S lattice; "no backward edge" is consistent with monotone layering. This is a correctly-enumerated relation — the F1 problem is not incompleteness but the omission of a *motion* (prospective revocation) from that enumeration's evidence-growth model.
