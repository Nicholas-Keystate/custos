## S7-1 — the law: rotation moves no decryption capability

A KERI rotation — bare, delegated, or superseding-recovery —
transfers signing authority through key state and moves zero
decryption capability. Ciphertext binds to keys, never to key
state: material sealed to a rotated-away key remains sealed to
that key after a lawful, witnessed, perfectly executed recovery.
A domain that executes supplement 5's entire recovery seam
flawlessly hands its successor the name and a library of
unopenable boxes, unless confidentiality was separately
provisioned before the seam was crossed.

The live specimen, at fixture scale: the workspace domain's
census convicted its own data-encryption-key custody stale
(`dek-cipher-stale`) after two lawful governed rotations of the
custodian identifier — the sealed key remained bound to the
pre-rotation verification key, and the cure was a pre-enrolled
hardware recipient, not any rotation. The conviction, the cure,
and the rewrap law that followed are committed in that domain's

## S7-3 — distinct offices: continuity authority is not a recipient

Supplement 5's continuity authority (S5-4) ends vacancies of the
*name*. It holds, by that office, no decryption capability. The
office that carries sealed material across absence is the
**pre-positioned recipient**: enrolled at seal time or never.
The two offices MAY coincide only by declaration; a domain whose
law assumes recovery implies estate access has committed the
precise error S7-1 names. The asymmetry is structural: the name
rail's recovery can be arranged at need (delegation, multisig,
the S5 seam); the sealed rail's recovery is precommit-only — no
retroactive act covers material already sealed to absent keys.
