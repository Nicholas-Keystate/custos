VERDICT: REFUTED — margin (i) Ordered fibers

**Exhibit:** KERI (Key Event Receipt Infrastructure), Samuel M. Smith, design documents and KAWA working-group papers, c. 2021–2023 (UNVERIFIED: exact document title, date, and page — working from memory of the KERI specification corpus, not from the document in front of me).

**Exact clause destroyed:** "Prior equivocation formalisms treat any divergence as a fork; here supersession makes some same-coordinate divergence *lawful*, and duplicity is specifically the *incomparable* pair."

**Mechanism:** KERI's data model assigns each key event a coordinate (prefix, sequence number). It distinguishes **establishment events** (inception, rotation) from **interaction events**. At a given sequence number, a rotation event lawfully supersedes interaction events — this is the recovery mechanism. Two events at the same (prefix, sn) that are not in a supersession relationship constitute **duplicity**. This is precisely a partial order on the fiber over each coordinate (rotation > interaction at same sn; events of the same type are incomparable) with duplicity defined as the incomparable pair (antichain of size ≥ 2). KERI does not treat all same-coordinate divergence as a fork — it explicitly carves out lawful recovery via rotation. The claim's sweeping assertion that "prior equivocation formalisms treat any divergence as a fork" is false: KERI is a prior equivocation formalism that does exactly what margin (i) claims as novel. The docket itself lists "KERI's own duplicity doctrine" as a suspect and the formalization under attack is transparently a mathematical restatement of KERI's design doctrine. Whether KERI uses the exact words "partial order" and "antichain" is immaterial — the concept (lawful supersession coexisting with equivocation detection, duplicity = incomparable pair) is fully present in KERI's architecture. The only residual novelty would be the mathematical language, which is a formalization of existing design, not the concept the margin claims.

VERDICT: NOT REFUTED — margin (ii) Quantitative layer

**Near-miss 1 (strongest): BFT forensics.** Sheng, Wang, Nayak, Kannak, Viswanath, "BFT Forensics: An Accountability Framework for BFT Protocols," 2021. This work quantifies how many honest replicas suffice to convict equivocators from transcripts — a quantitative sufficiency bound for equivocation detection. **Why it falls short:** The bound is a combinatorial threshold (honest vs. Byzantine replica counts), not a graph-expansion/spectral-gap argument. The claim's margin (ii) specifically prices detection as observation-graph expansion against a partition-budget adversary, framing "how many watchers" as a spectral-gap sufficiency question. BFT forensics asks "how many honest witnesses," not "what connectivity/expansion must the observation graph have." Different adversary model (threshold adversary vs. partition-budget adversary), different mathematical tool (counting vs. spectral graph theory). The quantitative *spirit* is anticipated; the specific *framing* is not.

**Near-miss 2: Eclipse-attack defense literature.** Heilman, Kendler, Zohar, Goldberg, "Eclipse Attacks on Bitcoin's Peer-to-Peer Network," USENIX Security 2015. Models the adversary as partitioning the observer graph and the defense as peer-diversity/connectivity. **Why it falls short:** This addresses network-layer partition attacks (isolating a node from the network), not equivocation detection. The graph-expansion-as-defense insight is present, but the application is to preventing eclipse, not to connecting deceived partitions for duplicity detection. The connection between "graph expansion prevents partitions" and "graph expansion ensures equivocation detection" is not made.

**Near-miss 3: Certificate Transparency gossip.** RFC 6962 §5/§7.3; Chuat et al., "Consistent View of Certificate Transparency Logs," 2015; Nordberg et al. CT gossip drafts. These establish that split-view detection requires cross-observer comparison and discuss gossip-based connectivity for detection. **Why it falls short:** They establish the qualitative connection (detection requires communication), not a quantitative spectral-gap bound. No expansion-based sufficiency criterion for how much gossip connectivity is enough.

VERDICT: REFUTED — margin (iii) Chart-relativity

**Exhibit:** The claim's own definitions (internal refutation) plus Bitcoin's UTXO-based double-spend detection as external counterexample (Nakamoto, "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System," 2008; UNVERIFIED: whether the claim's authors would classify Bitcoin's UTXO references as "coordinates").

**Exact clause destroyed:** "Under content addressing alone (no coordinates), the criterion is undefinable: each conflicting event is internally valid and observations factor as innocent partial visibility. Equivocation is a property of the coordinate fibration, not of any event."

**Mechanism (internal — circularity):** The formalism *defines* duplicity as "an antichain of size ≥ 2 in some fiber" (clause 4), and fibers are defined by the coordinate projection π(event) = (pre, sn) (clause 1). The chart-relativity lemma then says: without coordinates, the criterion (antichain in coordinate fibers) is undefinable. This is true *by definition* — removing the fibration removes the fibers, and a property defined on fibers is undefinable without them. The lemma is tautological: it adds zero information beyond clauses 1 and 4. A tautological lemma cannot be "a stated lemma rather than folklore" because there is no folkloric insight to formalize — the result is immediate from the definitions. The novelty claim for margin (iii) is that this is a substantive formalization of a previously informal idea; but the idea, as stated, is a definitional triviality, not a substantive result awaiting formalization.

**Mechanism (external — counterexample):** If the lemma is interpreted non-tautologically — as claiming that content-addressed systems without (pre, sn)-style coordinates cannot define equivocation — then Bitcoin refutes it. Bitcoin transactions are content-addressed (by hash). There are no KERI-style sequence numbers or rotations. Equivocation (double-spending) is defined and detected: two transactions spending the same UTXO constitute equivocation. The UTXO reference (txid, output_index) identifies the "same instance" being equivocated over, but it is part of the transaction's content, not a coordinate fibration imposed on the event space. The equivocation is a property of the events' semantic content (they reference the same UTXO), not of a coordinate fibration. The claim's assertion that "equivocation is a property of the coordinate fibration, not of any event" is false in this model: it is a property of the events (they spend the same UTXO), detectable without any coordinate fibration.

The lemma is thus either **tautological** (true by definition, no novelty) or **false** (refuted by Bitcoin), depending on whether "coordinate" is read broadly enough to include any instance-identifying reference. The claim does not resolve this ambiguity. Either way, the novelty claim — that this is a substantive lemma elevating folklore to formal result — fails.

**DRIFT**

- The detection theorem's "iff" (clause 6) conflates *eventual* detection with *timely* detection. The "iff" is correct as a statement about eventual connectivity, but the claim's framing ("duplicity is detected iff the comparison graph connects the deceived partition") silently assumes detection occurs before harm. Under asynchrony, the partition may be connected only after deceived parties have already acted on the equivocated event. This doesn't refute the "iff" (it's logically correct) but the claim should distinguish "detection is possible" from "detection is useful."

- The honesty criterion (clause 3, "the union of all observers' holdings is a chain") is ambiguous about temporal convergence. If observer 1 holds {I₁} and observer 2 holds {R₁} (where R₁ supersedes I₁ at the same sn), the union {I₁, R₁} is a chain — but this is only true once both observations are collected. During the transient period, observer 1's holding alone ({I₁}) is trivially a chain. The criterion can only be evaluated post-hoc by an omniscient observer. This is not a refutation (the criterion is defined on the union, not on individual observations), but it limits the criterion's operationalizability.

- The partial-order question (correctness attack) has a potential edge case: if the superseding rule is "rotation supersedes interactions issued *before* it" (temporal rather than per-sn), then post-rotation interactions at the same sn would be incomparable to the rotation, creating an antichain from honest sequential behavior (issue interaction, rotate, issue another interaction). Whether this is a soundness violation depends on whether the system permits post-rotation interactions at the same sn. The claim's formalization does not specify the superseding rule precisely enough to rule this out.
