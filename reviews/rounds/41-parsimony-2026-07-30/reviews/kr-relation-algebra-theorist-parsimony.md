## Executive summary

Custos 4.1 survives the reduction test better than the charge anticipates. Applied construct by construct, most of the vocabulary is *role naming over substrate mechanism*, and the document usually says so itself: the edict reduces to a bare SAD plus a source seal; the cone reduces to a verifiable dossier plus a replay-closure predicate; the warranty reduces to a schema-typed, registry-bound ACDC; the finding codomain reduces to the Kever/Tever acceptance machinery with the ground promoted into the type; the GEL's *log* reduces to a TEL and the document's own §17 track one demonstrates the reduction on the wire. In my lens the only construct that genuinely fails to reduce is the **Gever** — a fold whose transition rule is committed data rather than a protocol constant — and that is exactly the thing §1.5 says is new. The seven-primitive budget largely holds.

The defects I found are not inflation; they are **under-specified relations**. The single biggest is F1: one and the same relation — *does committed rule-set R satisfy committed clause-set C* — appears in this document under three names (covenant-seal verification, predicate congruence, semantic color comparison) at **three mutually incompatible grades**, ranging from "no canonical algorithm is committed, conjecture grade" (§12.1) to "convictable on the seal's own bytes" (§9, §16). The ruling docket's R12 disposed of the congruence half on the ground that "the blast radius is zero"; §16 falsifies that, because the standard's own succession-validity check is an instance of the relation. Second (F2): a warranty is required to pin only its **lens**, while the replay triple that identifies a finding is *(bundle, law head, position)* — so a warrantor accused of a false warranty can always answer "I folded a smaller bundle," which §7.3's own evidence-ordering makes a *lawful* answer. "Replay-falsifiable by construction" does not hold as constructed, and the abstract's economics rest on it.

Five findings, ranked. Nothing in my lens is manufactured; three constructs I expected to fault (the cone, the edict's no-issuer rule, the finding codomain) survived the reduction test and I say why.

## Steelman

The strongest reading of Custos 4.1, in KERI's own terms:

KERI draws a line and stops on purpose. "There MUST be at most one valid KEL for any identifier or none at all"; duplicity is made *evident and attributable*, and what a relying party then owes is deliberately out of scope (doctrine claims 2–3; bible 02). Above that line, every deployment improvises — and the improvisations do not compose, because each one is a *judge testifying* rather than a *computation any stranger can rerun*. Custos's move is to carry KERI's own trick up one tier: where KERI made key state a **fold over committed bytes** instead of an assertion by an authority, Custos makes *law in force* a fold over committed bytes instead of a document interpreted by an administrator. That is not a governance layer bolted on; it is the same construction at a third rung, and §1.5 identifies precisely the one place where the analogy is not free — the Kever's and Tever's transition rules are protocol constants, and the Gever's is committed, amendable data in the log it reads.

That single discontinuity is what earns the vocabulary. If the transition rule is data, then: it must be positionally resolved (law never applies to itself at a coordinate — §11), the ground must be *in the type* rather than annotated on it (§7.1, because a stranger recomputing needs the citation to recompute against), refusal must be a first-class outcome distinct from a finding (§7.5, because an evaluator that fills a gap in committed law has silently become a constructor), and law's own change must be an event judged under the law before it (§1.2 succession). Each of those is forced by the discontinuity, not decorative.

The document also *declines* the moves a reviewer expects it to make. It rejects the joint multi-signature identifier for federation on the explicit ground that "a group identifier is a new authority whose key state sits above both parties" (`:1591-1595`) — the super-frame it forbids. It refuses to enumerate observers, on the correct KERI reasoning that "an enumerated observer set is a witness set by definition" (`:2000-2003`), which is the spec's own watcher-confidentiality logic carried up a layer. It defers the evaluation seal with an admissibility doctrine that is a clean statement of end-verifiability — "Commit predicates, never verdicts. A sealed verdict a stranger cannot recompute is smuggled authority" (`:1341-1344`). And §6's object-typing clause adopts the substrate's typing discipline outright: warranty = schema-typed, registry-bound ACDC; requirement elements = schema identifiers; "nothing here requires a bespoke parser" (`:906-908`). That clause is a real absorption of the prior review's KN-18, not a vocabulary gesture.

So the honest frame for this review is not "does Custos over-build." It mostly does not. It is: **are the relations it introduces well-defined enough that two strangers compute the same thing?**

### The charge's two burning questions, answered from my lens

**(a) Why a GEL and not a TEL?** The *log* does not need to be a new type, and Custos already proves it: §17 track one places GEL events in "the substrate's registry event forms under their existing ilks." That is not a concession the reviewer extracted — it is a ratified lawful option. And the substrate cooperates better than the charge assumes: `issue(vcdig, regk)` takes a **bare qb64 SAID**, not an ACDC (`keripy vdr/eventing.py:227`), so a registry tracking the issued/revoked state of arbitrary SAID-addressed governance acts is the substrate's own mechanism used as designed, not an abuse of it. What does *not* reduce is the fold: §1.5's argument is correct and is the one place the tower genuinely gains a rung. So the accurate typing is **one new fold and one new schema family, not one new log**; §1.2's three-way parallel among *carriers* ("what the KEL is to keys and the TEL is to credentials, the GEL is to law") is one column wider than the machinery warrants, while §1.5's three-way parallel among *folds* is exactly right. The charge is correct that no passage argues GEL-vs-TEL directly; my answer is that the argument is unnecessary for the fold and unavailable for the log. On interleaving: committing governance and credential evolution into one registry would be neither harmful nor beneficial *cryptographically* — both anchor by the same seal discipline — but it would collapse the one thing §1.6's colorless-base story wants, namely that a governance-blind registry consumer can fold the credential events without folding the law. Separate registries under one gAID gets that for free with no new type.

**(b) Why a warranty and not an ACDC?** It *is* an ACDC; §6 says so at keyword force. The two claimed obligations are not symmetric. "The lens is pinned" is an ACDC edge with an `s`-constraint — pure substrate, zero remainder. "The attested finding is recomputable" is the real claim, and it is **under-discharged**: recomputability requires the closed triple that §1.4 axiom 2 and §7.3 both name, and the lens (defined at `:752-755` as rule-set + engine profile + predicate set) covers at most one leg of it. So the honest verdict is: *warranty* earns a **schema name**, not a type — and the obligation set that was supposed to be its remainder is missing its load-bearing member. That is F2, and it is a stronger result than "the warranty is ontological inflation."

## Top findings

### F1: One relation, three names, three incompatible grades — the covenant seal's verification is the relation §12.1 confesses it cannot compute

- **Severity:** HIGH · **Confidence:** CONFIRMED · **dedupe_key:** `rule-satisfaction-relation-three-grades` · **Objective function:** survivability · **Layer:** governance · **Bucket:** proof-scope
- **Location:** Custos 4.1 `:1309-1319` (covenant seal), `:1608-1622` (computed congruence), `:363-386` (color comparison), `:2165-2169` (succession check), `:1288-1297` (the substrate seal-kind list). Substrate: KERI spec `spec-body.md`:511–513 (typed seal); ACDC spec `spec-body.md`:1237–1243 (rule section).
- **Finding.** Strip the names and one relation remains: **`satisfies(committed rule-set, committed clause-set)`**. The document instantiates it three times and assigns it three grades that cannot all be true.
  1. **Covenant seal (§9).** "Verification is neither byte equality nor coordinate lookup: the verifier evaluates whether the successor satisfies the committed clause… an unsatisfying successor is **convictable on the seal's own bytes**" (`:1311-1315`).
  2. **Predicate congruence (§12.1).** "rules of one Constitution that another's committed rules satisfy — is an evaluation, not a medium fact: it is computable only under a stated lens, **this document commits no canonical comparison algorithm**… predicate congruence in particular travels at **conjecture grade**" (`:1613-1621`).
  3. **Semantic color comparison (§1.6).** The comparison "asks what distinctions each fold in force can draw, since **syntactically different clause sets may compute the same distinctions**" — i.e. mutual satisfaction — and yet "color is **exactly as replayable as everything else in the domain**: a stranger holding the logs computes what this domain is able to judge" (`:376-386`).
  These are the same computation. Grade (2) is the honest one. Grades (1) and (3) assert what (2) confesses is unspecified. §15's openness clause supplies the internal test and it fires: "prose anywhere in this document… that implies design beyond the boundary stated here SHALL be treated as a defect of that prose" (`:2047-2050`).
  The second limb: the covenant seal is not a member of the seal genus at all. A digest seal and an event seal are *medium-grade* — decidable by any holder of bytes with no law in the loop, which is exactly the property §5 says the medium has ("their agreement is cryptographic rather than negotiated", `:812-816`). The covenant seal's check is a **fold** — lens-relative, law-relative, non-medium. Grouping it with two medium-grade kinds and inheriting their "convictable on the seal's own bytes" force is the category slip. Its two honest reductions both exist in the substrate: **carriage** = the typed seal, which KERI spec:511–513 describes as "a generic facility for seals" with a versioned type field (KN-14's recommendation, still un-adopted); **semantics** = the ACDC rule section, whose Ricardian clauses are "referenceable by a cryptographic digest" and bind participants by acceptance (ACDC spec:1237–1243). Digest-seal-the-clause-set plus evaluate-the-successor is the whole construct, and both halves are named machinery.
- **Why it matters.** The docket's R12 disposes of the congruence-naming question on the explicit ground that "congruence of either grade is evidence, never force… A reader who wrongly assumes symmetry cannot cash the assumption into any machine consequence. The blast radius is zero." §16 falsifies that premise. Succession — the mechanism by which this standard replaces itself — is adjudicated by exactly this relation: "a successor that cannot satisfy the committed succession clauses is convictable on its own enactment bytes" (`:2167-2169`). Concretely: edition 4.2 is ratified as an enactment; verifier V1, under its lens, computes that 4.2's clause set satisfies 4.1's covenant seal and holds 4.2 to be law; verifier V2, under a lens that reads one succession clause more strictly, computes that it does not and holds 4.1 to still be law while convicting the ratifier on its own enactment bytes. Both are conforming, because no canonical comparison algorithm is committed. Two strangers then disagree about *which bytes are law* — the one disagreement the whole standard exists to eliminate, and one the medium cannot arbitrate because §7.4 puts governance-tier conviction under frame-relative force (`:1168-1176`).
- **Recommendation** (proposed, not imposed): (i) carry the covenant seal as a **typed seal** with a committed type/version value, so the commitment has substrate carriage and the digest half is medium-grade; (ii) state once, in §9, that the *satisfaction* half is an evaluation at the grade §12.1 already assigns it, and route "convictable on the seal's own bytes" through the ordinary finding machinery (a defeated finding citing the clause), not through the seal; (iii) either commit a satisfaction procedure for the §16 succession case specifically — it is a bounded instance, over one clause set, and does not require the general comparison algorithm — or state that succession validity is judged by digest congruence over the enumerated succession clauses. Then re-open R12 with §16 in evidence.

### F2: A warranty pins its lens but not the replay triple, so "replay-falsifiable by construction" and the false-warranty conviction do not hold

- **Severity:** HIGH · **Confidence:** CONFIRMED · **dedupe_key:** `warranty-pins-lens-not-replay-triple` · **Objective function:** survivability · **Layer:** governance · **Bucket:** proof-scope
- **Location:** Custos 4.1 `:879-885` (warranty definition and its two obligations), `:752-755` (lens definition), `:244-252` (axiom 2, the closed triple), `:1033-1038` (finding inputs), `:1101-1121` (the evidence ordering), `:1684-1699` (warranted consumption), `:1738-1741` (false-warranty conviction), `:1798-1811` (grounded enactment, which *does* pin all four).
- **Finding.** A finding is identified by exactly three inputs: "the committed evidence bundle, the committed law head under which it is appraised, and the appraisal position" (`:1033-1036`), restated as axiom 2's "closed" triple (`:244-247`). A warranty's obligations are "the lens is pinned, and the attested finding is recomputable" (`:884-885`). A lens is "which rule-set, which engine profile, which predicate set" (`:752-754`) — it covers the law head and the engine, and **neither the evidence bundle nor the position**. So a warranty commits to strictly less than the object it attests.
  The consequence is not cosmetic, because §7.3's own evidence ordering makes disagreement *lawful*: "where one committed bundle is a subset of another, appraisal under the larger bundle refines and never contradicts appraisal under the smaller" (`:1101-1105`), and pending→affirmed and pending→defeated are permitted edges conditioned on evidence growth (`:1058-1066`). Therefore a verifier who recomputes and gets a different value has *not* shown the warrantor false; it has shown that the two folded different bundles, which the document says is exactly what honest verifiers do. §12.4's discipline — "A warrantor whose attested finding diverges from replay is convicted on its own signature by any verifier that recomputes" (`:1738-1741`) — has no sound premise as constructed.
  The document already knows the fix and applies it one section over: a grounded enactment "SHALL commit, within its own content: the evidence bundle it rests on, pinned as a set of digests; the law head it invokes; the position at which it speaks; and the terminal finding it claims" (`:1802-1806`). Four items. A warranty asserts the same proposition — "the fold over these inputs returns this finding" — and is required to pin one of them.
- **Why it matters.** Concretely: warrantor W emits a warranty, under a correctly pinned lens, attesting `pending(absent: registry span R)` on the question "does party P hold power Y". W in fact held the span that discharges R and suppressed it. Verifier V fetches the charter-guaranteed cone, replays, and computes `affirmed`. V has no conviction: W answers "my bundle did not contain R", which is a lawful bundle under `:1101-1105`, and nothing in W's signed bytes contradicts it. The same construction runs in the other direction (attest `affirmed` while holding a defeater). This is the load-bearing premise of the abstract's economics — "warranties amortize replay… Judgment stays cheap to verify precisely because it is expensive to fake" (`:33-40`) — and of §12.3's claim that "Replay-native verification… disciplines everything else" (`:1680-1682`). Faking is cheap when the fake cannot be pinned to inputs.
  Docket relationship: this is **not** #8 (warranty framing/grade alignment) or #16 (dispute economics); it is a structural under-determination. It is, however, the corollary of R1/A applied outside §7: if a finding is an immutable function of a closed triple, then citing a finding without its triple cites an unidentified object. Ruling R1/A makes this finding's repair obvious rather than resolving it.
- **Recommendation:** extend §6's warranty obligations from two to four, matching `:1802-1806` — pinned lens, pinned evidence-bundle digest set, pinned position, and the claimed finding with its ground — and restate §12.4's conviction rule as "diverges from replay **over the pinned triple**." That is one clause, it makes replay-falsifiability true by construction rather than by assertion, and it costs the warrantor nothing it does not already hold.

### F3: §17 mints an ilk apparatus for GEL events while §6 types every other committed object by schema identifier; track two is indistinguishable from track one at the fold boundary by §17's own equivalence-vector rule

- **Severity:** MEDIUM · **Confidence:** CONFIRMED · **dedupe_key:** `gel-event-typing-bypasses-schema-typing` · **Objective function:** survivability · **Layer:** wire · **Bucket:** —
- **Location:** Custos 4.1 `:2213-2230` (the two tracks), `:2232-2252` (the bootstrap), `:2262-2272` (genus reservation), `:2286-2306` (vectors), against `:898-908` (§6 object typing). Substrate: `keripy vdr/eventing.py:227` (`issue(vcdig, regk)` takes a bare SAID); ACDC spec:252 (schema SAID as the comparison point for type expectation); `kering.py:344-360` (the TEL ilk set).
- **Finding.** §6 commits the substrate's typing discipline for three of the document's committed object classes: warranty "typed by schema identifier", requirement elements "SHALL name their required schemas by schema identifier in the same discipline", and the closing rationale — "Object forms typed this way are consumable by the substrate's existing toolchain; nothing here requires a bespoke parser" (`:901-908`). §17 then introduces a fourth committed object class, the GEL event, and types it **not** by schema identifier but by an **ilk**, with a two-track choice, an ilk table that is itself committed data, a CESR genus reservation, and a bootstrap rule that requires a verifier to derive the track and the ilk table from inception-sealed founding law *before it may admit the first event they govern* (`:2232-2241`). That is the one typing arrangement the substrate's type-is-schema principle exists to avoid: the type of the bytes is not in the bytes.
  Meanwhile track two's differentiating behavior is provably nil at the boundary the document cares about. §17 requires "equivalence vectors (both tracks expressing one governance act; **byte-identical fold results**)" (`:2288-2290`). If two tracks are required to fold identically, no conforming Gever behaves differently on them, and §1.5's own codomain test says the fold's boundary is what is fixed. The only cell in which track two is distinguishable is a *governance-blind* consumer's parse — and that consumer is exactly the one track one is designed to serve ("this track is the colorless base: any registry-capable consumer parses the events unharmed", `:2219-2222`). A cell that no conforming fold treats differently, whose only differentiating consumer is the one the sibling cell is built for, is filled for symmetry.
  The reduction is available and uses machinery already mandated in this document: keep track one's registry forms, and type the governance act by **schema identifier on the SAID-addressed content the `iss` event carries** — the substrate places no ACDC constraint on that SAID (`eventing.py:227`), and ACDC spec:252 describes exactly this comparison ("the business logic… starts by specifying the SAID of the composed Schema… The verified SAID of the actually presented Schema is then compared against the expected SAID"). Then: no second track, no ilk table, no genus reservation, no bootstrap-before-parse, and unrecognized governance types yield the same "committed evidence of exactly that" outcome §17 wants (`:2227-2230`) — via an unrecognized schema SAID, which every ACDC toolchain already handles.
- **Why it matters.** Two implementers reading `:2213-2230` may lawfully build disjoint wire realizations of the same standard, and every cross-frame consumption path between them requires both. The document confesses cross-implementation interoperability as an open deliverable, so the divergence itself is confessed — what is *not* confessed is that the standard doubled its own wire surface for a distinction its own equivalence rule erases. The genus reservation (`:2262-2272`) is real, permanent cost paid against it, and the compact-form gate (`:2274-2284`) is gated on "the governed ilk-table seats of track two", so track two's existence also gates the receipt-carriage work.
- **Recommendation:** collapse to one track — registry-form events whose content is typed by schema identifier under §6's own clause — and retain the ilk-table machinery only if a discriminating case is exhibited in which a conforming Gever must behave differently on the two. §17's vector families already include the right experiment; run it before ratifying the second track rather than after.

### F4: "A verifier that can evaluate a rotation can evaluate a quorum of endorsements" — the threshold arithmetic transfers, the slot-satisfaction predicate does not

- **Severity:** MEDIUM · **Confidence:** CONFIRMED · **dedupe_key:** `threshold-algebra-reuse-overstated` · **Objective function:** survivability · **Layer:** acdc · **Bucket:** deployment-gap
- **Location:** Custos 4.1 `:1257-1262` and the pending-discharge clause it grounds, `:1262-1267`; also `:868-872` and `:1653-1656`. Substrate: dossier spec:353 (the shared-threshold claim), :358 and :225 (what a slot must satisfy), :371–374 (the four operators); `keripy coring.py:4431` (`Tholder`); `grep -rn "MxN\|RMxN\|MxQ\|WAVG\|NAND" keripy/src/keri/` → no matches.
- **Finding.** §8 asserts: "The threshold algebra is one algebra at both ends of the system — the same weighted-threshold satisfaction that governs key-event signing governs evidence sufficiency — so a verifier that can evaluate a rotation can evaluate a quorum of endorsements (a derivation from the substrate's design, not new law)" (`:1257-1262`). Half of that is right and is sourced: the dossier spec says its operators use "the same fractionally weighted threshold KERI uses for key-event signing thresholds (`kt`)" (dossier:353). But "the same threshold arithmetic" is not "the same evaluation." Two different relations are being identified:
  - `Tholder.satisfy` ranges over **indices of verified signatures against a key list** (`coring.py:4431` docstring, re-anchored above). Its slot predicate is *did index i verify*.
  - A dossier slot is **Endorsed** only when it "references a signed Endorsement ACDC with `disp` `"endorse"` and `act` appropriate to the operation, issued by the expected endorser and **anchored in that endorser's KEL**", and, for the qualified operators, "carries a qualification proof (`e.qp`) that validates against the schema named in the operator's `qs` field" (dossier:225, :358). Its slot predicate is a five-part cross-log check.
  The weights-sum-to-unity monoid is genuinely shared. Everything that decides *what goes into the sum* is not, and it is the part §8's pending-discharge story depends on: "an unsatisfied operator group… discharges as a pending finding whose typed requirement set enumerates exactly the unfilled slots — each element naming the slot's required schema, its expected issuer, and the citing clause" (`:1262-1267`). Computing "which slots are unfilled" *is* the slot predicate. No implementation of it exists in the reference stack — no m-ary or threshold edge-group operator appears anywhere in `keripy/src/keri/`, and ACDC spec:1112 still assigns chained-validity logic to the EGF rather than to the verifier.
- **Why it matters.** §14's own "stated evidence scale" duty makes this reviewable inside the document: "a claim SHALL name the scale of the evidence behind it, and a claim that borrows the credibility of a scale it was never exercised at is a defect of this document" (`:1948-1952`). The parenthetical "(a derivation from the substrate's design, not new law)" claims derivation grade for a capability transfer that has never been exercised and whose non-arithmetic half does not transfer. Concretely, an implementer reading `:1257-1262` reasonably concludes that wiring `Tholder` to an edge group discharges §8's composed-evidence obligation; it discharges the arithmetic and none of the slot dispositions, so the resulting pending findings enumerate wrong requirement sets — and requirement-set contents are exactly what §7.3's byte-identity `SHALL` ranges over.
- **Recommendation:** narrow the sentence to what it can carry — the threshold *satisfaction arithmetic* is shared, the slot-disposition predicate is the dossier profile's own and is not yet exercised in any implementation — and attach the fixture obligation §14 already requires. Nothing else in §8 depends on the wider claim.

### F5: The compound-result product is not closed over the evaluator's actual result space, because refusal is outside the codomain

- **Severity:** MEDIUM · **Confidence:** CONFIRMED · **dedupe_key:** `compound-finding-product-not-closed-under-refusal` · **Objective function:** survivability · **Layer:** governance · **Bucket:** proof-scope
- **Location:** Custos 4.1 `:1184-1195` (the ratified separation rule, including the compound-product `SHALL`), `:1196-1207` (refusal is not a fifth value), `:966-972` (codomain totality claim), `:1243-1267` (composed evidence), `:1650-1656` (step 3 of the transformation), `:2296-2299` (refusal-boundary vectors, at the *grammar* grain only).
- **Finding.** The evaluator's actual result space is `Finding ∪ {refusal}`, where refusal is explicitly *not* a member of the codomain: "Refusal is not a fifth finding value — it is the evaluator declining to answer an ill-posed question, recorded as an operational fact" (`:1197-1201`). §7.5 then mandates a product former: "Compound evaluator results SHALL preserve their component propositions and grounds as a **product** rather than collapse them into a fifth scalar shape" (`:1193-1195`). The product operation is defined over findings; it is not defined over the result space that actually arises. Nothing in the document says what a compound result is when one component refuses.
  Both answers are conforming on current bytes and they are observably different. (i) *Refuse the whole invocation* — defensible from "the evaluator SHALL refuse the invocation" (`:1190-1192`), and it discards the components that did compute. (ii) *Return a product with a refused coordinate* — defensible from the `SHALL` to "preserve their component propositions and grounds", and it returns an object that is not a member of `Finding^n`, i.e. not a finding, which §7.1's totality claim says the evaluator only returns.
  The document draws this line at one grain and not the other. §17's refusal-boundary vectors distinguish "an unrecognized governance ilk yielding committed evidence and a rule-governed judgment" from "the refusal fired by an underivable grammar — the pending/refusal line drawn at the **grammar** grain" (`:2296-2299`). The same line at the **composition** grain is undrawn, and §8 puts weight on exactly that grain: an *unsatisfied* slot discharges as pending (`:1262-1267`), while an *uncomposable* slot fires refusal (`:1252-1256`) — and a compound standing question routinely contains both.
- **Why it matters.** §12.2 step 3 makes B's appraisal of A's edict a compound evaluation under B's adoption covenant "and under nothing else" (`:1650-1656`). Take a three-slot covenant: slot 1 discharges affirmatively, slot 2 is unfilled (pending, absent), slot 3 names a composition B's lens leaves uncommitted (refusal). Implementer B1 returns refusal and A learns nothing about which slots were fine. Implementer B2 returns a product `(affirmed, pending{…}, ⊥)`. Both hold the same bytes, both are conforming, and §7.3's "Two evaluations of the same triple SHALL return byte-identical findings" (`:1037-1038`) is violated without either engine having erred. This is the same class of executed divergence as docket #27, at a seam the docket does not reach.
- **Recommendation:** one clause in §7.5 naming the product's behavior on a refused component — my read is that "refuse the whole invocation" is the choice consistent with §1.4 axiom 3 and with refusal being an operational fact rather than a value, but either ruling closes it. If the whole-invocation reading is taken, add the corollary that the computed components travel as the refusal's operational record, so the cure path is not lost. This is a candidate for the same batch as R11.

## Additional patterns noted (not findings)

- **"Color" carries two denotations.** §1.6 fixes color as the *instrument*: "the color is the instrument, the Constitution its reading" (`:368-372`). §12.3 and §6 use it as the *output*: evidence "is colored by that fold's judgment" (`:1701-1710`). One word, two types — the configured evaluator and the finding it returns. The participial gloss (`:886-896`) guards against the worst reading (evidence owning a color) and the appendix records the C-3 provenance ruling, so the mechanism is sound and the label overreaches; LOW, and I drop it rather than spend a finding. It is worth noting that once color-of-evidence is recognized as *the finding*, "colored evidence" is `base + warranty + lens citation` — which §6 concedes ("a warranty may travel as a component of colored evidence", `:846-847`).
- **Federation is the symmetric closure of consumption pinned to a shared object.** The relation-axis of §11 presents "one law at four distances from the frame's own keys" (`:1476-1481`), but adoption and peer sit at the *same* key distance — an envelope is explicitly "two anchors, two logs, no shared spine" (`:1597-1598`) — so the axis's ordering principle does not order its last two cells. And the ladder makes them the same shape at recourse: exit under an envelope and revocation of adoption are described as "the same shape" (`:1865-1866`). The genuine remainder is real (the shared rule-object *bounds* B's conferral, which bare consumption does not), so the two cells are distinguishable — but note the resulting non-monotonicity: entering an envelope can *reduce* what A's edicts confer in B, relative to a generous B consuming A bare. Coherent, but the "distance" metaphor points the wrong way.
- **"No object in this standard performs both verbs" (`:205-206`) is falsified by the warrantor**, which computes a finding (evaluate) and emits a warranty (enact). The steelman holds — a warrantor is a domain with an evaluator half and a constructor half, exactly as §4 says of a GARD — so the claim is true of *primitive* objects and overstated as written about "every actor."
- **The cone's minimality is asserted but only half-decided.** §6 defines the cone as "the **minimal** committed log spans" (`:854-857`) while the stated decision procedure detects only shortness ("a replay that reaches for a span the cone lacks convicts the cone as short", `:875-877`). Over-inclusion is decidable by the producer and by any replayer running *the producer's* law, but a consuming frame replays under its own law (`:1650-1652`) and cannot decide the minimality of A's cone. Given §6's confessed disclosure posture, over-inclusion is the direction with the privacy cost — handing that limb to PRV under `cone-minimality-undecided` rather than pressing it here.
- **The governed-attestation class is heterogeneous under §11's own criterion.** Warranties are TEL issuances and pass cleanly; witness receipts have no self-addressing identity — the document says so itself at `:2278-2281`, which is why the compact-form gate exists — and no coordinate of their own, so they satisfy "X's events have positions" only by inheriting the receipted event's. The class is judgeable, so I do not raise it; but it is a projection of governed-key-state and governed-credentials rather than a peer class.
- **Where the reduction test comes out *for* the document.** The edict's "never an issuer-bearing credential container… an issuer field smuggles a spine, and authority lives in the anchors" (`:898-901`) is not parsimony theatre: it is the substrate's binding-to-key-state doctrine applied correctly, and it has a specific payoff — an organ's act must be attributable to the *domain* whose gAID anchors it, and an `i` field would name the organ, creating two candidate authorities for one act. The finding codomain's four values reduce to the Kever/Tever acceptance machinery, and §7.1 says so rather than claiming novelty (`:961-965`); what does not reduce — ground as a component of the type, the complete transition enumeration with all twelve ordered pairs classified, canonical selection over a ranked defeater class — is real and earns its name. Notably, the permitted-edge poset is a genuine lattice (pending ⊑ {affirmed, defeated} ⊑ self-convicted, with self-conviction as the join of affirmed and defeated), which is a semantically apt structure and not an accident.
