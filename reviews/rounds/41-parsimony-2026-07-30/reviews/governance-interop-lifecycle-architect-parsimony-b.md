## Executive summary

In my lens Custos 4.1 is sound in posture and defective in its lifecycle seams. The posture is right, and unusually so for a governance layer: no trust registry, no super-frame, an explicit and KERI-correct rejection of the group-AID shortcut, opt-in committed adoption, a colorless base that leaves governance-blind validators unharmed, non-retroactive succession, and a travel-posture clause that forbids the standard from filing claims against another corpus before its custodians answer. That is the decentralization scorecard passing on nearly every row.

The defects are all at the boundaries where the design has to survive a *second* party, a *second* revision, or a *second* implementation. My headline is that the federation envelope cannot bind membership: the bond is constituted by two independent anchors citing one content-addressed rule-object, so either the rule-object names its parties — in which case every accession changes its SAID and invalidates every existing envelope — or it does not, in which case any stranger who anchors that SAID acquires standing-conferring force inside an incumbent's frame without the incumbent's consent. Custos has recreated the schema-SAID versioning problem (bible 06 §6) at the federation layer, and the document's own `n(n-1)/2` composition claim forces the permissive horn.

Four further findings: the §17 grammar bootstrap grounds track placement in a locus that no domain incepted under a prior edition can carry — including this standard's own ratifying authority at KEL sn 188 — so the only complete migration path is re-inception of the gAID, the one migration KERI's whole architecture exists to avoid; the closed replay triple omits the substrate/carriage *semantics revision*, and the artifact that pins it is the one companion this document never pins; §9's anchor-grade doctrine grades a delegated establishment anchor as "physics" when KERI's superseding rule B says otherwise, and §10 recommends exactly the delegated configuration where the grade is wrong; and track two's stated unrecognized-ilk consumer semantics are contradicted by every implementation that exists — keripy raises a `SerializeError` before any evidence comes into being.

Nothing here faults KERI-core for a governance-layer property, and nothing here is a re-billing of the cross-implementation-interop debt the document already confesses at §2 and §14. Where a finding could be mistaken for that debt, I say why it is not.

## Steelman

The strongest reading of Custos in my lens is this. KERI's two-layer split (bible 06 §1) leaves Layer 2 to a per-vertical, opt-in, cryptographically-bound EGF, and every deployed EGF so far — GLEIF's vLEI framework, SEDI's `rules.json`, GCD's `gfw` — realizes that binding as *prose plus a SAID*, enforced by a human or an application policy engine, never by a fold. Custos asks the obvious next question: if Layer-2 rules are already SAID-committed and already versioned, why is their *evaluation* still testimonial? Its answer is to make the Layer-2 rule-set a fold input rather than a document, so that "what does this ecosystem's governance say here" becomes a computation with the same replay property KERI gives key state. That is not an importation of PKI or federation priors; it is KERI's own log-and-fold discipline applied one tier up, and it is the correct target.

The governance-lens virtues follow directly and I want them on the record before I strike:

- **No new authority.** §12.1 rejects the group multi-signature identifier for federation on the ground that "a group identifier is a new authority whose key state sits above both parties" and that exit from it becomes "a key-state operation a counterparty can contest rather than a unilateral committed act" (`:1589-1598`). That is exactly the reasoning bible 06 §2 and 07 §5.4 would want, reached independently. A reviewer's federation-reflex has no purchase here.
- **Adoption is opt-in and committed.** "Adoption SHALL be committed" (`:1564`); "no party is subject to a regime it never committed to" (`:1715-1716`); "No threshold of consumption by others binds any frame that never committed to it" (`:1572-1573`). This is bible 06 §2's opt-in/per-vertical/cryptographically-bound test, passed on the nose.
- **Bridge, don't adopt.** Track one is a genuine colorless base: "any registry-capable consumer parses the events unharmed" (`:2220-2221`), and the cone's carriage is the ToIP dossier — a wrapper whose issuer "attests to the integrity and composition of the collection — never to the veracity of the claims within it" (`:862-864`), which is the Dossier spec's own doctrine and the FAA stance generalized.
- **Migration is the cheap kind.** §16 makes succession strictly prospective: "SHALL bind no position before it: succession is never retroactive" (`:2124-2126`). No deployed credential, registry, or holder is forced to re-issue by the 4.0→4.1 transition. That is the unilateral, per-identifier migration KERI designs for (doctrine claim 13), not a global re-rooting.
- **Honest scale.** §14's stated-evidence-scale duty ("a claim SHALL name the scale of the evidence behind it", `:1949-1950`) and §2's confession that cross-implementation agreement is an undischarged deliverable are precisely the honesty bible 06 §9 asks of an ecosystem claim. I do not bill the confessed debt as a finding, and I flag below where a sibling reviewer might be tempted to.
- **Ecosystem etiquette.** §14's travel posture — nothing here travels as "a defect report, an allocation request, a custody selection, or an extension proposal" against a substrate corpus "before an authoritative answer from that corpus's own custodians" (`:2037-2041`) — is a discipline I have not seen another governance spec commit. It makes finding F6 an internal inconsistency rather than an oversight, which is a better position to be repaired from.

Judged as a whole, the reduction test comes out mostly in the spec's favor in my lens. The GEL reduces to a registry and the document says so at `:141-146`. The warranty reduces to a schema-typed registry-bound ACDC and the document says so at `:901-904`. The federation envelope reduces to two event seals citing one SAID, and §1.7 states that composition (`:401-402`). What does *not* reduce is the fold whose transition rule lives in the log it reads, and that is the one construct I would defend against a parsimony attack. My findings are not that Custos built too much; they are that the things it built do not yet survive a counterparty, a version, or a second implementation.

## Top findings

### F1: The federation envelope cannot bind membership — the shared rule-object is content-addressed, so either accession is unilateral or every accession voids every existing bond

- **Severity:** HIGH · **Confidence:** CONFIRMED · **dedupe_key:** `federation-rule-object-membership-unbindable` · **Objective function:** survivability · **Layer:** governance · **Bucket:** deployment-gap (a genuine gap in the idea as written, not an ecosystem-maturity gap)
- **Location:** `custos-4.1.md:1575-1598` (§12.1 Federation), `:1600-1605` (Larger figures), `:1657-1661` (§12.2 step 4 Confer); re-anchored against KERI spec `spec/spec-body.md:1610-1612` @ `4df80aa` (cooperative delegation), and bible 06 §6 on schema-SAID mutation.

**Finding.** The envelope's type is stated as:

> "the matched-anchor envelope — A's GEL commits a recognition event citing a shared rule-object SAID; B's GEL commits the same SAID; the bond exists iff both anchors verify and cite the same object." (`:1577-1580`)

Nothing in that type requires either anchor to name its counterparty, or to cite the counterparty's coordinate, or to be caused by the counterparty in any way. Compare the consumption clause immediately above it, which *does* require naming: "B's recognition of any regime of A's is an event in B's own GEL, **naming what of A it recognizes** (which registries, which schemas, which law heads)" (`:1564-1567`). The silence in the federation clause is not accidental — the "Larger figures" clause makes it load-bearing:

> "A federation of n frames is n(n-1)/2 bilateral envelopes citing one shared committed instrument — **the instrument, never any collective act**, is what makes it one federation." (`:1600-1603`)

If each envelope required its two anchors to name each other, an n-th frame joining would require n−1 new anchors from the joiner *and* n−1 new anchors from the incumbents — a collective act, which this clause expressly disclaims. So the document's own composition figure forces the reading that anchoring the instrument suffices.

That reading is a governance hole. §12.1's opening sentence says federation means "Frames A and B grant each other's edicts standing-conferring force inside their own frames" and "Force crosses only through the envelope" (`:1575-1576`, `:1586-1587`); §12.2 step 4 says "Under a federation envelope, conferral follows the shared rule-object" (`:1659-1660`). So a stranger who anchors the instrument's SAID acquires standing-conferring force inside every incumbent's frame, and "neither can fabricate the other's consent" (`:1585-1586`) is false in the direction that matters: the incumbent consented to the *instrument*, not to the *counterparty*.

The obvious repair — let the rule-object enumerate its parties — collides with content-addressing. The rule-object is cited by SAID; adding a party changes its bytes, changes its SAID, and every existing anchor now cites an object no longer identical to the one the new anchor cites, so "the bond exists iff both anchors verify and cite the same object" fails for every incumbent pair. This is bible 06 §6's schema-SAID rule reappearing one layer up: "Edit content → the SAID changes → registry.json and any credential referencing the old SAID must be updated." Custos has made federation membership a property of a content-addressed object, and membership is the one property such an object cannot carry mutably.

The dilemma is exhaustive as written: **unilateral accession, or accession-voids-all-bonds.** Both are defects, and no third reading is available from the text.

Note what the substrate does here instead, because it shows the design is available: KERI's cooperative delegation binds *two named parties at two coordinates* — "The pair of bindings (delegation seal in delegator's KEL and delegator's AID in Delegatee's inception event) makes the delegation cooperative. **Both MUST participate.**" (KERI spec `spec/spec-body.md:1612` @ `4df80aa`). The delegating seal names the delegate's event; the delegate's inception names the delegator. Custos's envelope has neither peg. §12.1 rejects the group AID for good reasons, but the alternative it adopted dropped the two-way peg along with the shared spine.

**Why it matters.** Concretely: frames A and B federate under instrument `E…`. Frame C — unknown to both, possibly a frame A has an active conviction finding against — anchors a recognition event citing `E…` in its own GEL. By the stated type, A↔C and B↔C envelopes now exist; C's edicts carry standing-conferring force inside A's frame as far as `E…` commits. A never acted. A's only remedy is §13.2's "exit: the envelope binds bilaterally and dissolves unilaterally" (`:1860-1864`) — but A's exit act is against the *instrument*, so exiting C also exits B. §13.2 speaks of "the bond dying with the breach finding attached," singular; there is no per-counterparty granularity in the type. The mirror question the charge poses ("does any part of the federation machinery manufacture an authority above the parties?") gets a nuanced answer: no authority, but a *membership register that no one administers and everyone is bound by* — which is a re-centralization of a subtler kind, since the shared instrument now performs the role a trust registry would, without anyone accountable for who is in it.

**Recommendation.** Smallest repair: make the recognition event name its counterparty gAID alongside the rule-object SAID, and define the bond as existing iff each side's recognition names the other and cites the same instrument. That restores KERI's two-way peg, makes exit per-counterparty (which §13.2 already assumes), and costs the `n(n-1)/2` figure its "never any collective act" gloss — which should be restated as "no collective *event*; n−1 bilateral pairs of named recognitions." If the authors prefer to keep unilateral accession as a feature, it must be stated as one, with the incumbent's pre-refusal mechanism designed rather than left to post-hoc exit.

### F2: The §17 grammar bootstrap grounds track placement in a locus no predecessor-edition domain can carry — including this standard's own authority — leaving re-inception as the only complete migration

- **Severity:** HIGH · **Confidence:** CONFIRMED · **dedupe_key:** `gel-grammar-bootstrap-unreachable-for-predecessor-domains` · **Objective function:** survivability · **Layer:** governance · **Bucket:** deployment-gap
- **Location:** `custos-4.1.md:2232-2252` (§17 The bootstrap), `:608-632` (§4 gAID genesis knot), `:2124-2126` (§16 prospectivity), `:2447-2452` (appendix: §17 is an addition with no predecessor counterpart); corroborated by `SUCCESSION.md` (authority AID `EFolWr6gUggZS9im4f1pWSoKB9Ngd-T9YI0c8tlGIaHU`, lineage 3.1→3.2 at sn 170/171, 3.3→4.0 at 181/182, 4.0→4.1 ratified sn 187 / effective sn 188).

**Finding.** §17 makes track placement — and for track two, the initial ilk table — a precondition of admitting the first GEL event, and grounds it deliberately *outside* the GEL:

> "That derivation grounds outside the GEL, in the genesis knot Chapter 1 and the definitions section already commit: a born-governed domain's founding law is sealed at inception, and the initial track placement and initial ilk table are clauses of that founding law, readable from the inception-sealed bytes before any GEL event is consumed." (`:2234-2239`)

> "Where a verifier cannot derive the initial placement or table from the applicable committed founding-law referent … it refuses the stream — a missing rule, not missing evidence." (`:2246-2252`)

Section 17 is new in 4.1 — the appendix says so: "section 17 whole (C-6, C-7, E-2, E-3)" listed among "Additions with no predecessor counterpart" (`:2451`). So for every GARD incepted under 3.x or 4.0, the founding law sealed at inception cannot contain a track-placement clause, because no such clause existed to write. The gAID definition forecloses the retrofit: a born-governed domain's identity ranges over K0's bytes including the seal of C (`:614-622`), and C is byte-immutable thereafter; the "later-anchored founding law" path at the "confessed lesser grade" is available only to a domain that "MAY instead incept bare and anchor its founding law later" (`:626-632`) — a choice made *at* inception, not afterwards. §16's prospectivity ("SHALL bind no position before it", `:2126`) does not help: the bootstrap derivation is not a judgment at a position, it is a precondition for consuming the log from position zero, which every fold at every later position must perform.

The standard's own reference deployment is exactly this case. Per `SUCCESSION.md`, the authority AID's Custos lineage runs from at least sn 170 through 4.1's effectuation at sn 188. Its founding law — whatever it seals — predates §17 by the entire lineage. Under §17 as written, a fresh verifier applying 4.1 to that domain cannot derive the initial track placement from the applicable founding-law referent, and the prescribed response is refusal of the stream.

**Why it matters.** The only construction that fully satisfies §17's bootstrap for a born-governed predecessor-edition domain is a new inception with a founding law that carries the clause — i.e. a new gAID, a new identity, and the abandonment of the lineage the whole architecture exists to preserve. That is the maximal migration cost, and it is the exact cost KERI's transferable-AID/pre-rotation design is built to avoid (doctrine claim 13; bible 06 §6: protocol-level migration is "one rotation per identifier, made unilaterally… not a global re-rooting"). A governance standard whose own version bump is only fully satisfiable by re-inception has inverted its substrate's migration economics.

I want to be precise about what does and does not survive the reading-rules objection. The bootstrap paragraph carries no all-capital BCP 14 keyword, so under §3 rule 1 it "binds nothing on its own." That does not dissolve the finding, it forks it: if the paragraph binds, predecessor-edition streams must be refused; if it does not bind, then track placement has *no* committed ground at all, and the derivational account §17 offers for how a verifier learns which grammar it is reading is vacuous. Either horn is a migration defect, and the second horn sits adjacent to round A's standing `gel-span-membership-underivable` (which grants that placement is committed and attacks membership). I am attacking the placement premise round A granted.

**Recommendation.** Add a transitional clause: for a domain whose founding law predates this edition, the initial track placement is derivable from the *ratification enactment* of this edition, at the confessed lesser grade already defined for adopted domains — with the grade difference stated, exactly as §4 states it for bare inception. One sentence plus a boundary vector ("a predecessor-edition domain's stream whose placement is grounded in the effectuating enactment folds, at the lesser grade"). Alternatively, state explicitly that §17 binds only domains incepted at or after this edition's effectuation, and say what a verifier does with the others.

### F3: The replay triple is closed over data but open over semantics-version — and the artifact that pins the substrate revisions is the one companion this document never pins

- **Severity:** HIGH · **Confidence:** CONFIRMED · **dedupe_key:** `substrate-revision-uncommitted-fold-input` · **Objective function:** survivability · **Layer:** governance · **Bucket:** deployment-gap
- **Location:** `custos-4.1.md:1033-1038` (§7.3 Inputs, ruled), `:244-247` (§1.4 axiom 2), `:568-577` (§3 substrate of record), `:1933-1939` (§14 Naming — the register that *is* pinned), `:998-1005` (§7.2 window-open), `:818-824` (§5 medium, superseding scoping), `:1243-1257` (§8 composed evidence), `:1642-1649` (§12.2 step 2, ruled MUST); re-anchored against KERI spec `spec-body.md:1802-1825` @ `4df80aa` (superseding rules A0–A2, B1–B3), Dossier spec `spec/dossier-spec-body.md:225, 371-374` @ `3900e62` (threshold-operator semantics).

**Finding.** §7.3 states, at ruled force:

> "A finding is a function of exactly three inputs: the committed evidence bundle, the committed law head under which it is appraised, and the appraisal position. No other input — wall clocks, local state, operator discretion, **ambient configuration** — may influence a finding. **Two evaluations of the same triple SHALL return byte-identical findings.**" (`:1033-1038`)

But the fold's decision procedure is not a function of those three alone. It dereferences external specifications whose semantics decide findings:

1. **Superseding-recovery semantics decide `self-convicted` and `window-open`.** §7.1: "At the key tier, whether a pair bears is decided by the substrate's own superseding-recovery rules" (`:957-959`). §7.2: window-open "is cured when the substrate's superseding rules no longer admit a superseding event at the position … for a delegated log the window closes only when no lawful superseding rotation remains admissible under **the substrate's delegated-recovery rules** … (the substrate's own recovery calculus is the decision procedure)" (`:998-1005`). Those rules are a live, enumerated, revision-bearing part of the KERI spec — A0/A1/A2 and B1/B2/B3 at `spec-body.md:1806-1819` @ `4df80aa`. A change to rule B changes whether a given pending finding has cured. Same bundle, same law head, same position; different finding.
2. **Dossier threshold-operator semantics decide `pending` requirement sets.** §8 makes composed-evidence sufficiency "expressed in the ACDC edge grammar as profiled by the dossier specification's threshold operators" and makes an unsatisfied group "discharge as a pending finding whose typed requirement set enumerates exactly the unfilled slots" (`:1247-1267`); §12.2 step 2 carries a MUST over dossier carriage (`:1642-1649`). The Dossier spec's operator semantics are concrete and version-bearing — `MxQ` declares "a uniform member weight in its own `w` field," requires a `qs` schema SAID and an `e.qp` qualification-proof edge, and a slot is Endorsed only under the conditions at `dossier-spec-body.md:225` @ `3900e62`. Change any of that and the unfilled-slot enumeration changes, hence the finding's bytes change.

Where is the revision pinned? §3 says: "The revision of record for each specification is pinned in **the engagement companion** under this document's own pin discipline" (`:573-576`). I grepped the full 2471 lines: `companion` appears at `:1936`, `:2048`, and `:574`. The only companion whose digest the ratification enactment SHALL pin is the **notation register** (`:1935-1938`). The engagement companion — the artifact holding the substrate revisions the fold's semantics dereference to — is named once and pinned nowhere in the ratified bytes. And the Dossier specification is not even inside §3's substrate of record, which is "KERI, with ACDC as its credential layer and CESR as its encoding layer" (`:568-572`); it enters at `:860` as an external corpus "cited, consumed, not restated," with no revision discipline attached at all.

The document half-knows this. §4's `Lens` definition treats the semantics coordinate as first-class — "which rule-set, **which engine profile**, which predicate set a finding or warranty was computed under, cited by identifier" (`:752-755`) — and §1.6 runs colour comparison over *four* coordinates including "the pinned engine profile of the comparing lens" (`:378-381`). A warranty therefore travels with a semantics coordinate that the fold's own closed triple excludes.

**Why it matters, and why this is not the confessed interop debt.** §2 confesses that agreement between *independent implementations* is undischarged, and §14 requires every claim to name its evidence scale. Neither confession reaches this finding, because the divergence here is *intra*-implementation and *over time*: one conforming engine, one pinned checkout, replaying the same committed triple two years later against the then-current KERI or Dossier revision, returns a different finding — and the ruled SHALL says it must not. That is a versioning defect in the design, not an ecosystem-maturity gap. It also defeats the discipline the whole warranty economy rests on: §12.4's false-warranty conviction "by any verifier that recomputes" (`:1738-1742`) convicts an honest warrantor whose only sin is that the substrate's recovery calculus moved under it.

Round A raised and the runner refuted a neighbouring claim keyed `fold-inputs-closed-triple-vs-pinned-lens`, on the ground that the opening-side spans carry no BCP 14 keyword and so bind nothing. That refutation does not touch this finding: I rest on the *ruled* SHALL at `:1033-1038` plus the *absence* of any pin, not on an unruled span opening the arity. If anything, the refutation sharpens the problem — if the lens's engine profile binds nothing, then nothing in the ratified bytes fixes the semantics at all.

**Recommendation.** Either (a) make the substrate/carriage revision a component of the law head — the Constitution commits the specification digests it folds under, and an amendment to those digests is an ordinary enactment judged under §11's positional recursion; or (b) pin the engagement companion's digest in the ratification enactment beside the notation register's, and add the dossier specification to §3's substrate of record so it inherits the pin discipline. (a) is the more Custos-native answer: it makes semantics-version a governed object under §11's own criterion, which the criterion already admits.

### F4: §9's anchor-grade doctrine calls a delegated establishment anchor "physics" — KERI's superseding rule B says it is not, and §10 recommends exactly that configuration

- **Severity:** HIGH · **Confidence:** CONFIRMED · **dedupe_key:** `establishment-anchor-grade-false-for-delegated-logs` · **Objective function:** survivability · **Layer:** keri-core (the misstated substrate rule) with governance consequence · **Bucket:** deployment-gap
- **Location:** `custos-4.1.md:1347-1363` (§9 Anchor grade, including the SHALL at `:1358-1360`), `:1410-1420` (§10 delegated organs SHOULD), `:998-1005` (§7.2 window-open, the document's own contrary concession), `:1985-1994` (§14 charter over "every stratum of its delegation tree"); re-anchored against KERI spec `spec/spec-body.md:1806-1825` @ `4df80aa`.

**Finding.** §9 grades anchors:

> "A seal carried in an interaction event is erasable in principle: superseding recovery at the key tier can displace the event that carried it … A seal carried in an establishment event **is physics**: displacing it forks the establishment lineage itself, and the fork is duplicity evident to any watcher holding both branches. Neither grade is forbidden — the difference is computable, **and a consuming frame weighs it**." (`:1350-1356`)

and then rules on it: "Designated act classes — charter, revocation of a seat, enactment amending law, and the succession acts of section 16 — **SHALL anchor in establishment events**" (`:1358-1360`).

The "physics" claim holds only for non-delegated logs. KERI's superseding rules, re-anchored live:

> "A1. A non-delegated rotation may not supersede another rotation." (`spec-body.md:1808` @ `4df80aa`)

> "B. A delegated rotation may supersede the latest-seen delegated rotation at the same `sn` under either of the following conditions: B1 … B2 … B3 …" (`spec-body.md:1813-1819`)

So for a *delegated* identifier, an establishment event at a coordinate is displaceable by another establishment event at the same coordinate, and the displacement is lawful reconciliation, not a duplicity-evident fork. §5 of Custos already concedes the general form of this: "its superseding-recovery rules lawfully admit a second event at a coordinate as reconciliation — a rotation recovering a compromised log is repair, cited, not duplicity" (`:819-822`). §7.2 concedes the delegated case specifically and even notes the window "stay[s] open longer" for delegated logs (`:1001-1005`). §9 nonetheless grades the delegated establishment anchor identically to the sovereign one.

This is not an edge case the design avoids; it is the configuration the design recommends. §10: "Seated organs SHOULD be delegated identifiers of the gAID: delegation dual-anchors the seat's key events … places custodial recovery of a compromised organ inside the substrate's own delegated-recovery rules" (`:1410-1415`). §14's availability charter is built over "every stratum of its delegation tree" (`:1988`), and §4's charter definition speaks of propagation "down a GARD's delegation strata" (`:796-799`) — a stratified architecture in which sub-authorities are delegated by construction. §9's own rationale for the SHALL is that delegated recovery is *desirable*; §9's grade doctrine assumes it is *absent*.

**Why it matters.** §9 explicitly tells a consuming frame to weigh the grade ("the difference is computable, and a consuming frame weighs it"). A consuming frame weighing a seat-revocation or a law-amending enactment anchored in a delegated organ's — or a delegated GARD's — establishment event will grade it "physics" and treat it as undisplaceable, when under rule B the delegator can lawfully supersede it via B1 (a later delegating event) or B3 (a superseding rotation of the delegating interaction). The stranger's computed weighing is wrong, and it is wrong in the direction that over-trusts. Because §9's closing sentence is "this standard does not let the two wear one name" (`:1362-1363`), a grade table that misgrades a whole class is a defect in the section's own terms.

**Recommendation.** Split the establishment grade in two: sovereign-establishment (rule A1 — physics) and delegated-establishment (rule B — displaceable by the delegator under stated conditions, with the residual window typed by §7.2's window-open species, which already exists for exactly this). State that the SHALL at `:1358-1360` is discharged at the sovereign grade only where the anchoring identifier is non-delegated, and that a delegated anchor of a designated act carries a stated residual window. This is three sentences and it makes the grade table complete rather than optimistic.

### F5: Track two's unrecognized-ilk consumer semantics are false against the substrate — keripy fails serialization before any "committed evidence" exists

- **Severity:** HIGH · **Confidence:** CONFIRMED · **dedupe_key:** `track-two-unrecognized-ilk-yields-no-evidence` · **Objective function:** survivability · **Layer:** wire · **Bucket:** deployment-gap
- **Location:** `custos-4.1.md:2222-2230` (§17 track two), `:2296-2300` (§17 refusal-boundary vectors), `:906-908` (§6 "nothing here requires a bespoke parser"); re-anchored against keripy `src/keri/core/serdering.py:1691-1700` @ `5ba9b473`, `src/keri/vdr/eventing.py:1560-1636` @ `5ba9b473`, CESR spec `spec/spec-body.md:618` @ `d35125e`, ACDC spec `spec/spec-body.md:1918` @ `651df33e`.

**Finding.** §17 asserts a graceful-degradation semantics for unknown governance ilks:

> "A consumer encountering an unrecognized governance ilk **holds committed evidence of exactly that**; recognition is a computed judgment under the consumer's own law." (`:2228-2230`)

and owes a vector family exercising it: "refusal-boundary vectors (an unrecognized governance ilk **yielding committed evidence and a rule-governed judgment**, against the refusal fired by an underivable grammar — the pending/refusal line drawn at the grammar grain)" (`:2296-2300`).

Verified against the reference implementation, an unrecognized ilk does not yield evidence; it yields a serialization failure before any object exists to hold:

```
ilk = sad.get('t')
if ilk not in ilks:
    raise SerializeError(f"Missing or unsupported message type field "
                         f"'t', {ilk=} for protocol={proto} ...")
```

(`src/keri/core/serdering.py:1691-1695` @ `5ba9b473`; the same gate on the serialize side at `:985-987`.)

### F6: A CESR governance genus reserved by unilateral enactment collides with the one code table CESR requires every protocol to share identically

- **Severity:** MEDIUM · **Confidence:** CONFIRMED · **dedupe_key:** `unilateral-cesr-genus-reservation-collides` · **Objective function:** survivability · **Layer:** wire · **Bucket:** deployment-gap
- **Location:** `custos-4.1.md:2262-2272` (§17 Genus), `:2300-2302` (recognition vectors), `:2034-2041` (§14 Travel posture); re-anchored against CESR spec `spec/spec-body.md:611`, `:615`, `:618` @ `d35125e`.

**Finding.** §17 makes genus reservation a domestic act:

> "The encoding substrate's genus namespace admits reservation, and a reservation of a governance genus is itself an enactment: committed in the GEL of the domain that reserves it, carrying the reserved coordinates and their intended grammar, judged and superseded under that domain's law. Recognition of the reservation by the substrate's stewards is a distinct, later, bilateral event — a federation-shaped recognition, **not a precondition**." (`:2262-2270`)

CESR's genus/version table is the single namespace it declares non-negotiable:

> "The only table that all protocols **MUST share (i.e., has identical values)** is the protocol genus and version table (protocol table for short)." (CESR spec `spec-body.md:611` @ `d35125e`)

The genus is three Base64 characters (`-_GGGVVV`, `spec-body.md:615`), and a parser switching tables on that code frames every subsequent primitive from the table the code names. Two domains that each enact a reservation of the same three characters, each lawfully under its own law, produce two incompatible table sets claiming one code point — and because CESR is self-framing, the resulting mis-framing is silent, not an error. That is precisely the hazard doctrine claim 13 flags: crypto agility and genus extension work *only* because a new code occupies a genuinely unused slot, and the sizing/table entry is exactly right.

I record two mitigations honestly. First, the paragraph carries no all-capital BCP 14 keyword, so under §3 rule 1 it binds nothing — it is doctrine the document practices rather than a rule it imposes, which is why I grade this MEDIUM rather than higher. Second, the document is candid that its own reservation is "enacted, unrecognized, and honest about the difference" (`:2270-2272`), and it registers the reservation as Exhibit-class evidence for §15's confessed frontier. Honesty about the record is not, however, a rule about the wire: nothing in the document says that bytes MUST NOT be emitted on an interoperable stream under an unrecognized genus, and §17's recognition vectors contemplate the reservation being "consumed as committed evidence" by other parties (`:2300-2302`).

The finding is sharpened by the document's own ethic. §14's travel posture forbids Custos from travelling as "an allocation request" against a substrate corpus "before an authoritative answer from that corpus's own custodians" (`:2037-2041`) — an admirable discipline that §17 then partly undoes by treating unilateral occupation of the allocation as lawful and steward recognition as optional-and-later. The document is scrupulous about not *asking* and permissive about *taking*.

**Why it matters.** A genus reservation that reaches the wire before recognition creates an archive hazard with no expiry: streams emitted under `-_XXX…` remain in logs forever, and if the stewards later allocate those three characters to another protocol, every archived Custos stream mis-frames under a conforming parser — silently. Because governance streams are precisely the artifacts a GARD promises will replay "forever" (`:126-128`), this is the one class of stream that cannot tolerate a namespace reassignment.

**Recommendation.** Add one ruled span: a domain SHALL NOT emit CESR streams under an unrecognized governance genus on any interoperable surface; until recognition, governance events travel in the recognized KERI/ACDC genus (which track one already does). Keep the enactment-as-reservation doctrine — it is a good record-keeping act — but sever it from wire emission. This also makes §14's travel posture and §17's genus doctrine say the same thing.

## Additional patterns noted

**The availability charter's scope at the consumption seam.** §14 obliges a federated GARD to keep available "the key state and evidence its judgments depend on … **at every stratum of its delegation tree**" (`:1986-1989`), while §6 requires the verification cone to be "closed transitively … every log span the finding's replay reads, across every citation" (`:872-876`). Under bare consumption, B's finding over A's edict reads A's spans, and "A cannot prevent consumption and need not know of it" (`:1571-1572`) — A is in nobody's delegation tree. Read narrowly, the charter does not reach the foreign spans B's own cone must contain, and a stranger cannot compute whose breach a short cone is. Read broadly ("evidence its judgments depend on" covers foreign spans; the delegation-tree phrase governs propagation, not scope), B owes them, and B can discharge the duty by mirroring — KERI events are self-authenticating and receipts travel as attachments, so a mirror is sufficient. The broad reading is the substrate-native one and is almost certainly intended (it is the IPEX/dossier convention: the discloser supplies the whole graph). I therefore do not raise this to a finding, but the two readings differ across a *frame* boundary, not within one implementer, so it is mine rather than SPC's, and one clarifying clause ("the charter's evidence scope includes every span the frame's own findings read, including spans consumed from other frames") would close it. `dedupe_key` if a sibling lens sees it independently: `charter-scope-excludes-consumed-foreign-spans`.

**Where the absorption of the prior review is real.** KN-16 (endorsement lineage for the warranty) and KN-18 (ACDC schema machinery for committed-object typing) are genuinely absorbed, not nominally: §6's object-typing clause is a SHALL that types the warranty as "a schema-typed, registry-bound attestation in the substrate's credential discipline — typed by schema identifier, revocable through its registry, its lens cited by edge" (`:901-904`), which is a real ACDC, with a real registry, and an edge doing real work. KN-17 (IPEX/dossier for cone exchange) is absorbed as carriage (`:858-871`) but the *revision* of the corpus it absorbs is unpinned — see F3. KN-15 (delegation for seating) is absorbed at SHOULD force (`:1410`), and F4 shows the absorption is incomplete in the direction that matters: the document adopted delegation's recovery benefit without adopting its consequence for anchor durability. KN-19 (CESR carriage posture) is the weakest absorption — see F5 and F6.

**On the two worked examples in the charge, from my lens only.** (a) GEL-vs-TEL: in my mandate the question is not whether a third log type exists but whether a foreign verifier can tell a governance registry from a credential registry, and the answer under track one is no — which is round A's standing top repair and I concur with it from the interop side without re-deriving it. (b) Warranty-vs-ACDC: the two obligations do real work in my lens, because "the lens is pinned" is the only place in the document where a semantics coordinate travels with an artifact. F3's complaint is that the *fold* does not enjoy the property the *warranty* does. That is an argument for the warranty earning its name, not against it.
