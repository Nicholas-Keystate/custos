## Executive summary

The reduction test does not sink this document. Most of its vocabulary is exactly what §1.7 claims it is — named compositions of primitives that already exist — and in the two places I expected to find inflation (the warranty, the finding codomain) the reduction has already been taken *in the text*, not merely gestured at. KN-01 through KN-13 and KN-16, KN-18, KN-19, KN-20 are all absorbed for real, and I say so below rather than re-deriving them.

Five constructs fail the test, and they cluster: **the document is thriftier with its concepts than with its wire.** The covenant seal mints a fourth seal semantics where the substrate ships a generic extension point (`-W` typed seal) and where the residue it claims — successor-satisfaction — is a fold, not a seal check (F1). The GEL's second event-form track and its governance genus reservation add a doubled conformance surface, a bootstrap-and-refusal apparatus, and a self-allocation in a shared namespace, for a construction with no exhibited user and a substrate clause that already grants what it is for at zero cost (F2, F5). Colored evidence is a fourth crossing object with no emitter, no consumer, no typing clause, no vector, and one component ("committed view echo") used once and defined nowhere (F3). And the one place where the substrate mechanism *is* structurally load-bearing — delegation for seated organs — is admitted at SHOULD, leaving lawful a non-delegated construction under which the document's own worked illustration (a checkable term limit) is not decidable at all (F4).

The single biggest finding is F2: three of §17's five owed vector families exist only to support a track nobody has exercised, on a standard that confesses it owes cross-implementation agreement and has zero conformance vectors today.

## Steelman

### Steelman of the proposal

The strongest reading of Custos is not "a governance framework." It is this: KERI carried one construction — *committed log, pure fold, ground-carrying computed state* — to two tiers and stopped, and every consumer above that line reinvents the third tier badly. Custos's claim is that the third tier is the same construction again, with exactly one column changed: the fold's transition rule is committed data rather than a protocol constant (§1.5, `:295-309`). That is a genuinely small claim, and the document knows it is small: §1.7 imposes a seven-primitive budget on itself and treats an eighth primitive as a defect *in its own terms*. A reviewer who reads Custos as an ontology land-grab has misread it; the ontology is deliberately thin and the document polices its own thinness harder than I would.

It also does the reduction work an SKP reviewer normally has to do for the author. The edict is expressly *not* a credential container — "a bare self-addressed data item, never an issuer-bearing credential container" (`:898-901`) — which is the anti-inflation move, not the inflationary one. The warranty is expressly an ACDC: "schema-typed, registry-bound attestation in the substrate's credential discipline — typed by schema identifier, revocable through its registry, its lens cited by edge… nothing here requires a bespoke parser" (`:901-908`). The cone's carriage is expressly the ToIP dossier (`:859-864`). The evaluation seal is *named and deferred with its admissibility doctrine attached* (`:1329-1344`) precisely so deferral cannot drift into silent adoption — that is a discipline I usually have to ask for.

### Steelman of do-nothing

Do-nothing here means: KERI + ACDC + the dossier profile + per-ecosystem convention. That status quo is stronger than Custos's abstract admits, and it covers more than the document credits:

- A governance registry is a TEL. The ACDC specification's own extensibility clause authorizes it in as many words: "Any number of transaction event types can be constructed for different applications that may be securely attributed without complicating KEL semantics. The seals need no semantics beyond their secure attributability to the AID of the KEL controller" (ACDC `spec/spec-body.md:1918` @`651df33e`).
- Composed evidence sufficiency is the dossier spec's threshold operators, which Custos correctly cites rather than restates (`:1243-1257`).
- Endorsement of a computed view is the substrate's own Endorsement (KERI `spec-body.md:1274` @`4df80aa`).
- Seating is delegation (`dip`/`drt`), whose approval is a seal in the delegator's KEL (KERI `spec-body.md:1282`).
- A commitment to a domain-defined semantics with its own version is the typed seal, `-W`/`--W` `TypedDigestSealCouples [t, d]` (KERI `spec-body.md:421-422`, prose at `:511-513`: "This provides a generic facility for seals").

What do-nothing genuinely cannot supply — and I could not defeat the document here — is the **Gever**: a fold whose transition rule is itself committed, amendable data resolved positionally, with a four-valued ground-carrying codomain, a canonical defeat selection, and a refusal discipline. Nothing in KERI, ACDC, CESR or the dossier profile occupies that role. `affirmed / defeated(citation) / pending(typed-requirement) / self-convicted(proof)` with the ground *in the type* (`:941-959`) is not what a keripy verifier computes today, and the requirement that two verifiers emit the same defeated finding "down to the byte" via a lexicographic minimum over (defeater-class rank, citation identifier, subcode) (`:1123-1136`) is real, new, and not reducible to a schema. That is the irreducible remainder of the whole standard, and it is a fold, not a log and not a wire format.

So my verdict on the charge's framing: **the fold earns its name; the log does not; and the wire surface the log drags behind it is where the parsimony debt actually sits.**

## Top findings

### F1: The covenant seal has no remainder — it is a digest commitment plus a fold, and its stated composition does not hold

- **Severity:** HIGH · **Confidence:** CONFIRMED · **dedupe_key:** `covenant-seal-reduces-to-digest-seal-plus-fold` · **Objective function:** survivability · **Layer:** keri-core · **Bucket:** non-goal (burden to add a fourth seal semantics unmet)
- **Location:** proposal §1.2 (`:170-185`), §4 seal kinds (`:765-777`), §9 (`:1284-1327`); KERI `spec/spec-body.md:511-513` and `:421-422` (typed seal + `-W`/`--W` count codes, re-anchored @`4df80aa`); KERI `spec-body.md:1282` (sealing); prior art KN-14.
- **Finding.** §9 now enumerates the substrate's seal table correctly — "digest seals, Merkle-root seals, event and source couples, a latest-establishment seal, a generic typed seal" (`:1288-1292`) — which absorbs KN-06. It then declines the reuse KN-14 named: "The third kind is this standard's extension" (`:1296-1297`). The absorption is nominal. Two things follow.

  First, the covenant seal has no irreducible remainder. Its subject matter is the covenant set, whose bytes exist at sealing time; a digest seal (or a `-W` typed seal carrying a `covenant` type/version) commits them exactly. What §9 calls the covenant seal's distinguishing property — "the verifier evaluates whether the successor satisfies the committed clause" (`:1311-1313`) — is not a seal verification at all. It is a Gever invocation returning a finding with its ground. So the construct decomposes into two things the document already has: *digest seal over the clause set* + *fold over the successor under committed law*. The convictability §1.2 attributes to the seal ("an unsatisfying successor is convictable on the seal's own bytes", `:177-179`) comes from the clauses being committed, which the digest already achieves.

  Second, the stated composition fails §1.7's gate. §1.2 introduces the covenant seal as a *seal* — "a commitment planted in a log", one of the five nouns. But a seal in the substrate's sense is verified by a computation every verifier performs identically (KERI `spec-body.md:393-405`: a digest commitment recomputed against presented bytes); the covenant seal is verified by a law-relative evaluation. The document itself draws that line as its central discipline: the medium "carries evidence and convicts duplicity; it never judges. Judgment begins only inside a frame, under committed law" (`:836-838`).

- **Why it matters.** This is the *same* defect this edition repaired for the word "duplicity." §7.4 now says: "key-tier duplicity convicts in the medium, for every verifier, under no frame's law. Registry-tier and governance-tier duplicity are law-relative… Extending the substrate's word up the tiers does not extend the substrate's force" (`:1168-1176`). The identical repair was not applied to "seal." Concrete failure: frame A plants a covenant seal binding corpus C; frame B holds the same bytes but never committed A's clause set. §9 requires "a conviction sourced from a seal names the seal kind it convicts under — a digest mismatch, a coordinate mismatch, and a clause violation are three different refusals" (`:1322-1327`). Two of those three are medium-grade and computable by B; the third is not, yet all three travel under one word, so a "seal conviction" arriving at B carries no readable force grade. A verifier that treats a clause-violation conviction as it treats a digest mismatch has imported another frame's law as if it were the medium's.

  There is also a cost the document's own §14 makes sharp. A genuinely new seal kind eventually needs carriage — a count code. §14's travel posture says "Nothing in this document SHALL travel as a defect report, an allocation request, a custody selection, or an extension proposal against any substrate or ecosystem corpus before an authoritative answer from that corpus's own custodians" (`:2037-2041`). The typed seal is precisely how not to need one; §15 leaves carriage open (`:2069-2072`), so the allocation is deferred rather than avoided.

- **Recommendation** (proposed, not imposed). Retire the covenant seal as a *kind*. State the composition explicitly: a covenant commitment is a digest seal — carried as a typed seal (`-W`, `t` = a covenant type/version) where kind-naming is wanted — over the covenant-set bytes; successor satisfaction is a finding of the ordinary machinery of §7, with the ordinary law-relative force of §7.4. The seal ladder then has two kinds and one deferred kind, all substrate-shaped, and §9's three-refusal discipline collapses to two medium-grade refusals plus one finding — which is what it actually is.

### F2: Track two and the governance genus are unexercised surface; three of §17's five owed vector families exist only to carry them

- **Severity:** HIGH · **Confidence:** CONFIRMED · **dedupe_key:** `gel-two-track-grammar-unearned` · **Objective function:** survivability · **Layer:** wire · **Bucket:** non-goal (surface ahead of demonstrated demand)
- **Location:** proposal §17 "The two tracks" (`:2213-2230`), "The bootstrap" (`:2232-2260`), "Genus" (`:2262-2272`), "Vectors" (`:2286-2306`); §2 non-goals (`:491-495`); §16 (`:2138-2146`); ACDC `spec/spec-body.md:1918` @`651df33e`; CESR `spec/spec-body.md:607-615`, `:801-808` @`d35125e`; keripy `src/keri/vdr/eventing.py:111-123, 252-259` @`5ba9b473`.
- **Finding.** §17 makes two event-form tracks lawful and the choice committed law. Track one is registry-form reuse and is "the colorless base" (`:2216-2221`). Track two mints governance ilks and carries a committed ilk table (`:2222-2230`). Track two has no exhibited user anywhere in the document, no named domain, and no fixture; the document's evidence scale is confessedly "one implementation at one pinned checkout, with purpose-built identifiers" (`:1952-1957`).

  Track one is buildable today, which I verified rather than assumed: registry event bodies already carry a self-addressing `d` in their own field (keripy `vdr/eventing.py:113` for `vcp`, `:254` for `iss`), so §17's "Every GEL event SHALL carry a self-addressing identifier in its own field" (`:2193-2198`) is satisfied by the substrate's existing forms. And the substrate's own extensibility clause already grants what track two is *for*: "Any number of transaction event types can be constructed for different applications that may be securely attributed without complicating KEL semantics" (ACDC `:1918`). Minting a governance transaction event type inside the existing KERI/ACDC genus costs nothing.

  What the genus reservation buys over that is a *dedicated code-table set* — the CESR genus is defined as the mechanism by which "each protocol may have its own dedicated set of code tables" (CESR `:611`), addressed `-_GGGVVV` (CESR `:615`). Custos reserves one for a carriage it has not designed (§15 leaves "the carriage encoding of this document's object classes" open, `:2069-2072`), for a track no domain exercises, and confesses the reservation is "enacted, unrecognized" (`:2269-2272`). Framing recognition as "a distinct, later, bilateral event — a federation-shaped recognition, not a precondition" (`:2266-2269`) is elegant, but it is a framing, not a need: it self-allocates a coordinate in a namespace shared with every CESR protocol, which is the shape §14's travel posture exists to avoid.

- **Why it matters.** Count the carrying cost off the document's own vector list (`:2286-2306`). Of the five families owed, **equivalence vectors** ("both tracks expressing one governance act; byte-identical fold results"), **refusal-boundary vectors** (the unrecognized-ilk vs underivable-grammar line), and **recognition vectors** (genus reservation enacted vs externally recognized) exist *only* because track two exists; so do three of the six boundary must-reject items (both-track placement without placement law; an underivable initial track or ilk table; a grammar migration not admitted under the prior grammar). The entire "bootstrap" paragraph (`:2232-2260`) — with its second refusal path distinct from the pending species — exists only to derive a track choice and an ilk table that a single-track standard would not have. Concretely: an implementer must build two fold front-ends and prove them byte-equal, on a standard whose §2 confesses cross-implementation interoperability is undischarged (`:491-495`), whose §16 lists it as an open debt (`:2138-2146`), and which today has no conformance vectors at all (ruling docket, #15). Track two also forfeits the colorless base that §12.3 makes load-bearing ("a validator that knows nothing of GARDs parses and verifies the same bytes unharmed", `:1710-1713`) — §17 concedes only track one carries it.

- **Recommendation.** Apply the document's own deferral discipline, the one it already uses well twice — the evaluation seal (`:1329-1344`) and the compact receipt form's ordered gates (`:2274-2284`). Make track one the definition of the GEL's grammar; name track two and the genus reservation, state their admissibility rule, and defer both until a domain exhibits a governance act that track one cannot express. If governance ilks are later demanded, mint them as transaction event types inside the existing KERI/ACDC genus per ACDC `:1918` and reserve a genus only if CESR-native carriage is actually designed. This deletes three vector families and the bootstrap refusal path from the standard's undischarged debt without deleting any capability anyone has exercised.

### F3: Colored evidence is a crossing object with no emitter, no consumer, no typing clause, no composition, and an undefined component

- **Severity:** MEDIUM · **Confidence:** CONFIRMED · **dedupe_key:** `colored-evidence-object-no-present-user` · **Objective function:** survivability · **Layer:** governance · **Bucket:** non-goal (fourth cell of a four-object list, filled for completeness)
- **Location:** proposal §2 (`:449-452`), §6 (`:886-896`), §6 object typing (`:898-908`); §12.2 step 4 (`:1657-1661`); §12.3 (`:1701-1716`); §13.2 (`:1866-1870`); §1.7 (`:396-404`).
- **Finding.** §6 says "Four object forms carry every crossing this document itself makes" (`:844-846`). Colored evidence carries none. The string "colored evidence" occurs exactly twice in 2471 lines: in §2's list of what the document specifies and in its own §6 bullet. No normative span requires producing it, consuming it, or verifying it. §12.2 step 4 and §12.3 carry the *doctrine* — color is computed at each consumer, never asserted — entirely without the object; §13.2's commons rung travels warranties, not colored-evidence objects.

  Three further gaps confirm it is unbuilt rather than merely unused. (a) §6's object-typing clause types the edict, the warranty, and requirement-set elements; it types neither the cone (which gets the dossier) nor colored evidence — so of the four crossing objects, this is the only one with no carriage and no typing at all. (b) One of its three named components, "a committed view echo" (`:892`), appears exactly once in the document and is defined nowhere. (c) §1.7's gate requires each construct to be "introduced as a named composition of those seven, and the introducing section states the composition in its own prose" (`:396-398`). §1.7 pre-states compositions for the warranty, the organ, the tier, consumption, federation, and engine strata; §17 runs the gate explicitly for the GEL event (`:2176-2181`). No section states a composition for colored evidence — "evidence" is not one of the five nouns.

- **Why it matters.** By §1.7's own terms — the section that is "normative for this document itself" — a construct with no stated composition is a defect *in the spec's terms*, not merely in mine. The practical failure is an implementer's: told the crossing space is carried by four object forms, they must implement a form whose components are undefined, whose typing is unstated, and for which no fixture, vector, or consuming clause exists. Two implementers will build two different things or, more likely, neither will build it and the §2 "this document specifies" list will be false at its fourth item.
- **Recommendation.** Delete the object and keep the doctrine. Colored evidence, as far as this document uses it, *is* the transformation law: evidence crosses, judgment does not, each frame computes its own color under its own committed lens (§12.2, §12.3). If a distinct wire object is genuinely wanted later — an evidence bundle carrying a claimed color as a warranty plus a lens citation — introduce it when a crossing needs it, define "view echo," and type it under §6's object-typing clause like the other three. Sibling handoff: SPC owns the undefined-term half under the same key.

### F4: Optional delegation for seated organs keeps a second seating construction lawful under which the document's own term-limit illustration is not decidable

- **Severity:** HIGH · **Confidence:** LIKELY · **dedupe_key:** `seat-delegation-optional-order-underivable` · **Objective function:** survivability · **Layer:** keri-core · **Bucket:** proof-scope (the checkability claim holds only in the delegated configuration)
- **Location:** proposal §10 (`:1410-1420`, `:1422-1433`), §11 governed seats (`:1487-1489`), §1.4 axiom 4 (`:266-270`), §4 position (`:745-750`), §8 (`:1274-1281`), §17 spine (`:2183-2191`); KERI `spec/spec-body.md:1282` (delegator seals the delegated event in its own KEL, re-anchored @`4df80aa`); prior art KN-15.
- **Finding.** 4.1 absorbed KN-15's *rationale* verbatim — the delegated-organ paragraph at `:1410-1420` names dual-anchoring, delegated-recovery, and the degenerate-policy exception exactly as KN-15 recommended — but adopted it at SHOULD. §11 then types the class as though the SHOULD were a SHALL: "**Governed seats** — delegated establishment events" (`:1487-1489`). The two spans disagree about whether a non-delegated seated organ exists.

  It matters because delegation is not merely tidier here; it is the only committed mechanism that supplies the *order* the fold needs. §10 illustrates: "Tenure is rotation policy: seat terms are establishment rotations on organ identifiers, and a term limit is a clause any verifier can check" (`:1422-1425`). §8 requires that "an enactment is lawful only if its author held the enacting power at the enactment's position" (`:1274-1276`), and §4 defines a position as a coordinate "in the committed order of the log it names" (`:745-750`) — per-log, as KERI's own design requires (per-identifier ordering only; no cross-KEL order exists in the corpus). For a *delegated* organ, every organ establishment event is sealed in the gAID's KEL (KERI `:1282`), so the organ's tenure rotation has a gAID coordinate and the comparison is derivable from committed bytes. For a *non-delegated* organ, nothing compels its rotations into the gAID's record: §17's spine anchors "seated organs' acts" (`:2185-2188`), but an organ's own key events are admitted by the medium without the gAID's participation and the gAID cannot force their anchoring.

- **Why it matters.** Failure scenario: a Constitution seats a non-delegated organ O (lawful under `:1410`). O performs a tenure rotation at O's own `sn` 5. An enactment by O is anchored at gAID `sn` 40. Appraising "did O hold the power at gAID `sn` 40" requires deciding whether O's `sn` 5 precedes gAID `sn` 40. No committed order relates them. The only thing that decides the finding is *how much of O's KEL is in the evidence bundle* — which is axiom 4's exact prohibition ("an uncommitted order that affects a finding is a commitment without ground", `:266-270`) and which also breaks §7.3's monotonicity ("appraisal under the larger bundle refines and never contradicts", `:1101-1106`): adding O's `sn` 6 span could flip a term-limit answer rather than refine it. The document's own claim that a term limit is "a clause any verifier can check" is true only in the delegated configuration it merely recommends.
- **Recommendation.** Raise `:1410` to SHALL for organs whose acts or tenure are appraised at gAID positions — which is every organ this document exercises — or, if a non-delegated construction must stay lawful, state the committed mechanism by which its key-state positions acquire a gAID-derivable order and add it to §17's boundary vectors as a must-reject when absent. Deleting the non-delegated construction is the cheaper repair and costs no exhibited capability. Sibling handoffs: SEC and KRT on the ordering hole under the same key.

### F5: The GEL is typed as a third log where the substrate already authorizes a governance-profiled registry; the log-vs-fold question is never argued

- **Severity:** MEDIUM · **Confidence:** CONFIRMED · **dedupe_key:** `gel-new-log-type-unearned-over-registry-profile` · **Objective function:** survivability · **Layer:** acdc · **Bucket:** non-goal (new type where a profile suffices)
- **Location:** proposal §1.2 (`:136-146`), §4 (`:642-663`), §17 track one (`:2216-2221`); ACDC `spec/spec-body.md:1918` @`651df33e`; keripy `src/keri/vdr/eventing.py:113, 254` @`5ba9b473`.
- **Finding.** The document concedes every structural fact that reduces the GEL: it is "a TEL-shaped log with governance semantics", it uses "the same discipline KERI's registry layer uses", and "this standard introduces no new anchoring pattern" (`:139-146`); §4 restates the seal discipline in full (`:652-663`), absorbing KN-10 properly. It then types the GEL as an extension log and asserts a three-log domain ("A governed domain reads three", `:138-141`). No passage anywhere in 2471 lines argues why the third log is a log rather than a registry profile — I read the whole text and grepped: "TEL-shaped" occurs twice, both as concession, and there is no GEL-vs-TEL passage.

  The substrate's own words close the gap in the reduction's favor: "Any number of transaction event types can be constructed for different applications that may be securely attributed without complicating KEL semantics. The seals need no semantics beyond their secure attributability to the AID of the KEL controller" (ACDC `:1918`). §17's own track one *is* the reduction — "GEL events use the substrate's registry event forms under their existing ilks, with governance semantics carried entirely by the committed law that interprets them" (`:2216-2220`) — offered as one lawful option rather than as the definition.

- **Why it matters.** This is nomenclature with a machine consequence, which is why it is a finding rather than a quibble. Typing the GEL as a distinct log is what makes a second grammar track feel like a natural degree of freedom (F2), makes a governance genus feel like a natural allocation (F2), and invites an implementer to build a GEL-specific store, fold front-end, and conformance surface beside the registry pipeline it could have profiled. It also spends the document's own scarce credibility: an outside standard that mints a third log type beside the KEL and TEL reads as annexation to this audience, whereas "a registry whose transaction event types are governance events, folded by a third fold" reads as consumption — and is the stronger claim, because the extension it actually needs (§1.5's amendable transition rule) is *fold* territory that nothing in the substrate occupies.
- **Recommendation.** Keep "GEL" as a name; retype it. One paragraph in §1.2 or §4 stating that the GEL is a registry (TEL) profiled by governance semantics, citing ACDC `:1918` as the substrate's own authorization for application-specific transaction event types, and that the standard's extension at this tier is the *Gever*, not the log. Then §17 track one becomes the definition and F2's deferral is natural rather than a subtraction.

F2's recommendation is nothing more than applying that habit one more time.
