**Date:** 2026-07-30 **Target(s):** `/home/daniel/code/3GR/custos` (subject: `spec/custos-4.1.md`, sha256 `ff8b9e7a…b72b05`, 2471 lines — pin re-verified at start); `kswg-acdc-specification`, `kswg-dossier-specification`, `kswg-keri-specification` (cross-referenced, re-anchored) **Effort:** medium **Objective lens:** survivability
**Sources used:** keri-doctrine (claim 11; disclosure shibboleths), review-house-style, orchestrating-reviews, bible `05` §4/§7-9, `06` §3-4, `07` §10; live re-anchors listed per finding.

## Executive summary

Custos 4.1 has a real and substantial disclosure surface — the verification cone, the availability charter's serving surface, and the SAID-commitment discipline of the seal ladder and edict typing — so this lens is load-bearing, and I do not return "nothing in my lens."

The document confesses the *baseline* privacy trade honestly (§6, `:910-922`), and I do not spend a finding on the confession itself. Three things it does **not** confess are findings. (1) The availability charter obligates a **serving surface at which strangers fetch cones and consumers query registries** (`:2008-2010`), with request authentication as the committed posture (`:2011-2015`) — a verification-time pull that identifies the replaying party to the very frame being audited. This contradicts §14's own deterrence claim that "the deterrence value of unenumerated observation survives only while the observers are unenumerated" (`:2002-2003`) and §12.3's claim that replay-native verification "is always available" (`:1679-1682`); and Custos nowhere adopts the substrate's own remedy, the Registrar/Observer split. This is the top repair from my lens. (2) The confessed confidentiality escape hatch does not compose: the redaction machinery it points at works by eliding an ACDC child block to its SAID, which is precisely what makes a replayed log span unreplayable — and the only fitting pending species, `absent`, has a committed cure ("arrival of the missing evidence", `:997`) that a confidentiality profile forbids by construction, so §6's "not a defect" is false as written. (3) The word *entropy*, *nonce*, *UUID*, and *salt* appear **zero times** in 2471 lines, while the kernel commits SAIDs and digest seals over low-cardinality governance content that is designedly undisclosed during contest windows — the "compact is not private" category error at the kernel's own typing clause.

None of these fault KERI-core for a property it never promised. All three are composition/deployment seams at the ACDC-disclosure and governance strata, and each names its stratum explicitly.

## Steelman

The strongest reading of Custos's privacy posture is that it is not a privacy design at all and correctly refuses to pretend otherwise. Governance evidence is *not* subject data: the GEL records law, and law that cannot be replayed by a stranger is not law but administration. §6's confession is exactly right in structure — of authenticity, auditability, and privacy, this kernel ranks authenticity first, says so on the record, and scopes the trade rather than hiding it. §12.3's "replay-native verification… needs no trust in the producing GARD at all" is the whole architecture's load-bearing move: it is what lets the standard delete the judge-above without replacing it with a warranty oligarchy, and *maximal* cone disclosure is what makes it work. A privacy critique that asks Custos to blind the cone is asking it to delete its own discipline mechanism, and that would be an invulnerability-flavored demand aimed at a survivability design.

Custos also gets several things right that a lazier design would get wrong. It has no phone-home *status* dependency: credential state rides the substrate's TEL discipline and the cone is bytes, not a live oracle. It refuses to enumerate observers (`:2000-2003`) — recognizing the watcher doctrine that unenumerated observation is the deterrent. Its `SHOULD` that seated organs be delegated identifiers of the gAID (`:1410-1415`) is, whether or not the author framed it this way, *per-facet AID discipline by construction*: a `dip` mints a fresh identifier per seat, so a natural person seated in two GARDs holds two uncorrelated organ AIDs. And "Discovery of the charter's endpoints rides the substrate's out-of-band introduction machinery" (`:2019-2021`) is the correct OOBI posture rather than a bespoke directory.

My findings attack the seams around that design, not the design.

## Top findings

### F1: The availability charter's serving surface makes replay-native verification a phone-home that identifies the replayer to the audited frame

- **Severity:** HIGH · **Confidence:** CONFIRMED · **dedupe_key:** `cone-service-identifies-replayers-to-audited-frame` · **Objective function:** survivability · **Layer:** governance (with an acdc-disclosure remedy available)
- **Bucket:** deployment-gap (a composition gap, not a confessed non-goal — see below)
- **Location:** `spec/custos-4.1.md:2008-2015` ("Request authentication"), `:1985-2006` (the charter), `:1642-1649` (§12.2 step 2, "B MUST fetch E's verification cone"), `:2000-2003` (unenumerated observation), `:1679-1682` (§12.3 "always available"). Re-anchored substrate: ACDC spec `kswg-acdc-specification/spec/spec-body.md:1693` — "A validator queries its Observer, not the Registrar, at a point of validation (PoV)… interactions between the Observer and Registrar occur when there are ACDC state changes, not when there is a PoV… This protects against forced validator-to-issuer correlation of ACDC usage, i.e., no forced phone home validation." Dossier spec `kswg-dossier-specification/spec/dossier-spec-body.md:467` names the signing AID of a request/citation as a primary correlation vector, and `:474` gives the mitigation (rotate the signing AID; AID pools for herd privacy).

**Finding.** §14 states flatly that "The charter obligates a serving surface — strangers fetch cones, consumers query registries, federates exchange recognition evidence — and every consumption path is a request at a host" (`:2008-2011`), and commits request authentication with "the substrate's own request-authentication mechanism [as] the design of record" (`:2013-2015`). Combined with §12.2's `MUST fetch` (`:1642`), the normative consumption path for a stranger is an authenticated pull against the producing frame's own host. The frame therefore learns, per request: which AID is replaying, which edict/cone, and when.

Two of the document's own claims do not survive that. First, `:2002-2003` — "the deterrence value of unenumerated observation survives only while the observers are unenumerated." A frame that serves authenticated cone requests **enumerates its own observers**, continuously and by design. The standard names the property its architecture depends on and then mandates the mechanism that destroys it. Second, `:1679-1682` — "Replay-native verification is always available to any party the availability charter reaches, and its permanent availability is what disciplines everything else." If reaching the baseline evidence requires identifying yourself to the party you intend to convict, availability is conditioned on a disclosure toll paid by exactly the party most exposed to retaliation. "Always available" is not achieved; it is *offered*.

Note precisely what this is **not**. It is not the OCSP shape — Custos has no verification-time *status oracle*, and I am not faulting KERI-core for correlation it never promised (doctrine claim 11). It is the *usage-correlation* half of the same family: the substrate solved it with the Registrar/Observer split (ACDC `:1693`), and Custos, which elsewhere reuses substrate machinery scrupulously, does not cite that split anywhere in 2471 lines. The parsimony charge cuts in the same direction as the privacy one here: the availability charter largely reduces to witness sets + OOBI + the Registrar/Observer split, and the place it fails to reduce is precisely the place it drops a privacy property the substrate supplies for free.

**Why it matters.** Concrete scenario: frame A's Constitution is under contest; an independent auditor B (or a rival federate) wants to replay A's GEL to test whether A's evaluator's affirmed finding recomputes. Under `:2008-2015`, B signs its request with an AID; A's host logs it. A now knows an audit is in progress, by whom, over which coordinate, before B publishes anything — and can rotate, freeze, withhold, or pre-emptively enact against B while B is still computing. §13's recourse ladder assumes the convicting party's evidence-gathering is not observable by the convicted; here it is. Worse for a federation: A learns the full topology of who consumes its edicts, which is exactly the "who is watching me" map that makes unenumerated observation deterrent. Two colluding frames A and A′ serving the same consumer join trivially on the consumer's request-signing AID (dossier `:467`).

**Recommendation (smallest change).** Two clauses in §14, no new construct:
1. State that the charter's replay-native baseline **SHALL be servable anonymously** — the composition-warranty and byte-verification path needs no requester identity, and identity may be demanded only for expanded or otherwise-restricted views (this is exactly the dossier spec's own pattern at `:480`: anonymous redacted service, signed request for expanded).
2. Add one sentence adopting the substrate's Registrar/Observer split by citation, as §6 already does for the dossier: cone and registry evidence MAY be served from consumer-side caches that sync on GEL/TEL state change rather than at consumption, and a frame SHALL NOT condition replay-native availability on service from its own host. Then §12.2 step 2's `MUST fetch` should say *obtain* — presented, cached, or fetched — rather than implying a pull at the producer.

This costs the standard nothing it claims: it is additive, cites machinery already in the substrate, and repairs both `:2002-2003` and `:1679-1682` on their own terms.

### F2: The confessed confidentiality escape hatch does not compose — a lawfully withheld cone span has no reachable cure, and the cited redaction machinery cannot apply to a replayed log span

- **Severity:** HIGH · **Confidence:** CONFIRMED · **dedupe_key:** `withheld-cone-span-has-no-cure-path` · **Objective function:** survivability · **Layer:** acdc (disclosure) / governance
- **Bucket:** deployment-gap (a layering that does not compose)
- **Location:** `spec/custos-4.1.md:910-922` (the confession), `:872-878` (transitive cone closure; "a replay that reaches for a span the cone lacks convicts the cone as short"), `:980-989` (the ratified pending-species rule), `:997-998` ("**absent** is cured by the arrival of the missing evidence"). Re-anchored: dossier spec `kswg-dossier-specification/spec/dossier-spec-body.md:458-460` — the redaction mechanism is "any child object within an ACDC can be replaced by its SAID without altering the SAID of the parent"; `:454-476` is the whole of that spec's privacy machinery.

**Finding.** §6's confession is not merely "we chose authenticity." It makes two affirmative, load-bearing claims that a deployment can rely on: (a) "nothing in this document forbids a deployment profile from adopting the graduated and redacted disclosure machinery the carriage specification provides," and (b) "a cone span withheld under such a profile is appraised by the ordinary pending species — an undisclosed span is a typed requirement, not a defect." Both fail.

(a) fails on *applicability*, not permission. The carriage specification's redaction machinery is ACDC child-block elision to a SAID (dossier `:458-460`). The cone's replay-relevant content is not ACDC child blocks — it is committed **log spans** (GEL, KEL, TEL) that the fold reads (`:854-857`, `:872-874`). A KEL event held only as a digest cannot be folded; eliding it does not "selectively hide sensitive information while remaining cryptographically verifiable" (dossier `:460`), it makes the fold undefined. The dossier machinery is applicable to the cone's *carriage metadata* and inapplicable to the thing that would need hiding. §6 points at a tool that does not fit the job, and does so in the one paragraph a privacy-constrained deployment will read.

(b) fails on *cure*. The pending species are a closed, ratified set — "absent, window-open, unresolved-conflict, and expired/abandoned" (`:982-983`) — and "Pending species describe cure paths" (`:985`). A lawfully withheld span can only be `absent`, whose committed cure is "the arrival of the missing evidence" (`:997-998`). Under a confidentiality profile the evidence will never arrive: withholding is the policy, not an accident of propagation. So the species' cure is unreachable by construction, and there is no species meaning *lawfully and permanently withheld*. The finding is not "a typed requirement" in any useful sense — it is a permanently non-terminal appraisal. And §6's disposition is in direct tension with §6's own closure rule three paragraphs earlier: a replay reaching for the withheld span "convicts the cone as short" (`:875-876`).

I want to be exact about the steelman here, because there is a good one: one may read "convicts the cone as short" as a judgment about the *carriage object* (the curator's composition warranty) rather than about the finding, so a redacted cone convicts the curator while the finding stays pending. That reading rescues the two clauses from formal contradiction — but it makes the composition *worse*, not better, from my lens: a deployment that redacts to protect a person thereby convicts its own curator on every presentation, permanently. Either way the escape hatch is not the costless deployment choice §6 presents.

**Why it matters.** Concrete scenario: a GARD's seating enactment cites a TEL span carrying a fitness attestation about a natural person (§6 concedes cones carry "evidence about persons", `:912-913`). A deployment operating under a data-minimization duty adopts a confidentiality profile and withholds that span. Every consuming frame's appraisal of every downstream edict whose standing traces through that seat now discharges `pending/absent` forever; no cure exists; the domain's edicts never affirm anywhere. The rational operator response is to abandon the confidentiality profile and disclose the person's evidence — which means the confessed escape hatch is not merely inert, it is *counter-incentivized*. The trade §6 says is scoped is, in practice, unscoped.

**Recommendation.** One of two small repairs, both kernel-local:
1. Add a fifth pending species — `withheld` (or `restricted`) — whose committed cure is *presentation under an admissibility profile*, i.e. it is cured by the consuming frame's law admitting a substitute (a composition warranty over the withheld span, or a predicate attestation) rather than by arrival of the bytes. This is the honest structure: a withheld span is a *frame-law* question, not an evidence-propagation question, and it needs its own cure path. It also gives F1's anonymous-baseline repair somewhere to land.
2. Failing that, correct §6 to say what is actually true: that redaction of replay-read spans is **not** available under the carriage specification's mechanism, that a withheld span discharges as permanently-uncurable `absent`, and that confidentiality profiles therefore trade *affirmability* for privacy rather than trading nothing. A confessed cost is fine; a cost presented as free is the finding.

### F3: The kernel commits SAIDs and digest seals over low-cardinality governance content and never requires a blinding factor — "compact is not private," at the object-typing clause

- **Severity:** MEDIUM · **Confidence:** CONFIRMED · **dedupe_key:** `committed-said-over-low-entropy-governance-content-unblinded` · **Objective function:** survivability · **Layer:** acdc (disclosure)
- **Bucket:** deployment-gap
- **Location:** `spec/custos-4.1.md:898-901` (edict typing: "a bare self-addressed data item, never an issuer-bearing credential container — … the center is the SAID"), `:1299-1302` (digest seal: "A commitment to exact bytes"), `:1403-1406` (contest windows "measured in log positions"), `:1347-1361` (anchor grade; charter/seat-revocation/law-amendment acts SHALL anchor in establishment events). Negative evidence, re-verified: `grep -ci 'entropy|nonce|uuid|salt'` over the pinned 2471 lines returns **0**. Re-anchored substrate: ACDC spec `kswg-acdc-specification/spec/spec-body.md:89` — "Without the entropy provided by the UUID, `u`, field, an adversary may be able to reconstruct the block contents merely from the SAID of the block and the Schema… using a rainbow or dictionary attack… Sufficient entropy in the `u` field, however, ensures that the cardinality of the power set allowed by the schema is at least as great as the entropy of the SAID digest algorithm itself."

**Finding.** Custos designs a commit-now/disclose-later pattern in several places at once. An edict's identity *is* its bare SAID, anchored by a GEL event (`:849-853`, `:898-901`). A digest seal commits to exact bytes with no requirement that the bytes travel (`:1299-1302`). §10 designs contest windows during which an act is committed but its effect is not yet settled (`:1403-1406`). §6 explicitly contemplates undisclosed spans (`:918-920`). In every one of those cases the digest is public in the GEL and the pre-image is not.

The kernel never once requires — or even mentions — a blinding factor over that pre-image. Governance content is exactly the worst case for this: an edict of a designated act class (`:1358` — "revocation of a seat, enactment amending law") is a small structured object over a governance schema whose value space is enumerable from public data — the seat roster is in the GEL, the act kinds are a closed table (§17's ilk table), the coordinate is public. The cardinality of that power set is trivially small against a Blake3-256 digest. Anyone can grind the space and read a sealed act before it is published — during the very contest window the frame designed to keep it contestable-but-not-yet-final.

This is the substrate's own named category error (`07` §10.2; ACDC `:89`, and "It only provides compactness, not privacy"), landed at Custos's own typing clause. Note the clause does not *forbid* the fix — a bare SAD may carry a `u` field — which is why this is MEDIUM rather than HIGH. But that is also its interop bite: two conforming implementations, one that blinds and one that does not, both satisfy §6's typing SHALL, and the unblinded one silently leaks every committed-but-undisclosed governance act. Under §14's "Stated evidence scale" and §14's interpretive-latitude duty (`:2023-2032`), this is precisely uncommitted latitude that the kernel should close rather than inherit open.

**Why it matters.** Concrete scenario: a GARD's Constitution commits a 40-position contest window on seat revocations (`:1403-1404`). An organ is to be revoked for cause. The revocation edict is anchored at position *n*; its SAID is public immediately, and by §9's anchor-grade rule it lands in an **establishment** event, so it is maximally visible and non-erasable. The target grinds the schema's power set — roster × act-kind × coordinate — recovers the pre-image within minutes, and has the full contest window to rotate assets, enact counter-measures, or flee, while the frame believes the act is committed-but-undisclosed. The kernel's own contest-window design is defeated by the absence of one field.

**Recommendation.** One sentence in §6's object-typing paragraph: an edict content or sealed governance object whose pre-image is not disclosed at anchoring time SHALL carry a high-entropy blinding UUID sufficient that the cardinality of the power set allowed by its schema is at least the entropy of the digest algorithm — citing ACDC §UUID Fields, in the same "cited, consumed, not restated" posture §6 already uses for the dossier. No new primitive, no new type, no wire change; it is a schema obligation on an object form the kernel already types. (Under the parsimony charge this is the rare place where *more* substrate reuse, not less, is the repair.)

## Additional patterns noted

- **Colored evidence's "committed view echo" (`:892`) is a candidate correlator I could not evaluate.** Round A already established the term is used once and defined nowhere. From my lens the specific unresolved question is whether the echo is a digest of *what the frame saw* (a per-consumer view fingerprint, and thus a join key any two consumers of the same object could compare) or a digest of the committed inputs (no new handle). I flag it under round A's existing key rather than opening a second one; whichever way §6 resolves the definition, the resolution should state which of the two it is. Handoff: KRT owns whether the echo *means* anything; PRV owns whether it *joins*.
- **Credit where due.** `:1410-1415` — "Seated organs SHOULD be delegated identifiers of the gAID" — is per-facet AID discipline arrived at from the delegation direction: a `dip` mints a fresh AID per seat, so a person seated in two GARDs is not correlatable by seat AID. The spec does not claim this as a privacy property and I am not asking it to, but if §6's disclosure posture is ever revised, this is the one place the design already pays a privacy dividend and should say so, so that deployments do not "simplify" it away by seating a pre-existing personal AID.
- **The parsimony charge and this lens agree on the availability charter.** Asked "does it reduce?", the charter is witness sets (substrate) + OOBI (substrate, and §14 says so at `:2019-2021`) + a cadence clause (§10) + a serving-surface obligation. The irreducible remainder is thin, and the one substrate component it conspicuously omits — the Registrar/Observer split — is the one that carries a privacy property. That is F1 restated in the charge's own terms.

## Residual unknowns

- **Whether §12.2 step 2's "B MUST fetch" is normatively a pull.** The sentence reads "B MUST fetch E's verification cone — presented as a verifiable dossier where the producing frame carries it across the boundary" (`:1642-1644`), which mixes a pull verb with a push clause. §14's "strangers fetch cones… every consumption path is a request at a host" (`:2009-2011`) resolves it toward a pull, and F1 is written on that reading. If the intended reading is push-only — the presenter always carries the cone and the serving surface exists only as a fallback — then F1's severity drops to MEDIUM and the repair collapses to a single clarifying word ("obtain"). I could not close this from the pinned text; it is the one thing that would most change my report. **needs-info is a legitimate disposition for F1's severity, not for its existence** — even under the push reading, `:2002-2003` and the authenticated serving surface still conflict for any stranger who was not presented to.
- **Whether any deployment profile is expected to exist.** §6's escape hatch and §14's admissibility profile for unenumerated-observer evidence are both "committed deliverables of the deployment lane." If the deployment lane is genuinely near-term, F2's repair could live there rather than in the kernel — except that the pending-species set is *ratified kernel law* (`:980-989`), so a deployment profile cannot add the species it needs. That is why F2 is a kernel finding.
