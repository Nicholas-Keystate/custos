## Executive summary

I did not expect to have a lens here, and for most of the document I do not: fourteen of seventeen sections are pure semantics above the bytes. But Custos 4.1 is not the pure-semantics proposal my calibration warns me about. Section 17 is new to this edition (the appendix accounts it as "section 17 whole" with no predecessor counterpart, `:2451`), and it reaches into the wire in three places — event identity, a genus reservation, and a count-code-framed receipt form. Two of those three are defective in my terms, and a third defect runs through §3, §6 and §14 together.

The single biggest finding: **Custos makes byte-identical replay its class-defining obligation and never commits a single byte of encoding.** Two conforming producers may serialize the same governance act as JSON or CBOR, digest its identity with any of five distinct 256-bit CESR suites, and (for count-code-framed material) take the preimage in either the text or the binary domain — all lawful under the text as written, all yielding different bytes. The document's own §14 forecloses the repair by asserting that "the encoding layer is closed," which is true of *parsing* a given stream and false of *producing* one. The repo's own ruling docket already circles this as R5 (#9, MAJOR); my contribution is the concrete shopping list of what "pin the encoding" would have to pin, and the sentence that has been hiding the gap.

Second: §17's **Genus** paragraph places an entry in the one CESR table the spec requires to be identical across every protocol under per-domain committed law, supersedable by that domain's own enactment. That is a category error at the wire layer, and its supersession limb is a silent mis-read rather than a clean reject.

On the charge's reduction test, my honest answer helps Custos on cost and hurts it on parsimony: **from the wire, the GEL and the warranty have zero irreducible remainder and zero cost.** Details in "The reduction test from the bytes" below.

## Steelman

Custos's wire posture is, on the whole, the *correct* posture for a governance layer, and I want to say so before I strike.

It refuses to invent a carriage. §1.2 states outright that the GEL "is a TEL-shaped log with governance semantics: its events are sealed into the anchoring KEL by the same discipline KERI's registry layer uses, and this standard introduces no new anchoring pattern" (`:141-144`); §4 repeats it as "a seal in the anchoring event carrying the GEL event's self-addressing identifier" (`:654-658`). That is literally KERI's existing seal-plus-anchor grammar — `-G` SealSourceCouples under the v1 counter table, `-S` under v2 (keripy `src/keri/core/counting.py`, `CounterCodex_1_0.SealSourceCouples = '-G'`, `CounterCodex_2_0.SealSourceCouples = '-S'`) — with a digest seal in the anchoring event's `a` field. Zero new primitives, zero new count codes, zero sizing entries, zero version bump.

It typed its objects into the substrate's own toolchain rather than inventing containers: §6 "A warranty SHALL be a schema-typed, registry-bound attestation in the substrate's credential discipline" (`:901-904`). That is KN-18 genuinely absorbed, not nominally.

It absorbed KN-08 correctly. The 4.0 kernel hardcoded "a placeholder of forty-four '#' characters"; 4.1 now reads "the substrate's rule is length-parametric by derivation code, and forty-four characters is this document's current profile, the 256-bit digest class" (`:551-554`). That is exactly the repair the prior substrate review asked for, and I re-verified the arithmetic: a 32-byte raw under a one-character code gives `ps = (3 - (32 mod 3)) mod 3 = 1`, `cs = 1`, `fs = ((32+1)*4)/3 = 44`. keripy agrees (`src/keri/core/coring.py:770-774`, `Sizage(hs=1, ss=0, xs=0, fs=44, ls=0)` for `E`,`F`,`G`,`H`,`I`). The document's arithmetic is right.

It absorbed KN-19 at the grade KN-19 recommended: §15 confesses "the carriage encoding of this document's object classes — a committed deliverable whose default posture is the substrate's native composable attachment grammar rather than document envelopes" (`:2069-2072`). A CESR author reads that and is reassured, not alarmed.

And §17's one substrate claim that I could falsify, I could not falsify: "the substrate's receipt form does not make its identifier field self-addressing" (`:2278-2280`) is **correct**. KERI spec `spec/spec-body.md:882`: "The top-level fields of a Receipt, `rct` message body MUST appear in the following order: `[ v, t, d, i, s]`… The Signatures or Seals are on the Key event indicated by the top-level fields of the Receipt, not the Receipt message body itself." The `d` is the receipted event's SAID, not the receipt's. Custos diagnosed that correctly and gated on it.

The strongest reading of the whole wire posture is therefore: *we are not touching the wire; where we must, we confess the deliverable and gate it.* My findings are the places where that discipline slipped.

## The reduction test from the bytes

The charge asks every persona to run the reduction test. My answer is narrow but, I think, useful, because it settles the *cost* limb (question 4) for two constructs decisively.

**(a) GEL vs TEL — nearest substrate equivalent, and the wire cost of the third log type.** Under track one (`:2216-2221`), a GEL event *is* a substrate registry event: "GEL events use the substrate's registry event forms under their existing ilks, with governance semantics carried entirely by the committed law that interprets them." Under track two, the difference is a `t`-field ilk — a JSON/CBOR string *value*, not a CESR primitive. No code-table entry, no `Matter.Sizes` row, no count-code group, no version-string change, no shadow table touched, no cold-start tritet affected. **The wire cost of the GEL as a distinct log type is exactly zero.** That is a genuine credit to Custos on question 4 and a genuine debit on question 2: whatever remainder the GEL has, it is not on the wire, and the spec is right not to claim it is. The only place the GEL costs bytes is the genus reservation, which is finding F3 — i.e. the one wire cost the GEL incurs is the one it did not need to incur.

**(b) Warranty vs ACDC.** §6 types it as an ACDC in so many words (`:901-904`), with the lens "cited by edge." An edge is the ACDC `e` section; a pinned lens is an edge with a SAID reference; a recomputable finding is `a`-section content plus the replay triple. Zero new bytes. **The warranty's wire cost over a plain ACDC is zero.** Whether "warranty" earns a name in a seven-primitive budget is SKP's and SPC's call, not mine — but nobody should argue for it on carriage grounds, because there is no carriage difference to argue about.

**(c) Covenant seal vs KERI's seal grammar.** Round A already settled that its carriage reduces to KERI's typed seal with a reserved `t` value. I re-checked and agree; I have nothing to add and do not re-derive it.

**(d) Cone vs dossier.** §6 delegates carriage to the ToIP dossier specification explicitly (`:859-862`). That is reuse, not invention; no wire finding.

The general shape: **Custos's constructs are semantically expensive and byte-free.** For a parsimony review that is worth stating plainly, because "it adds a type" and "it adds a wire cost" are different charges, and only the first one lands here.

## Top findings

### F1: Byte-identical replay is the class-defining obligation, and no encoding is committed anywhere in the document

- **Severity:** HIGH · **Confidence:** CONFIRMED · **dedupe_key:** `byte-identity-has-no-committed-encoding` · **Objective function:** survivability · **Layer:** wire · **Bucket:** deployment-gap
- **Location:** Custos `:423-426` (§2, the defining obligation), `:1037-1038` (§7.3, "Two evaluations of the same triple SHALL return byte-identical findings"), `:2209-2211` (§17, permuted-order streams "SHALL fold to byte-identical Constitutions"), `:2288-2290` (§17 equivalence vectors, "byte-identical fold results"); against `:551-554` (§3 rule 2), `:2193-2197` (§17 event identity, "the digest ranges over the event's complete canonical bytes"). Re-anchored substrate: CESR spec `spec/spec-body.md:1154` (four serialization kinds `JSON|CBOR|MGPK|CESR`), `:1135` ("Each field map MAY have a different serialization type"), `:1203-1205` (SAID derivation uses "the digest algorithm specified by the CESR derivation code of the copied SAID"); keripy `src/keri/core/coring.py:247-251` and `:770-774`.
- **Finding.** Custos requires byte-identity at four separate keyword-force sites and pins none of the three degrees of freedom that determine bytes.
  1. **Serialization kind.** CESR admits four (`JSON`, `CBOR`, `MGPK`, `CESR`-native), and per CESR spec `:1135` "Each field map MAY have a different serialization type." Custos never names one. The word "JSON" does not appear in the document; nor does "CBOR", "MGPK", "serialization", or "version string" (grep of the pinned bytes).
  2. **Digest derivation code.** §3 rule 2's repair of KN-08 was correct but stopped one step short: it pins the *length class*, "forty-four characters… the 256-bit digest class." Length is not a type. keripy `coring.py:247-251` registers five distinct 256-bit suites — `E` Blake3-256, `F` Blake2b-256, `G` Blake2s-256, `H` SHA3-256, `I` SHA2-256 — and `coring.py:770-774` gives all five the identical `Sizage(hs=1, ss=0, xs=0, fs=44, ls=0)`. Every one of them satisfies "forty-four characters, the 256-bit digest class." Verification is safe (a verifier reads the code off the qb64 and recomputes with it, per CESR `:1204`), but *derivation* is unconstrained: two conforming producers hand the same governance act two different SAIDs and both conform.
  3. **Domain.** Where a preimage is count-code-framed material rather than a field map, T and B are two lossless encodings of the same primitives with different bytes; Custos never says which. (This limb is sharpened separately in F4.)
  §17's "complete canonical bytes" (`:2197`) is the only place the document gestures at a canonicalization rule, and it defines nothing — it does not name insertion order (which is the substrate's rule, CESR `:1250-1256`, and a pointed rejection of JCS), a kind, or a code.
- **Why it matters.** Concretely: implementation A serializes a GEL amendment event as insertion-ordered JSON and SAIDs it with Blake3-256 (`E…`); implementation B serializes the identical logical event as CBOR and SAIDs it with SHA2-256 (`I…`). Both conform to every ruled span in Custos 4.1. Their GEL event identities differ, so their law heads differ, so their findings cite different law heads, so §7.3's "byte-identical findings" fails on identical inputs — and §16's confessed interop deliverable ("two independent implementations deriving equal corpus identities… from one committed corpus", `:2140-2142`) is not merely undemonstrated but *underivable from the text*. The §2 confession covers the missing evidence; it does not cover the missing rule. Per house style, a confessed gap asserted elsewhere as settled is a finding, and §2/§7.3/§17 assert it at SHALL force.
- **Relation to prior art.** This is the wire adjudication of the repo's own open ruling **R5 (#9, MAJOR)**, which frames it as "three predicates, one obligation" and offers "A — Pin the encoding now" vs "B — restate semantically." I take no position between A and B; I supply what A would actually have to pin, which the docket entry does not enumerate: *(i)* one serialization kind for committed objects (or a rule making the kind derivable from committed bytes — note the cold-start tritet does distinguish JSON/CBOR/MGPK from the first byte, CESR `:411-420`, so "derivable" is achievable); *(ii)* one digest derivation code as the document's default profile, stated as a code, not a length (KERI's own default is Blake3-256 — keripy `coring.py:3703`, `3720`, `4026`, `4060` all default `code=DigDex.Blake3_256`); *(iii)* insertion-order field maps, cited to CESR `:1250-1256`; *(iv)* for count-code-framed preimages, the domain and the genus/version (see F4).
- **Recommendation.** Smallest change: add one clause to §3's reading rules — "committed objects of this document are serialized as insertion-ordered `<kind>` field maps and self-addressed under CESR derivation code `E` (Blake3-256) as this document's current profile; a domain committing a different profile commits it in its founding law." That converts byte-identity from an aspiration into a testable predicate without designing anything §15 confesses open.

### F2: "The encoding layer is closed" is true of parsing and false of producing — and it cancels the confession duty over the exact surface F1 leaves open

- **Severity:** MEDIUM · **Confidence:** CONFIRMED · **dedupe_key:** `encoding-layer-closed-misstates-substrate` · **Objective function:** survivability · **Layer:** wire · **Bucket:** deployment-gap
- **Location:** Custos `:2027-2032` (§14, Interpretive latitude): "the encoding layer is closed (a serialization either parses canonically or fails), but semantic latitude above it… remains open wherever the substrate's own law is silent. A federated GARD SHALL state which latitude it has closed by committed profile and which it inherits open." Re-anchored substrate: CESR spec `:1135`, `:1154`, `:1203-1205`, `:1250-1256`.
- **Finding.** The parenthetical is a claim about the substrate, and it is half true. In the *consumer* direction it holds at spec grade: given correct tables, a CESR stream either parses or fails, and a verifier has no interpretive room. In the *producer* direction it is false, and the producer direction is the one Custos's obligations range over. CESR explicitly leaves the kind free per field map (`:1135`: "Each field map MAY have a different serialization type"), leaves the digest suite to the producer's choice of derivation code (`:1204`), and — the sharpest counterexample, because it is normative and in the spec's own voice — documents an unclosed round-trip: for CESR-native field maps, "once deserialized into an in-memory data object representation of that field map, there is no normative indication that the in-memory object was deserialized from a CESR native field map… This serialization kind indication would otherwise have to be provided externally" (CESR `:1154`). That is uncommitted encoding-layer latitude, stated by the encoding layer about itself.
- **Why it matters.** This sentence is not decoration; it is load-bearing on the SHALL beside it. §14 obligates a federated GARD to state which latitude it has closed and which it inherits open — and then tells it that the encoding layer needs no such statement. An implementer following §14 literally will confess threshold-derivation defaults and escrow retention (the examples the sentence gives) and will not confess its serialization kind or digest code, which are the divergence surfaces that actually break replay. §14 is therefore the mechanism by which F1 stays invisible.
- **Steelman I could not sustain.** Read charitably, the sentence means "there is no *interpretive* latitude in parsing," which is defensible. But §14's whole subject is the divergence of two conformant verifiers over the same governance question, and two verifiers that *produce* differently diverge exactly as badly as two that *read* differently. The narrow reading does not save the SHALL that depends on it.
- **Recommendation.** Replace the parenthetical with the honest split: "parsing a committed serialization admits no latitude; *producing* one does — kind, derivation code, and domain are producer choices the substrate leaves open — and a federated GARD SHALL commit its encoding profile among the latitudes it closes."

### F3: A CESR protocol genus is placed under per-domain committed law, supersedable by enactment — the one table CESR requires every protocol to hold identically

- **Severity:** HIGH · **Confidence:** CONFIRMED · **dedupe_key:** `governance-genus-self-allocation` · **Objective function:** survivability · **Layer:** wire · **Bucket:** deployment-gap
- **Location:** Custos `:2262-2272` (§17, Genus): "The encoding substrate's genus namespace admits reservation, and a reservation of a governance genus is itself an enactment: committed in the GEL of the domain that reserves it, carrying the reserved coordinates and their intended grammar, judged and superseded under that domain's law. Recognition of the reservation by the substrate's stewards is a distinct, later, bilateral event — a federation-shaped recognition, not a precondition." Re-anchored substrate: CESR spec `:609` (a genus/version code "MUST modify the protocol genus and Version of all the following Count Codes"), `:611` ("The only table that all protocols MUST share (i.e., has identical values) is the protocol genus and version table… All other entries in the small and large count code tables and all other tables MAY vary by protocol. Secondly, for a given protocol genus, a protocol genus and version code MUST provide the Version of that given protocol's table set"), `:615` (`-_GGGVVV`), Annex A `:783` (entry policy: "first needed first-entered"); keripy `src/keri/core/counting.py:29-33` (`GenusCodex`: KERI `-_AAA`, ACDC `-_AAB`, SPAC `-_AAC`, TSP_ `-_AAD`), `:222` (`self._genus = GenDex.KERI  # only supports KERI`), `:1235` (`raise UnexpectedCodeError("Unsupported code ={}.")`).
- **Finding.** Two defects, one paragraph.
  1. **Unilateral occupancy of a globally-shared slot.** `GGG` is a three-character namespace with 262,144 points, allocated centrally by a stewarded table under a stated entry policy (CESR Annex A `:783`) and mirrored in the reference implementation's `GenusCodex`. Custos makes occupancy a unilateral act of one domain, explicitly with steward recognition "not a precondition." Uniqueness of a genus point is not derivable from any domain's committed bytes and no fold can compute it — so two domains can lawfully hold the same `GGG` under their own committed law, and Custos supplies no committed rule that could ever convict either. The mirror question in the charge lands here: Custos's "no authority above the parties" is correct for law and does not carry to a shared code-point namespace, which is a resource-allocation problem with a steward, not a jurisdiction problem. Note this is a substrate requirement, not an imported PKI prior — doctrine claim 13's crypto-agility guarantee holds *only* because a new code occupies a genuinely unused slot.
  2. **Supersession without a version bump — the silent limb.** "judged and superseded under that domain's law" places revision of the genus's *table set* under domain law. CESR places it under the `VVV` triple carried in the stream (`:611`: "for a given protocol genus, a protocol genus and version code MUST provide the Version of that given protocol's table set. This allows versioning of the CESR code tables for a given protocol"). A domain that supersedes its genus grammar by enactment and keeps emitting `-_XXXCAA` has produced two different table sets behind one eight-character code. Because the universal count codes are shared and counts are in quadlets/triplets, the *outer* framing survives — so a parser does not throw; it types the group's contents against the wrong table and mis-reads them. That is the silent-accept downgrade, not a clean reject.
  Note the reassignment magnitude that makes this concrete: between the v1 and v2 counter tables of the *same* genus, keripy shows `-A` moving from `ControllerIdxSigs` to `GenericGroup` and `-G` from `SealSourceCouples` to `MapBodyGroup` (`counting.py`, `CounterCodex_1_0` vs `CounterCodex_2_0`). A table-set revision is not cosmetic.
- **What is genuinely fine.** An unrecognized genus *hard code* is cleanly rejected by keripy: `Counter._exfil` looks the hard up in `_sizes` and raises `UnexpectedCodeError` when absent (`counting.py:1233-1235`), and the parser only reacts to `-_AAA` (`parsing.py:1032`). So a third party is not silently corrupted by an unknown reservation. The hazard is between parties who both claim the same reserved genus, and between a reserver and a later steward allocation.
- **Mitigation I must state.** The Genus paragraph carries no BCP 14 keyword, so under §3 reading rule 1 it "binds nothing on its own." I do not think that disposes of it: the paragraph describes a reservation the standard's record has *already enacted* (`:2269-2272`), makes a factual claim about the substrate that is false as stated ("the genus namespace admits reservation"), and §15's openness clause is itself keyword-force that prose implying design beyond the boundary "SHALL be treated as a defect of that prose, reviewable and repairable as such" (`:2047-2050`). This is exactly that. But synthesis should weigh the keywordless status when ranking against the ruled-span findings.
- **Why it matters.** Domain A reserves `-_AAE` for governance grammar v1.00 and publishes cones. Domain B, independently, reserves `-_AAE`. A federate holding both, or a steward that later allocates `-_AAE` to a fifth protocol, has three incompatible table sets behind one code with no committed procedure to resolve them. Meanwhile domain A supersedes its own grammar under its own law without touching `VVV`; its pre- and post-supersession streams are indistinguishable to any parser, and groups after the genus code are typed against whichever table the reader happens to hold.
- **Recommendation.** Two sentences. First: a governance genus reservation SHALL cite a code point coordinated through the substrate's own code-table entry process, and a domain SHALL NOT emit streams under an unrecognized genus onto media shared with parties that have not committed to that reservation. Second: revision of a reserved genus's table set SHALL be expressed as a `VVV` version increment in the genus/version code, never solely as a GEL enactment — table-set revision is versioned in the stream, per CESR `:611`. Note also that the ilk table of track two is unaffected by this finding: ilks are `t`-field string values, not CESR codes, and governing them by GEL enactment is unobjectionable at the wire. The paragraph's defect is that it treats a code-table selector as though it were an ilk.

### F4: The compact-form gate omits the two commitments that actually determine the bundle digest — the domain and the genus/version

- **Severity:** MEDIUM · **Confidence:** CONFIRMED · **dedupe_key:** `bundle-commitment-preimage-domain-unpinned` · **Objective function:** survivability · **Layer:** wire · **Bucket:** deployment-gap
- **Location:** Custos `:2274-2284` (§17, The compact form gate): "A compact, count-code-framed receipt form… gated, in order, on: the bundle-commitment rule (a committed preimage recipe by which one digest addresses a receipt together with its attachments; owed because the substrate's receipt form does not make its identifier field self-addressing), the governed ilk-table seats of track two, and conformance vectors exercising both presentation orders." Re-anchored substrate: CESR spec `:5` (composability — a concatenation of primitives converts T↔B "as a group… without loss"), `:198` (24-bit alignment), `:581` (count invariant between T and B domains); keripy `src/keri/core/coring.py` `Matter.composable` (`len(qb64b) % 4 == 0 and len(qb2) % 3 == 0 and encodeB64(qb2) == qb64b`); `src/keri/core/counting.py` `CounterCodex_1_0` vs `CounterCodex_2_0`.
- **Finding.** The gate list is committed and ordered, and the diagnosis behind it is correct (KERI spec `:882` confirms the receipt's `d` addresses the receipted event, not the receipt). But "a committed preimage recipe by which one digest addresses a receipt together with its attachments" is under-determined in exactly two CESR-specific ways the gate list does not name:
  1. **Domain.** Attachments are count-code-framed CESR material, and T and B are two lossless encodings of the same primitives with *different bytes* — that is the whole content of composability. `Matter.composable` in keripy asserts precisely that `qb64b` and `qb2` are distinct byte strings related by Base64. A digest over "the receipt together with its attachments" is therefore two different digests depending on whether the preimage is taken in T or B. The recipe must pin one.
  2. **Genus/version of the framing.** The attachment framing itself is table-dependent: the same logical receipt couple carries the code `-C` (`NonTransReceiptCouples`) under the v1 counter table and `-M` under v2, and the whole letter space is reassigned between them. A preimage over framed material therefore also varies with the CESR genus/version in force, which the recipe must pin or make derivable.
- **Why it matters.** This is a deferred deliverable, and I am not faulting the deferral — I am faulting the *committed gate list*, which is normative-shaped and which an implementer will treat as complete. Two implementations building the compact form against exactly this list, both standing every named gate, produce different bundle digests from the same receipt-plus-attachments, and the divergence is invisible until a cross-implementation vector runs — which §16 confesses does not yet exist. The gate that would have caught it is the one missing from the list.
- **Recommendation.** Add to the first gate: "…and the recipe SHALL name the domain (text or binary) in which the preimage is taken and the CESR genus and version of the framing it ranges over." Two clauses, no design commitment beyond what the recipe already owes.

### F5: The edict's mandated form — a bare SAD — is not a framable stream element, while the same clause claims the substrate's toolchain consumes it

- **Severity:** MEDIUM · **Confidence:** CONFIRMED · **dedupe_key:** `edict-bare-sad-carriage-unstated` · **Objective function:** survivability · **Layer:** wire · **Bucket:** deployment-gap
- **Location:** Custos `:898-908` (§6, Object typing): "An edict's content SHALL be a bare self-addressed data item… Object forms typed this way are consumable by the substrate's existing toolchain; nothing here requires a bespoke parser." Also `:849-853` (edict definition, "a bare SAID-addressed content… the center is the SAID"), `:1642-1646` (§12.2 step 2 — B "MUST fetch E's verification cone"). Re-anchored substrate: CESR spec `:1131` ("Non-CESR serializations… when interleaved in a CESR Stream MUST have a Version String as their first field with field label, `v`"), `:1135` ("The Version String, `v` field MUST be the first field in any top-level field map of any interleaved JSON, CBOR, or MGPK serialization"), `:374-376` (top-level-only interleaving; when nested, non-native serializations "MUST be encoded as a CESR primitive and then enclosed in a special count code for non-native messages"), `:438`; keripy `src/keri/core/serdering.py:815` (`raise ValidationError(f"Missing version string field in {self._sad}.")`) and `:1042` (`raise SerializeError(f"Missing required version string field 'v'…")`).
- **Finding.** A "bare self-addressed data item" is, by construction, a field map without a `v` field. Such a body cannot be interleaved at the top level of a CESR stream — CESR `:1135` makes the version string a MUST for exactly that position — and the substrate's message toolchain refuses it outright: keripy's `Serder` raises on a missing `v` at both the parse and the serialize path. The only lawful carriage is the nested one: wrap it as a CESR primitive inside the non-native body count code (`-W`/`--W` in the v1 table, `-H`/`--H` in v2), or carry it as pathed material. Custos never says which, and §15 confesses carriage as an open deliverable (`:2069-2072`) — yet §6 asserts in the same breath that the form is toolchain-consumable and parser-free. That is the "confessed gap presented as settled" shape.
- **Steelman, partially sustained.** As *sealed data* rather than a stream message, a bare SAD is genuinely substrate-native: the substrate seals bare SADs by digest all the time, `Saider` computes and verifies their SAIDs with no `v` field, and no bespoke parser is required to read one once you hold its bytes and know its kind. So the sentence is defensible for the edict-at-rest. It is not defensible for the edict-in-transit, and §12.2 step 2 puts edicts in transit — a consuming frame fetches the cone across a boundary. The residual is narrow but real, and it compounds with F1: the edict's *identity* is its SAID (`:851`, "the center is the SAID"), and a SAID over a `v`-less field map is a function of kind, field order, and derivation code, none of which the document pins. So the one object whose identity is defined to be its content digest is the one object whose content digest is not determined by its content.
- **Recommendation.** One sentence in §6 or §15: "An edict travelling in a CESR stream is carried as a non-native body inside the substrate's non-native message count code; it is not a top-level stream element, and this document commits no `v` field for it." That preserves the bare-SAD design decision (which I think is right — an issuer field really would smuggle a spine) while making the claim about toolchain consumption true.

## Additional patterns noted

- **Insertion order is never stated, and it is the one canonicalization rule the substrate cares about.** §17 says the digest "ranges over the event's complete canonical bytes" (`:2197`) and §7.3 requires canonical order for requirement elements "(subject, then kind, then citing-clause bytes)" (`:1050-1051`) — a *lexicographic* rule. Nothing tells a reader that field maps are insertion-ordered, not lexicographic. CESR `:1250-1256` is emphatic on this point and treats lexicographic canonicalization (JCS) as a rejected prior.
