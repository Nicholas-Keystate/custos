## Findings register

### T41-01 — BLOCKER — Refusal is incorrectly made a pending finding

Chapter lines 126–130 say that when law runs out, the evaluator “refuses” and that “the refusal is itself a finding, typed pending.” The ratified kernel expressly distinguishes these cases:

- Pending means committed evidence is incomplete but the proposition remains well-formed (kernel lines 547–559).
- Refusal means the invocation is ill-posed because an ordering or composition rule is uncommitted; it is an operational fact, not a fifth finding value (kernel lines 790–813).

This is a direct contradiction of ratified codomain doctrine, not merely compression. It also infects the stranger story at chapter lines 42–45: silence in the page could mean either a pending evidentiary requirement or absence of a rule needed to form the question, and the text collapses them.

Repair shape: split “evidence missing under a committed rule,” which returns a typed pending finding, from “no committed rule makes the invocation evaluable,” which produces a refusal record outside the finding codomain.

### T41-02 — MAJOR — The smallest case collapses the four-valued codomain

Chapter lines 42–45 offer only:

1. the page answers, producing a cited finding; or
2. the page is silent, producing refusal.

The ratified codomain separately requires affirmed, defeated, pending, and self-convicted values (kernel lines 547–565), with distinct grounds and transition behavior. “The page answers” does not expose defeat by citation versus self-conviction by the subject’s contradictory bytes. The passage calls itself “the whole machine” (chapter lines 25–26) and §1.7 declares the minimal case expressible entirely in the chapter’s primitives (lines 272–282), so this is more than a harmless illustrative subset.

Repair shape: make the minimal case traverse or explicitly branch over all four finding values, while separately showing refusal at an invocation that lacks a committed evaluation rule.

### T41-03 — MAJOR — Replay is stated over an insufficient input identity

Chapter lines 158–165 say the same committed bytes produce the same state and that identical GELs entail identical Constitutions. Yet the chapter itself defines the Gever as folding a GEL in the context of cited KELs and TELs (lines 74–79). The kernel fixes a finding’s inputs as the evidence bundle, law head, and appraisal position, with byte identity required over the same triple (kernel lines 639–644). It also makes completeness of view explicitly non-ambient (kernel lines 1674–1682).

Two identical GEL byte streams can resolve differently if their cited key or registry evidence, law head, position, or available verification cone differs. The inherited kernel sentence at lines 299–306 has the same looseness; recomposition into an axiom amplifies it into chapter-wide law.

Repair shape: define replay identity over a canonical committed input tuple containing the GEL span, every cited lower-tier input, law head, and position; reserve “identical Constitutions” for equality of that complete tuple.

### T41-04 — MAJOR — The five-axiom “floor” silently omits fixed fold law

Chapter lines 150–152 say the five axioms bind every fold and that none is new; lines 183–188 then say these five are what a governed domain is. But the ratified evaluator boundary contains additional fixed obligations:

Repair shape: either expand the common axiom floor to carry every ratified fold-binding wall, or explicitly type the five as a non-exhaustive foundational subset and normatively import the remaining evaluator obligations by named composition.

### T41-05 — MAJOR — “A more capable Gever is a category error” closes ratified-open interior

Chapter lines 206–217 fix the Gever in every dimension except its law input and declare greater capability a category error. Ratified law fixes the evaluator’s boundary—codomain, refusal, determinism and related walls—but leaves its interior undesigned (kernel lines 1332–1345 and 1627–1657).

A future engine could acquire new scheduling, proof, optimization, or analysis capability while preserving exactly the fixed boundary. That would be a more capable implementation without being a new primitive or constructor. Engine independence does not entail capability invariance.

The later bounds at chapter lines 243–247 narrow the rule to the standard, but do not cure the ontological absolute at lines 207–210. This is new law wearing definitional form. As a design convention it may be sound; as a derivation from ratified law it is not.

Repair shape: constrain what the standard may add to the evaluator’s public type, while expressly preserving implementation and interior capability growth behind the fixed observable boundary.

### T41-06 — MAJOR — Color confuses fold, input law, and computed output

Chapter lines 251–258 define color as “fold joined to committed law” and then say Constitution and color are “one computed state read twice.” Those are different type constructions:

- The Gever is a fold/evaluator.
- Committed law is one of its inputs.
- The Constitution is its computed output (kernel lines 293–305).

A function-plus-input at a position may determine an output, but it is not thereby identical to that output. The following claim that colors are compared by comparing clause sets directly (chapter lines 260–266) also ignores engine profile, cited lower-tier state, position, and semantic equivalence: syntactically different clause sets may compute the same distinctions, while identical law bytes evaluated over different committed contexts may not yield the same state.

Repair shape: choose one typed identity and hold it consistently—either color is the configured fold-in-force, or color is a projection of computed constitutional state. Define color equivalence semantically over the complete replay input and observable distinctions, not by unqualified clause-set comparison.

### T41-07 — MAJOR — The covenant-seal commitment drifts from a covenant set to an unspecified predicate

Chapter lines 96–106 say a covenant seal “commits a subject to a predicate over its successors.” Ratified doctrine binds the subject to the GARD’s covenant set—standing law—with each successor evaluated against the committed clause set (kernel lines 378–388 and 915–925).

A predicate could be equivalent to that set only if its identity, derivation, governing law head, and succession scope are fixed. The chapter supplies none of those qualifications. It also drops the ratified admissibility boundary: covenant seals are lawful only where lineage is the invariant; where byte equality is possible, the digest seal is required.

Repair shape: retain commitment to the identified standing-law clause set, state how its successor predicate is derived, and preserve the lineage-only admissibility rule and digest-seal preference.

### T41-08 — MINOR — The no-write/succession explanation arrives after the apparent contradiction

Succession is defined at chapter lines 108–118 as “the fold’s law changing by enactment,” after the fold has been declared never to write (lines 74–82). Repair shape: at the first succession definition, identify enactment as a constructor operation and state that the fold merely reads the resulting committed successor law.

## Declared supersessions

**S1 — “KERI detects; a GARD adjudicates”: SOUND-WITH-REPAIRS.**

“Adjudicates” does not promise class-wide prevention. The ratified tier table confines prevention to consequence inside the frame and says that beyond the perimeter recourse is conversion, never prevention (kernel lines 1439–1457). The verb therefore survives the prevention test.

Its remaining risk is plane ambiguity: adjudication sounds like the evaluator’s judgment alone, while a GARD’s autonomic loop also contains constructor enactment (kernel lines 1350–1361). The chapter’s two-verb split can carry that distinction, but the opening should make clear that “adjudicates” names the domain-level governance office, not a license for the evaluator to enact.

**S2 — testimony polarity: SOUND-WITH-REPAIRS.**

The chapter does carry the intended distinction in axiom 2: the replayable record testifies, while unreplayable judicial say-so is excluded (chapter lines 158–165). The banked §1.1 line is therefore reconcilable with the ratified negative pole.

The repair is needed around the consequence, not the sacrosanct wording: replay identity must be defined over the complete committed input tuple, and “receipt” must not imply that replay creates a KERI witness receipt or adds authentication to the original enactment. It is a governance-tier verification analogy unless a committed receipt artifact is separately specified. See T41-03.

## Discriminating fixture shape

The highest-stakes untested claim is the normative closure claim that the complete standard composes from seven primitives without losing ratified law.

The fixture should contain:

- A committed minimal domain corpus with KEL, TEL, GEL, law head, and appraisal position.
- Queries exercising affirmed, defeated, pending, self-convicted, and a separate ill-posed invocation that must refuse outside the codomain.
- Evidence-growth vectors exercising every permitted transition and rejecting every backward edge.
- Lower-tier defeat and lower-tier duplicity vectors proving annihilation and taint remain distinct, including first-seen survival.
- Two presentation orders proving no ambient ordering affects results.
- A lawful amendment enacted by a constructor, proving the fold never writes while old and new positions replay under their respective law.
- Covenant-seal positive and negative successors, plus a case where a digest seal is required and covenant substitution must be rejected.
- Two color comparisons: syntactically different law with equivalent observable distinctions, and identical law bytes with different cited context or position.
- A composition manifest mapping every object and operation used by the fixture to the seven primitives and common axioms. Any unmapped object or required invariant fails the closure claim.

Until such a record exists and survives independent replay, the seven-primitive closure and color-equivalence claims remain **UNVERIFIED/fixture-pending**.
