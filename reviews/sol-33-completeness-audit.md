# SOL succession-completeness audit — Custos 3.3 → 4.0 kernel draft

**Audit date:** 2026-07-23  
**Disposition:** **INCOMPLETE**

## 1. Authority, subjects, and method

The controlling bedrock requires commitments to remain byte-rederivable, orders consumed by a fold to be committed or proven irrelevant, assurance claims to carry discriminating records, and sealed predecessors to remain immutable (bedrock, `AGENTS.md`: laws 1–9). It also requires specification register rather than confessional artifact prose (bedrock: law 10). This audit therefore treats a 3.3 unit as transferred only where the pinned 4.0 bytes contain equivalent-or-stronger doctrine, or where a user-ratified record gives the unit a lawful companion or supersession disposition. Mere thematic resemblance, an open kernel confession, or survival somewhere on the shelf is not succession.

## 2. Executive result

The 4.0 kernel successfully carries or transforms the medium-first thesis, GARD/gAID identity, the four-valued finding codomain, its transition graph, registry-evidence/standing separation, rotation-policy genus, the cross-frame transformation law, grounded recourse, availability duties, and whole-document succession.

It does **not** succession-completely dispose the predecessor. Twenty-four dropped groups remain. The most consequential losses are:

- the granted/committed/derived standing triad and elevation law;
- the enactment-echo triple;
- the three closure tiers and bilateral endorsement envelope;
- the two-seam doctrine and typed exogenous-claim failure rules;
- the MALFORMED/ILL-SHAPED strata distinction and closed defeat registry;
- the Smith-tree corpus, clause-kind registry, enactment/supersession rules, capture floor, and N1–N5 constructions;
- CPX’s committed predicate-language profile and salt discipline;
- the declared-posture, membrane, erasure, membership, and genesis partitions;
- conformance rungs, vectors, bridge profiles, projection rules, and the unboundness/debt schedule;
- the predecessor’s exact detached-record succession lifecycle.

Several related subjects are openly redesignated in 4.0, but no ruling assigns the particular 3.3 law to a companion and no explicit supersession ruling rejects it. They are therefore **DROPPED**, not merely “condensed.”

## 3. Findings — dropped units

### CA-01 — BLOCKING — standing triad and elevation law lost

**3.3 unit.** The predecessor distinguishes granted standing, committed standing, and derived standing, with bounds and scope; it requires elevation to be explicit and forbids one grade from silently claiming another. The doctrine is rooted in the opening distinction between evidence and standing (`3.3:3–24`), CT-02/CT-19 (`3.3:252–270`), the finding-consumption rules (`3.3:1023–1047`), and the closure-tier/elevation discipline (`3.3:549–564`).

**Loss.** Section 7 correctly says registry state is evidence and standing is covenant-derived judgment (`4.0:816–887`), but it does not preserve the three grades, their grade-transition rule, or the elevation law. A stranger cannot reconstruct when a committed grant becomes derived present force, nor what proof changes its grade.

**Cheapest lawful disposition.** Add a compact standing-grade table to §7, or rule the triad superseded and assign grade mechanics to a named standing-profile companion.

### CA-02 — BLOCKING — enactment-echo triple dropped

**3.3 unit.** Every enactment binds an echo triple: predecessor enactment coordinate, warranted predecessor-head SAID, and enacting-seal SAID; genesis has a specific carve-out; missing or mismatched echoes are classified defects (`3.3:252`, `1099–1152`).

**Loss.** The kernel uses GEL anchors, law heads, positions, and grounded enactments, but never carries the triple or expressly rejects it (`4.0:262–312`, `358–368`, `1381–1415`). Posteriority and trajectory therefore lose their predecessor-defined committed shape.

**Cheapest lawful disposition.** Seat the three fields in §12.1’s grounded-enactment content, or explicitly supersede the triple by a ruled GEL event schema.

### CA-03 — BLOCKING — bilateral endorsement envelope and closure tiers dropped

**3.3 unit.** Endorsed identities require two independently failing bonds, with the self-assignment tuple bound to gard, rule object, and role; blinded-envelope states have defined findings (`3.3:494–547`). Three non-interchangeable closure tiers—delegated, endorsed-profile, seal—carry distinct prevention/detection/appraisal claims, and no tier may advertise another’s properties (`3.3:549–564`).

**Loss.** The kernel defines organs and seats but declares seating procedure and organ architecture open (`4.0:370–376`, `1646–1655`). It recommends delegated organ AIDs (`4.0:996–1008`) but neither carries nor supersedes the endorsed-profile tier and bilateral consent envelope.

**Cheapest lawful disposition.** Assign the full envelope and tier table to the enactment/seating companion by explicit ruling, retaining a kernel sentence that closure grades may not be conflated.

### CA-04 — BLOCKING — two-sided perimeter and seam doctrine dropped

**3.3 unit.** The predecessor permits at most one exogenous seam identity with typed claim classes and declared failure semantics, while every GARD necessarily has an interior seam. No fact crosses either seam; only signed utterances binding their signer do (`3.3:618–681`). Seam absence, fallback failure, and seam duplicity have exact pending/evidence consequences (`3.3:628–660`).

**Loss.** The kernel’s frame-local transformation law carries “judgment does not cross; evidence does” (`4.0:1132–1144`), but the exogenous/interior distinction, cardinality asymmetry, typed claim classes, fallback behavior, and seam-duplicity routing disappear. Section 14’s generic uncommitted-seam refusal is not an equivalent disposition (`4.0:1627–1672`).

**Cheapest lawful disposition.** Seat a compact seam rule in §§4 or 11 and assign typed-class schemas to a companion; otherwise expressly supersede the one-seam doctrine.

### CA-05 — MAJOR — perimeter/closure force doctrine incompletely transferred

**3.3 unit.** Prevention is constitutive only within the prevention-grade perimeter; detection and appraisal refuse and find but do not prevent (`3.3:14–24`). The associated perimeter doctrine is that prevention shrinks with the perimeter and becomes conversion/testimony beyond it.

**Loss.** The recourse ladder carries the latter half—prevention only within the frame, conversion beyond it (`4.0:1434–1460`)—but omits the closure-grade conditions that define when a perimeter actually earns prevention. The result states the conclusion without its predecessor’s grade test.

**Cheapest lawful disposition.** Cross-reference a restored closure-tier rule from §12.2, or explicitly narrow 4.0’s prevention claim to substrate delegation only.

### CA-06 — BLOCKING — wrongness strata and MALFORMED/ILL-SHAPED vocabulary dropped

**3.3 unit.** 3.3 reserves MALFORMED for zero-evaluation byte-scan failure; later committed-object defects are ILL-SHAPED and map to findings. Missing fields inside committed objects are defeated; missing bundle items are pending (`3.3:232–248`, `751–765`). The distinction is used throughout CPX and `@ROOT` statics (`3.3:2331–2343`, `2453–2470`).

**Loss.** 4.0 introduces a new two-kind conviction family—canonical-form versus GARD-law violation (`4.0:1556–1566`)—but neither maps nor expressly supersedes the predecessor vocabulary. It also lacks the committed-object versus bundle-absence general rule outside the codomain signature.

**Cheapest lawful disposition.** Add an explicit supersession/mapping table: MALFORMED → canonical-form refusal; ILL-SHAPED committed content → defeated with cited clause; absent bundle evidence → pending.

### CA-07 — BLOCKING — closed subcode and field-role registry dropped

**3.3 unit.** Defeat classification is determined by the first check consuming the bytes and the schema-declared field role; the additive-only registry has total precedence, 33 initial codes, a site-to-code map, and register-first extension discipline (`3.3:894–926`, `2521–2638`).

**Loss.** Kernel §6.3 retains the four class ranks but changes subcodes to clause-local enumeration and permits an empty subcode (`4.0:729–742`). No ruling explicitly supersedes the standard-level registry or its field-role rule. This is a real semantic change: two 3.3-conforming verifiers could be required to emit a code where 4.0 allows empty.

**Cheapest lawful disposition.** Issue an explicit supersession ruling for the 33-code registry and state the clause-local replacement, including migration behavior for predecessor findings.

### CA-08 — BLOCKING — Smith-tree corpus and clause-kind registry dropped

**3.3 unit.** Law is a SAID-addressed Smith tree with closed node kinds, one-clause/one-ACDC/one-SAID leaves, authoritative atom bytes, additive-only kind extension, per-node strata, and structural invalidity rules (`3.3:1288–1322`, `2139–2167`).

**Loss.** The kernel defines Constitution as computed state and edicts as bare SAID-addressed content (`4.0:299–312`, `460–464`), but supplies no corpus model or explicit supersession. Section 14 says act-registry and carriage encoding remain undesigned (`4.0:1646–1654`); that confession does not dispose an existing ratified corpus law.

**Cheapest lawful disposition.** Assign the Smith-tree and node registry to a named corpus-schema companion while retaining the one-clause/one-ground citation invariant in the kernel.

### CA-09 — BLOCKING — enactment, supersession, disposition, and capture-floor law dropped

**3.3 unit.** 3.3 defines enactment echo, unit of enactment, per-class cascade/grandfather posture, adoption migration maps, probationary windows, emergency sunset behavior, and the one-epoch capture floor constraining amendment step shapes (`3.3:1099–1247`; `2249–2268`).

**Loss.** 4.0 says governed law is positional and judged under prior law (`4.0:1097–1121`) and succession is nonretroactive (`4.0:1698–1705`), but it lacks the capture-floor predicate, disposition classes, migration maps, emergency vector, and cascade/grandfather law.

**Cheapest lawful disposition.** Seat the capture floor and per-class supersession declaration in §10 or §15; assign disposition schemas and the EMG fixture to the fixture lane by explicit ruling.

### CA-10 — BLOCKING — genesis battery and structural/posture partition dropped

**3.3 unit.** Genesis carries a fixed-point bundle, exact seal list, head, membrane and seam declarations, witnessed inception, conflict-language pin, unboundness schedule, and position-zero constraint. Failures are partitioned into structural nonexistence and curable posture deficiency (`3.3:1248–1287`, `2189–2216`).

**Loss.** 4.0 adds a stronger born-governed `(K0,C)` knot (`4.0:228–252`) and calls genesis constructed rather than judged (`4.0:1097–1113`), but does not dispose the remaining battery or the structural/posture classification. The new knot supersedes only part of the old genesis mechanism.

**Cheapest lawful disposition.** Add a ruling explicitly superseding the old fixed-point/seal-list shape by `(K0,C)`, and assign the surviving genesis validity/posture rows to a genesis-profile companion.

### CA-11 — BLOCKING — N1–N5 constructions dropped

**3.3 unit.** When elected, the following are normative: pre-rotated law including plural deterministic branches and weakest-branch confession; conservative attenuation proofs; sealed clauses with force-at-reveal and anti-grinding; co-anchored treaty subtrees with lifecycle states; and sealed disposition vectors with conflict-conditioned duplicity (`3.3:1323–1482`).

**Loss.** Kernel federation transforms part of N4 into matched recognition anchors (`4.0:1162–1192`) and rotation law resembles the genus behind N1 (`4.0:953–1030`), but no ruling disposes N1, N2, N3, or N5, nor N4’s amendment/pending/nonrenewal lifecycle. These are ratified elective constructions, not explanatory examples.

**Cheapest lawful disposition.** Explicitly supersede N4 by the federation envelope and assign N1/N2/N3/N5 to named construction profiles. Until then all five remain succession holes.

### CA-12 — BLOCKING — CPX and predicate-language commitments dropped

**3.3 unit.** A GARD must pin a conflict language at genesis; CPX is the exemplar with a pure two-operand domain, five closed scalar types, ten closed operators, canonical byte comparison, size bounds, `kfs`/`cpr` binding, epoch anchoring, `@ROOT` legality, and conservative attenuation procedure (`3.3:1484–1521`, `2292–2517`).

**Loss.** The kernel carries “committed predicates” and evaluator refusal but defines no conflict-language property set, pin, or lawful companion assignment (`4.0:398–405`, `784–813`, `1195–1208`). Section 14’s “general algebra open” does not expressly assign CPX. S-14’s open-interior ruling concerns the Gever construction, not automatic disposal of the predecessor’s predicate language.

**Cheapest lawful disposition.** Assign CPX and alternative-language conformance to the evaluation-engine companion with a kernel requirement that the language be pinned, terminating, canonical, and fail-closed.

### CA-13 — MAJOR — salt discipline dropped

**3.3 unit.** Every salted commitment must use at least 128 CSPRNG bits, bind a domain-separation tuple, prohibit reuse, and reveal `(salt, content, kind)` for recomputation; conformance is checked at reveal (`3.3:2642–2684`).

**Loss.** Kernel sections mention hidden custodian digests and self-addressed objects but contain no salt law and no ruling assigning it outward (`4.0:979–994`). The doctrine was explicitly ratified in 3.3 and is security-bearing.

**Cheapest lawful disposition.** Assign Annex F intact to an encoding/privacy profile and cite it wherever hidden commitments are allowed, or carry the four rules in a short kernel annex.

### CA-14 — BLOCKING — declared-posture registry dropped

**3.3 unit.** Genesis or live state declares concentration, N1–N5 election, per-class supersession behavior, escrow default, heartbeat, rotation coupling, cure windows, PAC posture, seam classes, frozen status, and profile-citation mode. Omission and false declaration are the two exhaustive posture violations (`3.3:1665–1692`).

**Loss.** 4.0 carries custody plurality, evidence scale, disclosure baseline, rotation axes, and latitude (`4.0:517–529`, `979–1030`, `1524–1615`) but no unified registry, no exhaustive violation taxonomy, and no explicit supersession.

**Cheapest lawful disposition.** Restore a minimal posture registry in §13 and assign each posture’s schema to its relevant companion.

### CA-15 — BLOCKING — membrane, blinding, and erasure law dropped

**3.3 unit.** Public/confidential/partitioned strata apply per event, artifact, and Smith-tree node; constitutional outputs cannot be blinded; blinded appraisals are non-consumable; duplicity defeats confidentiality; erasability is limited to escrowed partitioned content while commitment and destruction act remain permanent (`3.3:1694–1722`).

**Loss.** The kernel confesses a full-disclosure cone baseline and permits deployment redaction (`4.0:517–529`) but does not carry the strata, blinding rules, forfeiture rule, or scoped erasure doctrine. “Deployment law” is not a named companion assignment.

**Cheapest lawful disposition.** Assign the membrane wholesale to a confidentiality/disclosure companion and retain in the kernel the non-consumability and permanence floors.

### CA-16 — MAJOR — membership, expulsion, and root/organ boundary dropped

**3.3 unit.** Membership admission and expulsion split party-facing appraisal from constitutional seating; suspension, credentials, disputes, and piercing remain organ-only; root appraisal has a twelve-case boundary table (`3.3:1724–1731`, `2270–2288`).

**Loss.** Kernel recourse names expulsion and withdrawal of standing (`4.0:1439–1442`) but leaves seating procedure open and does not preserve the root/organ allocation (`4.0:370–376`, `1646–1655`).

**Cheapest lawful disposition.** Assign the edge-case table to the seating/enactment companion, retaining the no-root-party-appraisal rule in §12.

### CA-17 — MAJOR — asker-blindness intake-class reservation dropped

**3.3 unit.** Asking is a distinct intake class; each serving surface declares requester-distinguishing material; offline replay is structurally asker-blind; live-query resolution remains reserved and may not be amended without the PQ/F5 rerun obligation (`3.3:1739–1756`).

**Loss.** The kernel discusses request authentication (`4.0:1591–1604`) and cone disclosure (`4.0:517–529`) but drops intake-class declaration and the binding reservation gate.

**Cheapest lawful disposition.** Assign live-query privacy to the deployment companion and carry the intake-class declaration plus rerun condition in §13.

### CA-18 — BLOCKING — exact document-succession lifecycle dropped

**3.3 unit.** Succession has candidate, ratified, and effective states; a SAID-closed succession object; derived ratification coordinate; detached lineage record; latest-unsuperseded eligibility; prospective effect; a named KEL-anchored lineage and fork rule (`3.3:1758–1852`).

**Loss.** Kernel §15 carries enactment, anchoring, effectuation, prospective force, and successor requirements (`4.0:1685–1734`) but omits the succession object, detached-record rule, candidate/ratified state semantics, superseded-ratification attack defense, and lineage-order mechanism. No ruling says the simpler procedure supersedes these ratified controls.

**Cheapest lawful disposition.** Carry the detached-record and latest-unsuperseded rules into §15; assign object schema and ceremony details to a succession companion.

### CA-19 — MAJOR — conformance ladder and consumption-tier separation dropped

**3.3 unit.** G-VERIFIER, G-GENESIS, G-MEMBER, G-ORGAN, G-GARD, and G-DOSSIER are distinct rungs; consumption tiers are orthogonal and do not discharge conformance (`3.3:1923–1937`, `2169–2187`).

**Loss.** The kernel states fixture-scale evidence and cross-implementation debt (`4.0:41–47`, `1524–1540`, `1717–1725`) but does not preserve the role ladder or the prohibition on consumption policy masquerading as conformance.

**Cheapest lawful disposition.** Assign certification/rung machinery to a conformance companion and retain the orthogonality rule in §13.

### CA-20 — MAJOR — vector families and executable refusal surface dropped

**3.3 unit.** The predecessor names vector families for invariants, genesis, finding transitions, envelopes, predicate placement, proof types, projection, primitive deletion, and registry mappings; many have MUST-pass/MUST-fail semantics (`3.3:1880–1965`, including `1899–1921`).

**Loss.** The kernel records fixture scale and dockets three fixture items (`triage-board.md:443–444`) but does not assign the predecessor’s vector corpus as a whole. Only the 52-span codomain signature has a conservativity gate.

**Cheapest lawful disposition.** Add the full predecessor vector inventory to the fixture-lane docket by ruling, marking obsolete vectors only after an explicit supersession map.

### CA-21 — MAJOR — projection doctrine and bridge profiles dropped

**3.3 unit.** Projections are always derived and nonauthoritative; they must carry gAID, corpus root and position, and dereference path; divergence and stale projection are distinguished; relying without dereference falls to insufficient-grade reliance (`3.3:2054–2076`, `2102–2128`).

**Loss.** 4.0 carries dossiers, warranties, colored evidence, and consuming-frame judgment (`4.0:451–515`) but does not carry the projection metadata or failure rules. No companion ruling names the bridge profiles.

**Cheapest lawful disposition.** Seat the nonauthority and required-reference fields in §5; assign concrete bridge profiles to an interoperability companion.

### CA-22 — MAJOR — economics/liability shapes dropped without lawful assignment

**3.3 unit.** 3.3 makes declared service terms, declarer-bears-liability shapes, forfeiture-funded bounty mechanics, subsidy disclosure, and a no-toll appraisal floor normative; it also carries obligation artifacts, the L1–L5 liability ladder, tenure attachment, and membrane-piercing warrants (`3.3:1646–1663`).

**Loss.** 4.0 correctly confines within-GARD vocabulary to evidence, liability, and standing and disclaims monetary consequence (`4.0:875–878`), but that sentence does not explicitly supersede or assign the predecessor’s non-payment liability and service-term rules. The price-register migration is therefore incomplete, even though “price/pay” terminology has been removed from the kernel.

**Cheapest lawful disposition.** Explicitly supersede monetary/equilibrium material and assign the obligation/liability ladder and service-term commitments to a liability companion.

### CA-23 — MAJOR — witness/watcher doctrine only partially carried

**3.3 unit.** Witnesses are committed, enumerated receipt issuers; watchers/jurors/judges are surveillance roles; detection inherits first-seen, witness receipts, KAWA, and DEL behavior, with behavior pins required where specification and implementation diverge (`3.3:367–387`, `813–840`). Watcher evidence and witness closure are intentionally distinct.

**Loss.** Kernel §13 preserves the crucial rule that enumerated observers are witnesses and unenumerated observation must remain unenumerated (`4.0:1568–1589`), and §14 disclaims a deployed watcher infrastructure (`4.0:1674–1682`). It drops KAWA/DEL pinning, first-seen edge classification, and the defined juror/judge roles without companion disposition.

**Cheapest lawful disposition.** Assign KAWA/DEL and receipt-race behavior pins to the deployment/observation companion; retain only the witness/watcher distinction in the kernel.

### CA-24 — MAJOR — predecessor confessions and debts incompletely transferred

**3.3 unit.** The predecessor’s unboundness schedule and tensions include same-substrate confound, unexecuted emergency fold equivalence, affected-context vector debt, CPX/conflict-language limits, first-seen edge gaps, data-availability economics, cryptographic agility, pre-anchor status, PQ deletion obligations, and other named unresolved rows (`3.3:1866–2054`, `2709–2753`).

**Loss.** Kernel §§1, 13–15 confess cross-implementation, observation, implementation scale, semantic latitude, authority-lineage materialization, and evaluator-interior debts (`4.0:92–108`, `1524–1540`, `1606–1615`, `1627–1682`, `1707–1725`). They do not carry or rule away the old emergency vector, affected-context vector, KAWA/DEL edge pins, CPX limits, crypto-agility, PQ deletion, or several named genesis/corpus debts.

**Cheapest lawful disposition.** Publish a predecessor-debt migration table at ratification: carried debt, discharged debt with evidence, companion docket, or explicit supersession. Whole replacement without that table loses the audit trail.
