## S8-1 — the law: enforcement grade equals observability

A covenant's enforcement grade equals the observability of the
conduct it constrains. Four grades, below the guard:

- **act-detectable** — the constrained conduct is itself a
  committed event; violation derives from the same bytes.
- **omission-detectable (promoted)** — the conduct is an
  absence, made observable by a committed instrument: cadence
  law (silence becomes positionable, dateable absence) or
  demand-of-record (an omission enters evidence through the
  asker's committed demand). The promoting instrument is part of
  the declaration.
- **fruit-detectable** — the conduct mints no artifact; only its
  use self-evidences. The surfacing condition is part of the
  declaration.
- **testimonial** — neither act nor fruit necessarily surfaces;
  enforcement rides warranties, discovery, testimony. The
  interior-fact doctrine's home grade.

The register clause, candidate spec text extending the
installation discipline (review-edition §2.5 lineage; the
two-site rule the ratified text already carries):

> Every covenant SHALL declare the enforcement grade of the
> conduct it constrains — its grade, its surfacing condition
> where fruit-detectable, its promoting instrument where
> omission-promoted, and its attribution scope — and a covenant
> declaring a grade above its conduct's observability is
> mis-specified. What can be void must never be merely
> answerable; what can only be answerable must never advertise
> itself as void; and what can only be testified must never
> advertise itself as evidence.

The three-component declaration — (grade, surfacing condition,
attribution scope) — is load-bearing: the filed finding shows
grade alone under-specifies exactly where it matters most.
