## What this seed carries

One rule, in two halves that pull against each other and are
both kept.

An evaluator's actual result space is the finding codomain
together with refusal, and refusal is expressly not a codomain
member. Section 7.5 then mandates a product former over
components that is defined only over findings. Nothing states the
product's value when one component refuses, and both readings
conform on the ratified bytes.

11a rules both halves at once: **refusal propagates** to the
invocation, and **findings preserve** at their own coordinates.
Neither half alone is the answer, and the interesting content is
that they compose without either giving way.

## Repair — the propagation rule

**Ratified spans.** Cited, not edited. The amendment block
(L1184–1195), whose two relevant SHALLs pull in opposite
directions:

> Where evaluation would require an uncommitted ordering or
> composition rule, the evaluator SHALL refuse the invocation and
> SHALL NOT legislate the missing seam. … Compound evaluator
> results SHALL preserve their component propositions and grounds
> as a product rather than collapse them into a fifth scalar
> shape.

The plain statement that follows it (L1197–1201):

> Refusal is not a fifth finding value — it is the evaluator
> declining to answer an ill-posed question, recorded as an
> operational fact.

The codomain's totality claim (L966–972). And section 8's two
dispositions, which put the weight on this seam — refusal where
composition is uncommitted (L1254–1257), and `pending` where a
composed group is merely unsatisfied (L1262–1267):

> An unsatisfied operator group is not a defect and not a defeat:
> it discharges as a pending finding whose typed requirement set
> enumerates exactly the unfilled slots.

**The defect.** A three-slot adoption covenant in frame B: slot 1
discharges affirmatively, slot 2 is unfilled, slot 3 names a
composition B's lens leaves uncommitted. One implementer refuses
the whole invocation and returns nothing else; another returns a
product containing a refused coordinate, which is an object
outside what section 7.1 says the evaluator returns. Both hold
the same bytes, both conform, and section 7.3's byte-identity
SHALL is violated with neither engine in error.

The compound case is not exotic. A compound standing question
routinely contains an unsatisfied slot and an uncomposable one at
once, which is exactly the input on which the product former is
undefined.

**Replacement — a clause in section 7.5, after the amendment
block.**

> **Composition and refusal.** Where any component of a compound
> invocation refuses, the **invocation** refuses. Refusal remains
> an operational outcome about the composition seam, and its
> record names the refusal's class and cites the components
> already computed.
>
> A cited component stands as an ordinary finding at its own
> coordinate: it was computed from a committed triple, and a
> sibling's refusal un-happens nothing. No computed finding is
> discarded, and no product object contains a refused
> coordinate.

## Ground — why the codomain wall holds either way

The two readings the finding exhibits are not equally costly, and
the asymmetry is what decides it.

Returning a product with a refused coordinate breaches the
codomain's totality, which is the first of section 15's six
walls. It also puts an object that is not a finding inside a
structure whose whole purpose is to preserve component
*propositions and grounds* — and a refusal has neither. There is
no proposition, because the question was ill-posed; there is no
ground, because nothing was computed.

Refusing the invocation and discarding the computed components
breaches nothing formally, and loses real work and real facts. A
component finding computed over a committed triple is a fact,
and facts do not become un-computed because a sibling's seam was
uncommitted.

11a takes both constraints seriously by separating **what the
answer is about** from **what the record carries**:

> Refusal answers about the SEAM. Findings answer about the
> SUBJECT.

The invocation's outcome is a refusal because the invocation is
what could not be answered. The components' findings survive
because they were never about the invocation — each is about its
own proposition, at its own coordinate, and remains recomputable
from its own triple by anyone holding the bytes. The citation is
what makes them reachable from the refusal without smuggling
them into a codomain the refusal is not a member of.

## Ground — the grammar grain extends to the composition grain

Section 17 draws the pending/refusal line at the grammar grain
and gives it vectors (L2296–2299): an unrecognized governance ilk
yields committed evidence and a rule-governed judgment, against
the refusal fired by an underivable grammar. The distinction is
between *evidence the law can speak to but has not received* and
*a rule the law never committed*.

The same line at the composition grain is already half-drawn by
section 8 and needs only to be named as the same line:

> **Unsatisfied discharges as `pending` inside the product.
> Uncomposable refuses the product.**

An unfilled slot is missing evidence under a committed
composition rule — the rule exists, the operator is committed,
and the cure path is readable off the finding. An uncommitted
composition is a missing rule, and axiom 3 refuses missing rules.
So the two dispositions section 8 already assigns are the
grammar-grain rule wearing different words, and one sentence
makes them one rule.

## What the record carries, and what is not committed here

The clause says the refusal's record cites the components. It
does **not** commit a refusal-record form, and the distinction is
load-bearing rather than fussy.

Section 7.5 already rules that a refusal is "recorded as an
operational fact", so the existence of a record is ratified. What
section 15 leaves open — and R11 expressly declined to close — is
whether a *committed* refusal-record form exists, the resolution
path L2081–2085 names as "a committed refusal record showing no
well-formed invocation exists". This seed's clause constrains the
record's **content**, which the ratified text already presumes,
and says nothing about its carriage.

A drafting pass that mandated a committed form here would resolve
a ratified openness question as a side effect of a composition
rule — the same class of move R5 declined for the carriage
encoding and R11 declined for the abstract's phrasing.

## Interaction with R11

R11 scoped the abstract's replay claim: a stranger recomputes the
same Constitution and the same findings byte for byte, and
reaches the same refusals as decisions derivable from the same
committed triple. PR #63 carries that seed.

11a is what makes the scoped claim true of compound invocations.
Without it, two engines holding one bundle reach different
*objects* at a compound seam — one a refusal, one a product — so
"the same refusals" would be underdetermined exactly where the
document puts the most weight on composition. With it, the
decision is determined: any refusing component refuses the
invocation, and the surviving findings are the same findings at
the same coordinates for both engines.

The two seeds should be read together and can land
independently. Neither reword the other's spans.

## Notes for the drafting authority

Things surfaced in drafting that 11a did not name.

1. **"Its class named per section 9's three-kind discipline" does
   not fit a composition-seam refusal, and this is the second
   seed to hit it.** Section 9's three kinds are seal-shaped — a
   digest mismatch, a coordinate mismatch, a clause violation
   (L1321–1327) — and supplement 1's C-1 rules that refusal
   grounds are named per that discipline. A refusal fired by an
   uncommitted composition is none of the three. The semantics-
   pinning seed reaches the same gap from R20's side, where a
   semantics-pin mismatch is likewise none of the three. Two
   independent rulings now name a class vocabulary that does not
   cover their cases, which suggests the discipline needs a
   genus term above the three seal kinds rather than two local
   patches. Section 14's conviction-kinds duty (L1973–1983) is
   the model: a record from which the kind cannot be read is not
   a conviction record, and the same is now being asked of
   refusals.

2. **The product former's own type is still unstated, and this
   repair narrows rather than closes it.** After the clause, the
   product is a product of findings and nothing else — which is
   what makes the codomain wall hold — but section 7.5 never says
   what a product *is*, only what it must preserve. Whether a
   product is itself a finding, a tuple of findings, or an
   object outside the codomain that carries findings is
   undecided, and section 15's openness confession does not
   obviously cover it. Recorded because an implementer building
   to the amendment block will meet the question immediately.

3. **A refusal citing components makes the refusal's own extent a
   design question.** If an invocation has forty components and
   the fortieth refuses, the refusal cites thirty-nine findings.
   Whether it cites them by identifier, by coordinate, or by
   carrying them is a carriage decision, and it belongs with the
   encoding round's refusal-record object (#57 lists the refusal
   record among the object classes the round designs). Naming it
   here would pre-empt the round.

4. **Station obligation: one vector, routed to #15.** The
   three-slot compound — one component affirmed, one pending, one
   uncomposable — expected to yield an invocation-refusal citing
   both computed components. It is the finding's own exhibit
   turned into a fixture, and it discriminates the two conforming
   readings that made this MAJOR.
