## What this seed carries

Eleven composition sentences, one per construct, written to the
model §17 already sets. Nothing else: no ratified claim is
scoped, narrowed or extended by this file, and every sentence
states a composition the surrounding prose already implies.

§1.7 is normative for the document itself. It requires that every
construct introduced after chapter 1 be introduced "as a named
composition of those seven, and the introducing section states the
composition in its own prose." Exactly one section does — §17,
whose sentence is the pattern used throughout below:

> Composition, per the comprehension gate: a GEL event is a log
> entry (log) carrying an enactment or its evidence (enact),
> committed by seal into the gAID's KEL (seal), read by exactly
> one fold (fold, finding), and subject to the law in force at its
> position (succession). Nothing here requires an eighth
> primitive.

The seven are the five nouns — log, fold, finding, seal,
succession — and the two verbs, evaluate and enact.

## §4 — the vocabulary section

**Covenant** (L733–735), appended:

> Composition, per the comprehension gate: a covenant is a clause
> of committed law (log) that a fold evaluates by replay
> (fold, evaluate) and whose satisfaction is a finding (finding);
> it enters and leaves the law only by enactment (enact,
> succession). It constrains and does not act, which is why it
> plants no seal of its own. Nothing here requires an eighth
> primitive.

**Lens** (L752–755), appended:

> Composition, per the comprehension gate: a lens is a set of
> citations carried by the artifact that cites it — the committed
> law head at its coordinate (log, succession) and the predicate
> set a finding or warranty was computed under (fold, finding) —
> and it is cited by identifier rather than folded. See the
> primitive-closure note in §1.7 for the engine profile.

**Organ; seat** (L757–763), appended:

> Composition, per the comprehension gate: an organ is a seated
> constructor — an identifier holding a committed grant (seal) to
> exercise the constructor's verb in a named role (enact), whose
> acts enter the GEL as log entries (log) and are read by the fold
> like any other enactment (fold, finding); the grant is created
> and withdrawn by enactment (succession). Nothing here requires
> an eighth primitive.

**Availability charter** (L796–804), appended:

> Composition, per the comprehension gate: an availability charter
> is an enactment (enact) committed into a GARD's law (seal, log)
> whose obligation ranges over the availability of committed spans
> (log); its discharge and its breach are decided by replay like
> any other clause (fold, finding), and it is amended by
> enactment (succession). Nothing here requires an eighth
> primitive.

## §6 — the objects that cross frames

**Edict** (L848–852), appended:

> Composition, per the comprehension gate: an edict is content
> committed by seal into the GEL (seal, log) as the act of the
> domain's constructor (enact), citing the law head it was enacted
> under (succession), and read by a consuming frame's fold like
> any other committed evidence (fold, finding). Nothing here
> requires an eighth primitive.

**Verification cone** (L853–878), appended:

> Composition, per the comprehension gate: a verification cone is
> a set of committed log spans (log), closed by the citations a
> replay actually reads (seal), sufficient for a fold to recompute
> the finding an edict's authentication rests on (fold, finding),
> at the law positions those citations name (succession). It is a
> collection over the other forms and introduces nothing of its
> own. Nothing here requires an eighth primitive.

**Warranty** (L879–886), appended:

> Composition, per the comprehension gate: a warranty is an
> enactment (enact) committed by seal into its maker's log (seal,
> log) attesting a computed finding (fold, finding) under a pinned
> lens — evidence about a judgment, never the judgment — which is
> chapter 1's own statement that a warranty is an enactment
> binding its maker to a finding's ground. Nothing here requires
> an eighth primitive.

> Composition, per the comprehension gate: an envelope is a pair

> committed by seal citing one shared rule-object identifier

## The one result that is not clean

**The lens cites an engine profile, and an engine profile is not
one of the seven.**

§4 defines a lens as "which rule-set, which engine profile, which
predicate set a finding or warranty was computed under." The
rule-set is a law head, which composes. The predicate set is law
content, which composes. The engine profile is neither: it is an
implementation artifact, not committed evidence of the governed
domain, and no composition of log, fold, finding, seal and
succession produces it.

§1.7 gives a failing section two lawful moves, and this is a
primitive-closure question rather than a law-closure one: repair
chapter 1 by succession, or remove the prescription in the
section that needs it. **Neither is right here, and the reason is
already ruled.** R1 holds that the engine profile is not a member
of the closed triple — it is the lens-side citation whose
inertness conformance tests, under the reversal condition. So the
document does not need an eighth primitive to describe a fold's
inputs. It needs to stop implying that everything a lens cites is
one.

The repair shape offered, and it is the only place this seed
proposes anything beyond a restatement:

> A lens cites three things and they are not of one kind. Two —
> the law head and the predicate set — are committed evidence, and
> compose. The third, the engine profile, is deliberately outside
> the composition: it is cited so that a comparison can test its
> inertness, and a fold that consumes it has violated the closed
> triple. The gate is satisfied by exclusion, not by derivation.

Written into §1.7 as a named exception, or into §4 as part of the
lens's own sentence — the drafting authority owns which. What it
should not be is silent, because a reader running the gate finds
the same gap and has no way to know it was seen.

The finding that produced #37 makes this concrete: an argument
that the lens is a fourth fold input had to be raised and refuted
by hand, and the refutation is exactly the sentence above. Had §4
carried it, the argument would have had nowhere to start.

## What this pass does not do

**It does not touch §1.7's inventory.** The illustrative list at
L399–404 names warranty, organ, tier, consumption, federation and
engine strata, and omits edict, cone, lens, covenant, availability
charter and congruence. Two readings are available — that the list
is an inventory the chapter owes completeness on, or that it is
illustrative and the binding obligation is the per-section
sentence — and they lead to different repairs, one of which is a
chapter 1 succession. The per-section sentences above satisfy the
gate under either reading, so this seed does not need to choose
and does not.

If the drafting authority wants the inventory to read as
illustrative, the smallest honest change is to say so in the
sentence that carries it, and the eleven sentences above become
its examples rather than its competitors.

**It does not re-run the gate on chapter 1 itself.** The gate is
normative for the document after chapter 1; the primitives are
chapter 1's subject, not its product.
