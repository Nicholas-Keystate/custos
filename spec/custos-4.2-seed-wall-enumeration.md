## What this seed carries

Two spans are replaced — section 1.4's imported-wall sentence and
section 15's fixed-wall enumeration — and they are replaced
together because the defect is that they are two.

The enumeration moves to one place, section 15, with seven walls
instead of six. Section 1.4 cites it and restates nothing. The
import from the 4.0 kernel disappears: every wall is carried by
this edition's own ratified text, and predecessor lineage is
confessed history rather than a binding mechanism.

One wall is retyped in the move. "First-seen survival" leaves the
evaluator wall list and is replaced by **no fold-tier selection**;
the sentence it came from stays in section 7.4, where it is a
correct description of medium-tier behavior.

This is a re-ruling under `CONTRIBUTING.md` property 2 on both
spans.

## Repair 1 — section 1.4 stops importing

**Ratified span (4.1 L277–289).** Cited, not edited.

> These five axioms are the common floor of every fold — and they
> are a floor, not the whole house. The ratified evaluator carries
> further fixed walls this chapter imports whole rather than
> restates — from the ratified Custos 4.0 kernel, the edition of
> record at sha256 9cefdc5d5842…, whose evaluator sections bind
> here by that committed referent: the complete transition system
> among the four finding values and its refusal of backward edges,
> the canonical ordering and selection of evidence, the distinct
> upward currents of defeat and duplicity, first-seen survival,
> and the rule that acts consumed as grounds require committed
> receipts. Those walls bind with full force; a fold satisfying
> the five axioms alone is not yet a conforming evaluator.

**The defect.** Section 1.4 names five walls; section 15 names
six. Counting section 15's way — it splits the transition pair
that section 1.4 states as one item — the union is eight, and
merged, seven. Each list carries walls the other omits, and the
provenance claims disagree: section 15 says its six are "each
carried by ratified text in this document", while this sentence
says its walls are imported by referent from a predecessor.

A count in ratified prose is a commitment, and a list restated in
two places drifts. It already had. The two walls section 1.4
carried alone — canonical ordering, and first-seen survival — are
exactly the two that rotted, in findings #2 and #25. That is a
mechanism and not a coincidence: a wall imported by pointer can be
neither read nor repaired in the document that binds under it, so
nobody owns it.

**Replacement.**

> These five axioms are the common floor of every fold — and they
> are a floor, not the whole house. The ratified evaluator carries
> further fixed walls, enumerated once in section 15 and binding
> here by that enumeration. Those walls bind with full force; a
> fold satisfying the five axioms alone is not yet a conforming
> evaluator.

**Ground.** The repair is a deletion and a pointer. Restating the
list here is what created the drift, so the fix cannot be a better
restatement.

The 4.0 kernel keeps its place in this document as the predecessor
of record, pinned in section 16 and in the succession record. What
it stops being is the *source of binding force* for these walls.
Section 16 already supersedes the predecessor whole; section 1.4's
partial re-incorporation of it left a reader able to conclude
either that the imported walls survive supersession or that they
were superseded and are merely described. That ambiguity goes with
the import.

## Repair 2 — section 15 carries seven

**Ratified span (4.1 L2052–2062).** Cited, not edited.

> **What is fixed.** Six commitments bound the governance
> evaluator, each carried by ratified text in this document: the
> evaluator's return type is the four-valued finding codomain and
> nothing else; at an uncommitted composition seam the evaluator
> refuses rather than legislates; no backward edge exists in the
> transition system; defeat annihilates upward while duplicity
> taints upward, and the two currents never merge; acts consumed
> as grounds require committed receipts — an unreceipted
> operational drop is never a finding; and the transition system
> is explicitly and completely enumerated. These six are walls:
> evidence-ruled, fixture-exercised, and binding.

**Replacement.**

> **What is fixed.** Seven commitments bound the governance
> evaluator, each carried by ratified text in this document and
> enumerated here and nowhere else:
>
> 1. **Codomain totality.** The evaluator's return type is the
>    four-valued finding codomain and nothing else.
> 2. **Refusal at an uncommitted seam.** At an uncommitted
>    composition seam the evaluator refuses rather than
>    legislates.
> 3. **The transition system is explicitly and completely
>    enumerated, and no backward edge exists in it.** One
>    commitment, not two: an un-enumerated system cannot prove the
>    absence of an edge.
> 4. **The two currents never merge.** Defeat annihilates upward
>    while duplicity taints upward.
> 5. **Acts consumed as grounds require committed receipts.** An
>    unreceipted operational drop is never a finding.
> 6. **Canonical ordering.** Any order a fold consumes is
>    derivable from committed bytes, under the ordering
>    declaration this document states; the ordering rules at
>    individual sites descend from that declaration rather than
>    standing beside it.
> 7. **No fold-tier selection.** The fold consumes digest-cited
>    committed spans — including the governance span, whose
>    membership is derivable from the designation committed in
>    founding law rather than taken by position or convention —
>    and never adjudicates between competing versions of an
>    event. Competitors at one coordinate entering the bundle
>    convict as duplicity; they are never inputs to a choice.
>
> These seven are walls: evidence-ruled, fixture-exercised, and
> binding. Every other mention of a wall in this document cites
> this enumeration and restates none of it.

**Ground, item by item.** The union of the two ratified lists was
adjudicated member by member rather than merged wholesale.

- **1, 2, 4, 5 are kept as ratified.** Item 1 was convicted in
  live fire by finding #24 and held; the codomain really is total,
  and the clause that appeared to breach it was reworded instead.
- **3 merges two ratified items into one.** Section 15 stated
  enumeration and edge-absence separately. They are one
  commitment, because absence of an edge is only provable over an
  enumerated system.
- **6 is kept and rehoused.** It was a section 1.4-only wall and
  it rotted there (#2). As a wall it now hooks the ordering
  declaration rather than any particular site's rule, so a site
  rule cannot drift away from it without contradicting the
  declaration above it.
- **7 replaces first-seen survival.** See repair 3.

**Explicitly adjudicated out.** Two candidates were considered and
excluded in this cycle: R2's discharge discipline and R1's
position-indexing. Both are section 7.3 text and both are now
ruled, but a new wall enters through its own review, not as a
rider on a drift repair. They can be proposed as walls later on
their own evidence.

## Repair 3 — first-seen survival is retyped, not deleted

**Ratified span (4.1 L1157–1160).** Cited, not edited, and
**staying**.

> - **Duplicity taints upward.** A self-conviction at a lower tier
>   does not un-happen the history above it: committed history is
>   monotonic, first-seen survives […]

**The defect, restated.** Finding #25 read this as a conflict
between a wall and axiom 4 — an order the fold consumes that is
not derivable from committed bytes — and asked which of the two
should give way. Both readings it offered were inside a frame the
answer is outside of.

The sentence is a correct description of medium-tier behavior.
First-seen acceptance is the substrate's own observer-local
policy, and comparing first-seens is how duplicity is detected at
all. Nothing about it is wrong where it sits.

The defect is that section 1.4 imported the phrase as an
**evaluator obligation**. First-seen is constitutively
observational, which is the one thing axiom 4 forbids the fold to
consult — so the wall, as typed, was unimplementable. That is why
both blind implementations had to silently reinterpret the clause
before they could build: the correctly-typed rule is the only one
that can be written.

**Replacement.** The section 7.4 sentence stands unchanged. The
wall becomes item 7 of section 15's enumeration, above.

**Ground.** Survival is settled below the fold — at bundle
assembly, per holder, by the medium's own inherited policy — and
is reimplemented nowhere. Inside a fold there is no "which
version" question to answer: E cites spans by digest, so a
competitor at one coordinate is not an alternative to choose
between but a second committed fact, and two of them bearing on
one question convict as duplicity.

**The defect class, worth naming in the candidate.** This is
**collision-by-addition**: unchanged predecessor text made
defective by a successor's new commitments. The 4.0 sentence was
innocent; 4.1 added axiom 4 and section 17 around it. A
regeneration census that only re-derives survivals will not catch
the next one — it has to check survivals against the *new* axioms.

## Notes for the drafting authority

Some things surfaced in drafting that the findings did not name.

1. **Item 6 forward-references a declaration that does not exist
   yet.** The ordering declaration is finding #11, and it is
   drafting work rather than a ruling. Item 6 as written binds to
   it. If the candidate lands this seed before the declaration,
   item 6 should either carry the byte comparison rule from
   `custos-4.2-seed-canonical-order.md` inline as its interim
   content, or say plainly that the declaration is owed — an
   enumeration item pointing at a clause that has not been written
   is the pointer defect this seed exists to remove.

2. **The record's annotation on item 6 was an erratum, and the
   seed's reading was the right one.** R4's adjudication marks
   wall 6 "RETYPED by R9 below" and wall 7 "REMOVED … by R9",
   while R9's own text retypes one wall and never touches
   canonical ordering. This seed read R9 as reaching item 7 only
   and left item 6 as a keep-and-rehouse. Supplement 1, E-1,
   confirms it: the annotation on wall 6 is the error, R9 retypes
   exactly one wall, and the canonical-ordering wall stands
   precisely as R4 adjudicated it. R5's convergence argument for
   byte-identity is unaffected in substance, because the variance
   source it cites as removed is the ordering wall as R4 ruled it.
   Item 6 above is drafted against the supplement, not against the
   record's annotations.

3. **Item 7's entry is a retype, and the record now says so.**
   The adjudication excludes R1 and R2's products on the principle
   that new walls enter through their own review, and item 7 is
   new text, so on the page the principle and the action pointed
   opposite ways. Supplement 1, C-2, resolves it as this seed
   drafted it: the wall enters "under that same principle
   correctly because it is not a new commitment: it is the retype
   of an existing wall … whose stated obligation was a category
   error", and membership is conserved — seven walls before the
   retype, seven after, nothing entering that was not already on a
   ratified list. A genuinely new wall still takes the gauntlet
   road.

4. **Section 1.4's deleted sentence carries a second commitment
   that must not be lost.** "A fold satisfying the five axioms
   alone is not yet a conforming evaluator" is preserved in the
   replacement. It is easy to drop when deleting a list, and it is
   the sentence that makes the axioms a floor rather than the
   whole conformance test.

5. **Finding #21 dissolves and is closed, but the dissolution is
   contingent on this seed landing.** With the walls carried by
   this edition's own text there is no imported "evaluator
   sections" referent left to lack a computable extent. If repair
   1 is not taken up, #21 is live again.
