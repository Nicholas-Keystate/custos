## What this seed carries

One definition, and the boundary it draws by drawing itself.

"Bearing" gates every edge into `self-convicted`, gates
terminality, and triggers section 13.1's recourse. The document
gives it a decision procedure at exactly one tier and leaves the
other two — including the Gever's, the evaluator this standard
exists to specify — to the reader. R13 rules that it was never
one question: bearing decomposes into **conviction** and
**pertinence**, each with a separate committed home, and both
decidable from the closed triple.

The decomposition is what makes the definition sayable in one
sentence. Neither half is new machinery; each half is a ratified
rule that was already answering its own question in a different
section.

## Repair — the definition

**Ratified spans.** Cited, not edited. The gate, at the three
edges into `self-convicted` (L1062–1066):

> | pending | self-convicted | a bearing contradictory pair, **or
> new governed-status evidence** … enters the bundle |
> | affirmed | self-convicted | a contradictory pair bearing on
> the question enters the bundle |
> | defeated | self-convicted | a contradictory pair bearing on
> the question enters the bundle |

At terminality (L1084–1089):

> **Terminality.** Affirmed and defeated are final except for one
> event: the arrival of a contradictory pair bearing on the same
> question, which moves either to self-convicted.

At the recourse trigger (L1823–1825):

> Self-conviction keeps its ratified meaning and is not borrowed
> here: an enactor whose own committed bytes contain a bearing
> contradictory pair self-convicts.

And in the evidence ordering, where the term does its doctrinal
work (L1109–1112):

> Contradictory pairs convict only where they bear on the
> question — duplicity elsewhere in a subject's history taints
> that history's standing, but it does not convert this
> question's finding.

The one procedure the document supplies, at the key tier
(L957–959):

> At the key tier, whether a pair bears is decided by the
> substrate's own superseding-recovery rules — a lawfully
> superseding event reconciles rather than convicts.

Against the instantiation claim (L961–965), which puts the same
four-valued scheme at all three tiers, and section 4, which
defines twenty terms and not this one.

**The defect.** A blind implementation could not decide the three
edges and pinned a refusal it was not confident in. The reason it
could not is sharper than "the term is undefined": the natural
reading — bearing is a matter for the domain's own law — collides
with a ruled span. If law is silent on *bearing*, axiom 3 says
refuse. If law is silent on the *violated predicate*, section
7.4 says consume as evidence and do not refuse (L1170–1174):

> Registry-tier and governance-tier duplicity are law-relative —
> they convict only within frames that committed the violated
> predicate, and a frame that never committed the predicate SHALL
> consume them as evidence, never as conviction.

From inside the fold the two silences are indistinguishable: in
both, committed law fails to speak to the pair in front of you,
and the two dispositions are opposites. Both available moves
violate a ruled span, which is what made this BLOCKING rather
than a clarification.

`self-convicted` is terminal and unrehabilitatable (L1087–1089),
so two verifiers resolving bearing differently reach permanently
different terminal states from identical committed evidence, with
no later evidence able to reconcile them.

**Replacement — a definition added to section 4, cited from
section 7.1.**

> **Bearing** (extension). A contradictory pair bears on a
> question exactly when the pair is conviction-grade at its own
> tier — at the key tier by the substrate's superseding-recovery
> rules, at the registry and governance tiers by the law-relative
> rule of section 7.4 — and the convicted identifier's committed
> artifact is a member of one of that question's own
> birth-committed enumerations: the citation enumeration of its
> evidence bundle, or its discharged requirement space.
>
> Membership is flat and finite: an enumeration is a committed
> list, membership in it is a lookup, and no closure, chain, or
> reachability relation extends it. Pertinence is derived from
> those enumerations and is never declared: a domain commits
> predicates, which section 7.4 already requires of it, and
> commits no pertinence relation of its own.

## The conviction half

The question this half answers is whether the pair is
conviction-grade at all, and every tier already has its answer in
ratified text.

**Key tier — unchanged.** Section 7.1's sentence stands as
ratified: the substrate's superseding-recovery rules decide, and
a lawfully superseding event reconciles rather than convicts.
This seed adds nothing there, and R20's semantics pin is what
makes that delegation replayable over time.

**Registry and governance tiers — section 7.4's law-relative
rule, read as the answer it already is.** A pair convicts only
within frames that committed the violated predicate. So the
frame's committed predicates decide conviction-grade, and the
locating question the finding asked — where in the Constitution
does a verifier find the bearing rule? — has the answer that
there is no separate bearing rule to find. There are committed
predicates, and a pair either violates one or does not.

**The silence case is therefore already dispositioned, and it was
one silence rather than two.** No committed predicate means no
conviction, which means the pair is ordinary evidence and the
fold consumes it. The two silences the blind implementation
reported are the same silence, and section 7.4 rules its
disposition. Axiom 3's refusal guards law running out on the
**question** — the fold has no committed rule making the
invocation evaluable at all — and never on this gate. A fold that
refuses because the law is silent on a pair has refused a
question the law does answer, which is its own defect.

That last sentence is the boundary the finding asked for, drawn
at the grain the finding asked for it: not "which silence is
which", but "this gate never fires the refusal, and here is what
it does instead."

## The pertinence half

The question this half answers is whether the conviction touches
*this* finding, and the answer is membership in a list the
finding committed at birth.

Two enumerations qualify, and both are ratified or ruled:

- **The evidence bundle's citation enumeration.** R1 rules E a
  set of committed log spans closed by citation. Closure by
  citation is what makes the membership test flat: everything the
  finding leans on is already in the list, at the top level,
  because that is what closure means. A transitive search would
  be searching a set that has already been transitively closed.
- **The discharged requirement space.** R2 rules that no terminal
  finding stands while an enumerated check in the question's
  committed requirement space is unexamined, and the space is
  committed ex-ante (L1106–1109): "everything that could defeat a
  question is in that question's committed requirement space
  before appraisal begins." For the antinomy case the branch is
  clause-membership: a producing clause of the circuit is in the
  discharged space.

**No transitivity, and the reason is structural rather than
economic.** The tempting reading is that a conviction bears if it
touches anything the finding depends on, transitively — and it is
tempting because it sounds strictly safer. It is not safe; it is
undecidable at the boundary the standard cares about, because the
transitive closure of "depends on" over committed logs has no
committed extent. Two engines computing it to different depths
return different terminal values from identical bytes, and
`self-convicted` never lets them converge again. This is the
divergence the one-sentence rule exists to foreclose.

**Pertinence is derived, never declared.** No clause admits a
domain declaring which convictions bear on which questions.
Section 7.4 requires domains to commit predicates, and pertinence
falls out of the finding's own committed lists. A declared
pertinence relation would be a fourth input to a function axiom
2 closed at three — the exact shape R20 repairs at the semantics
face and R15 at the membership face.

## The role refinement

Conviction and pertinence decide *whether*; the convict's role
decides *which edge*.

> **Role.** Where the convicted identifier is the question's own
> subject (subject-pertinence), the finding's next appraisal
> returns `self-convicted`. Where the convicted identifier is a
> cited third party (ground-pertinence), the taint current of
> section 7.4 applies: the next appraisal returns `pending`,
> carrying the taint as a typed requirement element of species
> unresolved-conflict. The edge into `self-convicted` fires if
> and only if the pair is conviction-grade and bears by
> subject-pertinence.

The distinction is the one section 7.4 already draws between the
two currents, made computable. A third party's self-conviction
poisons the finding's **voice**, not its **question**: the
finding leaned on testimony whose author has convicted itself,
which is a cure-by-owned-act situation, and R6 already routes it
— `pending`, species unresolved-conflict, cured by an owned act
of the party whose conflict it is (L1005–1006). The subject's own
self-conviction poisons the question, which is what
`self-convicted` means and why it is terminal.

Without the refinement, every bearing conviction would fire the
terminal edge, and a finding would be permanently poisoned by a
cited witness's unrelated duplicity. The ratified ordering
sentence at L1109–1112 says the opposite in as many words —
duplicity elsewhere in a subject's history taints that history's
standing but does not convert this question's finding — so the
refinement is the ratified doctrine's own consequence, not an
addition to it.

## Ground for one sentence

The sitting's reason is transitivity, and it is worth stating why
that specific hazard rides on sentence count. A definition
delivered as "bearing means X" followed by "and note that Y" puts
the transitivity question in the second sentence, where a drafter
can later trim it as commentary and an implementer can read it as
non-binding gloss. Prose *between* ruled spans motivates and
binds nothing on its own — section 3's first reading rule says
exactly that (L535–540) — so a transitivity clause outside the
keyworded sentence is not a commitment at all. One sentence puts
the flatness inside the ruled span.

The candidate repair prose the sitting offers, recorded here
because it states the doctrine better than the clause does:

> a finding declares what it stands on at birth; a conviction
> bears exactly when it strikes something on that declaration;
> both halves of the check were committed before the question
> ever arose.

## Notes for the drafting authority

Things surfaced in drafting that R13 did not name.

1. **The definition's home is a live choice, and the finding
   argues one way.** #32 observes that section 4 defines twenty
   terms and not this one. Placing the definition there matches
   the document's own discipline — "each is defined once, and
   every later use means exactly this" (L590–591) — and it makes
   the term reachable from section 13.1's use, which is nowhere
   near section 7. The cost is that the conviction half cites
   section 7.4 from section 4, which is a forward reference in a
   document whose scope section confesses only two of them
   (L517–520). Placing it in section 7.1 instead avoids the
   forward reference and leaves section 13.1's use bare. The seed
   drafts for section 4 and flags the cost rather than hiding it.

2. **"The convicted identifier" is precise where "the convicted
   party" is not.** The standard convicts identifiers, not
   entities — R15's fifth item says so plainly for its own case —
   and the pertinence test is a membership lookup, which needs an
   identifier to look up. But R10's antinomy convicts "the GARD's
   administrators", which is not an identifier and, as that seed
   notes, not a defined term. The clause-membership branch
   sidesteps this for antinomy specifically: the lookup is over
   clauses, not parties. A drafting pass should check whether any
   other conviction kind reaches the pertinence test with a
   non-identifier convict.

3. **Section 13.1's use has no question to test pertinence
   against.** L1823–1825 says an enactor whose own committed
   bytes contain a bearing contradictory pair self-convicts. The
   pertinence half is defined over a *question's* birth-committed
   enumerations; a grounded enactment is not a question but an
   act carrying a claimed terminal finding. The natural reading
   is that the relevant enumerations are the claimed finding's,
   which the enactment already pins (L1803–1806, the four
   committed components). If that is the intent, saying so at the
   site costs one clause and removes a real ambiguity; if it is
   not, section 13.1's "bearing" means something the definition
   does not cover.

4. **"Question" is not a defined term either, and the definition
   leans on it.** It appears throughout section 7.3 —
   terminality, the ordering, the forbidden table — and is
   nowhere defined. The definition above inherits that
   looseness rather than creating it, and R1's closed triple is
   the natural place to anchor it: a question is what F(E, L, p)
   is a function *of*, so two findings are about one question
   when they differ only in E and p. Whether that is worth
   stating is a drafting call; it is recorded because the
   pertinence test says "that question's own enumerations" and
   the reader has to know when two appraisals share a question.

5. **The second disjunct at L1062 is gated by the same word and
   is not obviously covered.** The `pending → self-convicted`
   edge admits "new governed-status evidence" as well as a
   bearing pair, and the definition above is drafted over pairs.
   Under R10's ruling the second disjunct is the antinomy
   trigger, whose admission runs through the clause-membership
   branch — but the branch is stated as a *pertinence* rule,
   while the disjunct's evidence may not be a pair at all. The
   drafting pass should make the second disjunct's gate explicit
   rather than leaving it to inherit a pair-shaped definition.

6. **Station obligations: four bearing vectors, routed to #15.**
   The sitting names them — a pair that bears and convicts; a
   pair that convicts with no pertinence; a bearing-shaped pair
   under no committed predicate, whose expected disposition is
   consumption rather than refusal; and antinomy-pertinence
   through the clause-membership branch. The third is the one
   that records this ruling's boundary, since it is the case the
   blind implementation refused. They belong in #15 and are not
   carried by this seed.
